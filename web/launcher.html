GiantbornAge of Titans

Design your giant in the character creator: his build and face, hair, beard and beads, skin, eyes, tattoos, war paint, scars, and clothing and armour piece by piece. Then you wake as a 12-foot juvenile giant in Giant's Hollow. The Valley of Broken Kings is your tutorial: wolves and an ogre in the Old Wood, a village you can help, cow or force, and the Ogre King at Kingsfall, growing to 18 and then 26 feet at its shrines (about 20–30 minutes). Then the Kings' Door opens onto the Old Kingdom, an open world of five regions, two kilometres across, where you grow to 36, 48 and 62 feet, towns react to your size, pikes, shield walls and siege engines hold the roads, and oaths are won by help, fear or force. Win two of the three oaths, grow to 62 feet at the Standing Stones, enter the Moot, face Harrok the Unbowed and take the Stone Throne. Then the rim breaks: climb the Breach into the Hoarfell, a cold upland built for giants far bigger than you, and grow to 78 and 96 feet. The soldiers, townsfolk, riders, rival giants, brutes and wolves you meet are as real as you are.

About 68 MB to download. Play on a computer with a keyboard and mouse; a gamepad may also work.

Skips the Valley: the character creator, then a new game on the Kingsgate shelf as the 26-foot giant who took the crown. It replaces a game saved in this browser.

What to try

      
    
        
  1. Choose New Game. The character creator opens first: pick a preset or go through the categories (body, face, hair, beard, tattoos, war paint, scars, clothing, accessories, colours), open a row to choose from its grid, turn the giant with the mouse or Q / E, undo with Ctrl + Z, then press Begin your journey (Ctrl + Enter). The quest tracker (top right) and the diamond marker show the way. In Giant's Hollow, try both cameras (V) and spar at the post to learn block, parry and dodge.
    2. 
        
  2. In the Old Wood, wolves circle and lunge: dodge, then punish. Stagger a soldier with a heavy attack, grab it with E and throw it.
    3. 
        
  3. Grow to 18 ft at the Grove Shrine, choose how to deal with Elder Maren at Thornwick, break into the ruins and face the Ogre King at Kingsfall. Lock on (Tab) and the mouse wheel picks the trophy you strike; the keg on his back drops him to a knee. Below half health he charges: make him hit a pillar, then punish.
    4. 
        
  4. Take the crown and walk through the Kings' Door: the Old Kingdom opens below the Kingsgate shelf. Start in the open world begins here.
    5. 
        
  5. Drive the scouts off the beacon hill and relight the Kingsgate beacon. The Kingsgate shrine then grows you to 36 ft, and Heavy Footfall and Boulder Hurl become Titan Stomp and Crag Hurl. At every shrine, the Appearance tab changes your look, the Talents tab spends the point each level brings in three trees (new tiers open at levels 6, 8, 9, 10 and 12), and in the Old Kingdom the Ancestry tab lets you choose, once, Stoneblood, Stormborn or Wildheart.
    6. 
        
  6. Open the map (M): it fills in as you explore, the waystones and shrines you have visited are fast-travel points, and P pins a spot for the compass. The journal (J) lists your quests; T tracks the next one.
    7. 
        
  7. In Brannock, swear the march oath by helping its people, cowing them or breaking the gatehouse; the town remembers which. Pikes at the King's Road toll fort and a shield wall in the Raiders' Hollow brace together as you close: step out of the band drawn on the ground, then go for the banner.
    8. 
        
  8. In the Greywood, trolls close their wounds unless they burn: smash a bonfire or a brazier beside one, or throw it into the flames. Sedge at Hollowmere asks for the Troll Matriarch: slay her in her den, or burn her three nests and drive her out. Trees between the camera and you (or the target you lock) fade to a ghost, so the pines no longer hide the fight.
    9. 
        
  9. At the war camp, Aldmere's herald names the crown's three roads: break Fort Aldmere's gate and fight the Iron Regiment until Warlord Corvane yields (hold Enter, the mouse button or pad A on Spare or Strike; the screen tells you which ballistae shoot: smash them); wreck the seven war engines of the camp and Ballista Ridge; or show her a token (Brannock's banner, the deserters' word, Stoneblood, or your 48 ft) and sound the horn at the fort's gate for a parley.
    10. 
        
  10. Siege engines warn before they shoot: a ballista's bolt follows its aim line, a catapult's stone falls in its circle, and riders charge down a line: step aside. At 62 ft, seize a staggered or uncrewed engine with E and throw it.
    11. 
        
  11. Your first oath lets the shrines of the Greywood, the Sunken Steps and Aldmere grow you to 48 ft; two oaths wake the Shrine of the Standing Stones, which grows you to 62 ft. Below 62 ft the Giant's Stair turns you back: go round by the Ironway, up from Aldmere's road. Then climb the Stair, enter the Moot, settle the clans' champion, Harrok, in its ring, and take the Stone Throne.
    12. 
        
  12. After the throne the rim breaks and act two begins. The rim council by the throne tells you who will stand with you; then take the Rim Road east of the Moot to the Breach and climb it at 62 ft into the Hoarfell. Find Last Hearth and its hold-outs, reach the Rimstone and grow to 78 ft: Colossus Stride, a new ability, vaults walls and houses, trees no longer stop you, and soldiers break at the sight of you. Maera's trials then grow you to 96 ft.
    13. 
        
  13. Off the road: pay, cow or breach the King's Road toll, the ogre pair in the Barrow Fields, Millford's millrace and the Shepherd's Watch beacon; the Needle and the great pines in the Greywood; Wenna's five sunken bells and the Stoneknuckles, the sunken vault and the Titan Forge at the Titan's Bones; the deserters at the burned mill; Skarn and its siege in the Stonehold; and back in the Valley, the sealed Citadel that only a 36-foot giant can breach.
    14. 
        
  14. The world moves without you: raiders fall on Millford, an army column marches, and bands wander the roads. From 36 ft a lone soldier runs at the sight of you; pikes and shield walls still stand. Walk out of a boss's arena and the fight starts over (the screen warns you first), except in Fort Aldmere's bailey, whose portcullis drops behind you until the Iron Regiment is beaten.
    15. 
        
  15. Death returns you to the last shrine or waystone. Progress is saved in this browser, so Continue picks up where you left off. Sandbox is still there for practice.
    16. 
      

    


    

      
About this build

      
What it is. Giantborn: Age of Titans (Godot 4.6.3): a realistic giant you design yourself, among realistic people and creatures, in the Valley of Broken Kings as the tutorial, then the Old Kingdom, a streamed open world of five regions with a map, a compass, discovery and fast travel. Growth to 96 ft, abilities that evolve as you grow, talents and an ancestry, formations with banners, siege engines and cavalry, trolls, dire wolves and rival giants, three bosses (the Troll Matriarch, the Iron Regiment and Harrok the Unbowed), towns that react to your size, three oaths each won more than one way, and the campaign's end on the Stone Throne - act one's end - then act two beyond the rim.

      
Build 0.16.0-m16 completes the expansion's third milestone. New: the Stone Throne is act one's end now - a short finale (skippable) shows the rim cracking open, then the campaign's end screen, and play goes on into act two. The council by the throne tells you which of the peoples you dealt with will send help. The Breach, a gorge up through the rim east of the Moot, takes a 62-foot giant up into the Hoarfell: a cold upland of fallen giant-kings' works - a causeway, a toppled colossal statue, chain anchors driven into the mountains - where rimkin giants, the Rimward's warband and its engines, trolls and dire wolves wait, and the hold-outs of Last Hearth ask what a colossus can do. At its standing stone, the Rimstone, you grow to 78 and then 96 feet: Colossus Stride vaults walls and houses, Earthquake becomes Shattering Tread, Crag Hurl throws a house-sized boulder and the Titan Roar routs armies. A finished game from 0.15 or 0.14 plays the finale on its first Continue and goes on into act two. Also new: the Bastion Titan, the Broken Warden, guards the old toll-keep above the toll fort - break his guard before his shield will let a blow through. Press I for the inventory: what you wear in its slots - head, shoulders, chest, arms, weapon, waist, legs and trinket - your numbers, and a bag of everything else you carry, with what you just looted marked new. The aim indicator shows what your next swing will strike and says when it is out of reach; a man, a wolf or a fence at a big giant's feet is stamped on (a light) or stomped (a heavy), and you can look down far enough to aim at your own feet. Forges are smithies now, with a tall chimney whose smoke and fire you can find from far off, and every waystone is a rune-stone monument - a grey column of light rises from one you have not reached yet, a bright one from one you have. Sound effects - swings, blows, footsteps - now play in the browser too, placed left or right where they happen. And Last Hearth grows: Maera's tally-board by its fire (E) says what the hold-outs need, and it is giant's work - carry great timbers from the Windfall and king-stones from the toppled causeway in your arms (E takes one up and sets it down), heave the rockfall off its path, drive the trolls off the crags, bring iron and titan iron, then raise each work yourself at its site - it rises out of the ground under its dust as you do. In three steps the camp becomes a hold: a roofed hall with a fire to rest by, a ring of stone with archers who shoot beside you when the Rimward raid it (the raiders come for the hold-outs, and their warleader comes for you: the hold stands or falls by what you do), a forge that works every titan's parts, a waystone at the foot of the Colossus Steps, a beacon, and the Colossus Anvil that reforges your weapon for a 78- and 96-foot giant. Millford, Thornwick, Hollowmere and the toll-keep each have a smaller work of their own once their titan has fallen. The Character menu's Settlements tab and the map show how each has grown.

      
New in 0.15.0-m15. armour that changes the numbers - an Iron and a Hide set from any forge, and the Quarry set and the Quarry Maul cut from Stoneback's stone at Millford's forge once his quarry reopens - worn and shown or hidden piece by piece from the Character menu (C, or Y on a gamepad), reforged to better tiers with a perk you choose. The Ogre King now wears his trophies - a crown, a beaten bell, cart wheels and a keg that splinter off as you break them - and after his fall Thornwick's smithy makes the Kingsfall set from them; the armour sets were remodelled. Deep in the Greywood the Rootwarden sleeps rooted in his grove: his living bark grows back over every wound unless fire reaches it - the woodfolk of Hollowmere know how to lay him to rest, and make the Warden set from his wood. Saves from 0.14 and 0.13 carry over; a giant who felled the Ogre King in an older build finds his hide, crown-iron and bell-bronze in its pack.

      
New in 0.13.0-m13. The people and beasts around you are realistic now, made the same way as your giant. Soldiers, pikemen, shieldmen, archers, mages, bannermen, siege crews and the iron guard wear mail, leather and helms under a surcoat in their side's colour, so you can tell a formation's side across a field; villagers and elders, men and women, walk the towns and farms; the named characters, Warlord Corvane and the riders on their horses are people too, and no two in a crowd look quite alike. The ogres, the trolls and the whelps are heavy brutes, and the Ogre King and the Troll Matriarch wear their own gear; the wolves and dire wolves have fur; the rival giants and Harrok are giants like yours, in looks of their own. They all move as bodies: they walk, run, flee and brace, and their attacks wind up where you can read them. A pikeman crouches over the planted pike and thrusts it in both hands, a shield bash comes across the chest, an archer kicks, a rival giant lifts its axe in both hands for an overhead chop, a wolf draws back with its jaws wide before it snaps. A soldier you hold lets its weapon hang away from you, one you throw goes down and gets back up, and the dead drop their weapons on the ground and stay where they fell. Nothing about the fighting changed: the same enemies, numbers and warnings as in 0.12.0-m12. Your saves and looks from 0.10.0-m10, 0.11.0-m11 and 0.12.0-m12 carry over unchanged.

      
Sound on. The Valley, every region and every boss have their own score, and the Valley and every region their own ambience; towns and camps keep their region's music. The music follows the place and the fight, and you can hear threats coming. Browsers start a page's sound only after a click or a key press, so if you hear nothing, click the game once. The volumes are in Settings, and the sound mutes while the tab is hidden.

      
What it is not yet. Some bodies still look rough up close: the women share the men's face with softer features; the wolf's ears are bare, the horse has no saddle, and the troll's and the ogre's heads show flat facets at the brow and jaw; a hand that holds a pike, a staff or a club with the other hand lies open on the shaft. Some motion is plainer than it should be: a soldier you threw gets up by playing its fall backwards, the dead all lie in the same pose (turned different ways), a rank seen from afar stands in one pose, the horse's gallop is plainer than a real one, a brute's two-handed swing leaves its other hand open, and a dead horse falls with stiff legs. A whelp the Troll Matriarch throws passes through her arm as she lifts it. The rival giants' axes are still simple shapes, and so are the sparring post, the dummies and the siege engines, and the world itself. Hair is still made of shaped strips and layers, not single strands. In first person you do not see your legs or feet. The balance is a bot's: it walks from objective to objective, no person has played the world yet, and some fights are still harder for it than they should be (the Iron Regiment, if you take the crown's oath first at 36 ft). Siege trains do not wander the roads. The browser version runs on one thread, so expect slow frames and music that can stutter on weaker computers; in the browser the creator's grids show names instead of pictures.
