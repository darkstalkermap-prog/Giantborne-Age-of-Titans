Spec §16 lists what every milestone handoff must contain: changed files and a runnable project, the
exact engine version, launch and export instructions, controls, implemented versus deferred
features, test results with measured evidence, known bugs, and the next smallest milestone. This
document provides each for milestone M10 of `docs/plans/M10_PLAN.md` (the campaign's handoff is
[M6_M9_HANDOFF.md](M6_M9_HANDOFF.md); the earlier ones are [M2_M5_HANDOFF.md](M2_M5_HANDOFF.md) and
[M1_HANDOFF.md](M1_HANDOFF.md)), with the gate table of the plan's §4.1, the findings the gate fixed

**In one paragraph.** The player's giant is now the realistic body the owner approved as renders: a
skinned, textured, CC0-derived model with hair and beard cards, garments and procedural eyes, driven
by the same pose brain as before, growing through stages 1-6 by uniform scale, clean in first person,
with hit flashes and status tints kept. A character creator opens at New Game, at the launcher's
open-world start and from a new Appearance tab at every shrine: skin, eyes, hair and beard styles and
colours with grey, tattoos, war paint, scars, ancestry marks and clothing with dyes, eight presets,
Randomize and Undo, saved in the character without a save-schema bump. Every asset is regenerated by
committed tools from audited CC0 data and compared with a manifest. Nothing that plays changed: the
seeded soak, the world bot's smoke run and a Valley run that grabs and throws are byte-identical on
`0.9.0-m9` and on M10's code, with the skin on or off and with different looks. The Web build keeps the
1024 px maps inside the 26 MiB budget the owner chose. Everything was measured in a Linux container
without a GPU, a real browser, a gamepad or a human player, so the look on a real screen, the frame
times, the warm-ups and the pad are **NOT RUN** and are the owner's (§8). **The owner's approval of the
look (character sheet B and the release candidate) is pending as this document is committed**: the tag
`0.10.0-m10`, the play link and the merge to `main` wait for it (§7).

**The gate's result**: eight lenses, four reviewers; 21 findings, 19 confirmed and 2 refuted, plus one
note of the orchestrator's; 5 majors, all fixed; 19 of the 20 items fixed or settled at the gate and
one deferred to M11; the audit of the fixes came back dry on its first round; the merged code passed
its full check with 330 suites, 56,119 checks and 0 failures (§5, §6.1).

## 1. Engine, build and environment

- **Engine**: Godot 4.6.3-stable (`7d41c59c4`), the standard build, GDScript, the Compatibility
  renderer, Jolt, the fixed 60 Hz simulation. Unchanged since M9.
- **The giant's toolchain** (only to regenerate `assets/giant/`; never to build, export or play):
  Python 3.13.18 with `bpy` 5.3.0 (Blender 5.3 as a module), numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1, Pillow 13.0.0
  and OpenEXR 3.5.4 - fifteen wheels pinned with their sha256 in `tools/giant/requirements.txt`,
  installed by `tools/giant/setup_venv.sh` into a venv outside the worktree (`GIANT_VENV`; the bake
  cache is `GIANT_CACHE`). `assets/giant/manifest.json` records the versions and the reference CPU.
  This container is the reference toolchain.
- **Where it ran**: a Linux container with 4 shared CPUs, no GPU (llvmpipe natively under Xvfb,
  SwiftShader in headless Chromium), no speakers, no gamepad. Every frame time here is a software
  renderer's and indicative only.
- **Version**: `0.10.0-m10`, set by the ship step with the project's description and the launcher's
  notes. `tools/build_web.sh` reads the `-m10` and applies the 26 MB budget by itself.

### 4.2 Deferred, and planned but not built

- **By the plan** (§0.5): the other giants, the people and the world keep their low-poly look; no
  face or build sliders (one face, one build); no "World style" switch and no realistic or re-shaded
  weapons (the owner's answers 3 and 4, §7); animation beyond M10's clips (foot IK on slopes, roar,
  flinch and roll clips, two-handed grips, head look, bone-driven hair, expressions, a growth tween);
  gameplay pivots matched to the real skeleton.
- **From the plan's cut list, not built**: the stomp's leg lift, the end-screen portrait, the title
  figure.
- **Planned and done another way**: braids are cards, not a braided-strand atlas; a hair or beard
  option ships one cut of its mesh where the plan named two or three; the sheet tool is a scene, not a
  script (`DECISIONS.md` §2.3).
- **Left by the gate for M11**: §5.4.

## 5. The gate

The gate ran on the W27 merge (`wave-27`, `54aa5ab`), 18 November 2026: four independent reviewers,
each with two of the plan's eight lenses (lenses 1 and 5; 2 and 4; 3 and 8; 6 and 7), every finding
then verified adversarially by another agent (reproduced, or dropped), the confirmed ones fixed in
three worktree lanes (ui, web, look; only the look lane wrote the generated assets), the lanes merged
and checked, and the fixes audited.

Legend: **PASS** = verified by running the game, the engine or the tools here. **PARTIAL** = verified
here with a part that only real hardware or a person can confirm, or with part of the requirement
met. **FAIL** = measured and not met. **NOT RUN** = needs hardware or people that are not available
here, or was not run on this code. A Result is the verdict on the gate's merged code (`m10-gate`,
`348a7bf`): where the review found a failure that a lane then fixed, the row says both.
