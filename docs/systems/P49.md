# P49 — Web, tools and performance debts; the M11 pre-declarations

Plan: `docs/plans/M11_PLAN.md` §3.2 (and §1.1, §1.5-§1.7, §2.11, §2.12). Wave W29, lane B, beside P48 (the body
pipeline II). Built on 18 November 2026 on `pkg/P49` from the tag `m11-plan`; the version stays `0.10.0-m10`.
The measurements are in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`, "M11 W29: the Web, tools and performance debts (P49)"; this file
says what was built and why. Every number here was taken on the shared container (4 CPUs, no GPU: llvmpipe
natively, SwiftShader in headless Chromium), the other lane baking beside it; the loads are given where they matter.

## Probes

**Probe 9 of plan §1.2: the launcher unpacking a 44 MiB pack in four parts - peak memory with the parts decoded
one at a time against joined text.**

- **What was run.** A stand-in pack of 50,560,000 bytes (34.56 MB of noise and 16 MB of text: mostly
  incompressible, as the game's pack is) was gzipped (`-9`) and base64-encoded to 46,156,936 characters
  (44.02 MiB: the last budget) and cut into four parts of 11,539,234 characters, the cut falling inside a group of
  four as `build_web.sh`'s does (the part length leaves a remainder of 2). A page served from a local port ran one
  of three unpackers and then read the result whole (`Response.arrayBuffer()`, as the engine reads the pack) in
  headless Chromium (Playwright's build, `Uint8Array.fromBase64` present), a fresh browser for each run, three runs
  each; the renderer process's resident memory was read from `/proc` every 20 ms (the checks' memory guard's
  reader) and the output's SHA-256 compared with the pack's:
  - **join** - the launcher as 0.10.0-m10 has it: every chunk of every part kept, one text made of them
    (`new Blob(chunks).text()`), decoded in one call, the bytes handed to a `DecompressionStream`;
  - **parts** - each part decoded when it has arrived (the one to three characters past a whole group carried into
    the next part), the four byte arrays inflated at the end;
  - **stream** - every network chunk decoded as it arrives (with the same carry) and written straight into one
    `DecompressionStream`, whose readable side is the response's body.
- **What was seen** (the renderer's resident memory: at rest before the run, its peak, the rise; the time to the
  whole 50.56 MB in hand; every output's checksum matched):

  | Unpacker | At rest | Peak (three runs) | Rise | Time |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | join (0.10.0-m10) | 76-78 MB | 418, 407, 409 MB | **329-342 MB** | 485-527 ms |
  | parts (a part at a time) | 77-78 MB | 388, 373, 382 MB | **296-311 MB** | 504-568 ms |
  | stream (a chunk at a time, one stream) | 77-78 MB | 292, 298, 303 MB | **214-225 MB** | 396-399 ms |

  The unpacked pack itself is 48 MiB and its `ArrayBuffer` another 48 while the response is read, so about 100 MB
  of every row is the result; what the unpacker adds is about 230-240 MB joined, about 200-210 MB a part at a time
  and about 115-125 MB as one stream.
- **The decision.** The probe passed: nothing here is near the 1.4 GB row of plan §2.12, so the fallback ("keep the
  join and record the peak") is not taken, and of the two ways the plan left open it is **one stream**, not a part
  at a time: it holds 110-120 MB less than the join at the last budget (a part at a time saves 30 MB), never has the
  text of more than one network chunk in hand, and was the fastest of the three. `web/launcher.html` does exactly
  that (below); `tools/web/test_launcher_parts.mjs` runs the launcher's own lines.

No other probe of §1.2 is this package's.

### 3. The launcher (`web/launcher.html`)

- **One stream, a chunk at a time** (probe 9). `unpackParts(urls, type, fetchUrl, onBytes, onDone)` downloads
  the parts in order; `base64Pieces()` decodes each network chunk as it arrives, carrying the one to three
  characters past a whole group of four into the next chunk (a part may be cut anywhere); the bytes are written
  into one `DecompressionStream` whose readable side is the body of the `Response` the engine gets. The text of
  the parts is never joined and no `Blob` is built. The first piece is read and checked before the function
  returns, so a failed first download ("could not be downloaded (HTTP n)") and a damaged file ("arrived damaged")
  still reject as before; a later part that fails, or text cut short, ends the body with that error, which the
  engine's start reports through the same failure screen.
- **The download note** ("About 36 MB to download.") is written from the build's own byte counts
  (`Math.round((wasmPacked + pckPacked) / 1e6)`), so it follows the staged budgets by itself.
- The block between `// <parts>` and `// </parts>` is what `tools/web/test_launcher_parts.mjs` runs outside a
  browser: 345 checks - packs of one to four parts, every remainder of the part length by four, chunks of
  65,536, 4,099 and 1 bytes, the browser's `Uint8Array.fromBase64` and the `atob` fallback, wrapped text, the
  parts asked for once and in order, the decoder never handed more than a chunk and its carry, and the five
  failures (first part missing, damaged, empty, a later part missing, text cut short, no `DecompressionStream`).
- A four-part pack was built with the tool (`tools/build_web.sh --parts=4`: four parts of 5,897,803
  characters), judged with `--check-size` (`tests/slow/test_build_web_parts.gd` judges a real 45.9 MB pack in
  four parts) and played in Chromium (below).

### 5. Export reproducibility (`tools/scene_kit.gd`, `tools/build_web.sh`, every generated scene)

- **What the engine does** (read in its source, 4.6-stable, and shown by the test): an export converts a text
  scene by loading it, instancing it and **packing it again** (`EditorExportPlatform::_export_customize`), and
  packing gives every node that has no unique id a random one from the engine's cryptographic generator
  (`ResourceUID::create_id`), which cannot be seeded. So "clear the cache and seed the ids" is not something the
  engine allows; the other way the plan names is.
- **The builders write stable ids.** `SceneKit.save` no longer strips the ` unique_id=<n>` attribute Godot
  writes on each node line: it replaces it (`stable_unique_ids`) with `stable_node_id(scene path, node path)`,
  the first 31 bits of a SHA-256 of both (never 0; a clash inside one scene takes the next salt). The scene's
  own `res://` path is hashed, not the folder a test redirects the output to. **Every generated scene was
  regenerated**: 124 files, 4,297 node lines, nothing else. Instancing reads the ids back and packing keeps
  them, so the conversion is the same in every checkout.
- `tools/build_web.sh` **empties `.godot/exported/` before it exports** (`--keep-export-cache` leaves it), so no
  conversion of an earlier commit or another branch is reused, and prints **`PCK_SHA256`** (also written to
  `<out>/index.pck.sha256`).
- `tests/slow/test_export_reproducible.gd`: five scenes converted twice the way the export converts them give
  the same bytes; the soldier's text with its ids taken out converts to different bytes each time (the
  engine's behaviour, kept as a control: at most four bytes a node differ); every scene under `scenes/` has its
  own positive id on every node; the script empties the cache and prints the checksum.
  `tests/unit/test_scene_kit.gd` and `test_checkpoint_triggers.gd` asserted "no unique_id attributes": they now
  assert the kit's stable ids on every node (`_assert_stable_ids`), which is the stronger statement.
- **The imported scenes** (`tools/normalize_imports.gd`): the two-checkout export still differed in one packed
  file, `.godot/imported/giant.glb-<hash>.scn` (128 bytes: its 28 node ids and the file's checksum) - the glTF
  importer packs the scene it built, its nodes get random ids at every import, and the pack holds that file as
  it is. The tool finds every import of the scene importer, gives each node of the imported scene
  `SceneKit.stable_node_id(source file, node path)` through `PackedScene._bundled["node_ids"]`, saves it where it
  was (compressed when it was) and writes the new checksum into the import's `.md5` record, so the editor does
  not import it again; a scene that already has its stable ids is not written (`--check` writes nothing and
  exits 1 when one would be). `build_web.sh` runs an import of its own and then the tool before the export
  (the `NORMALIZE_IMPORTS` line; a scene it cannot rewrite fails the build). Compared node by node with the
  importer's own: the same 28 nodes, classes, properties, surfaces, skins and LODs; only the ids differ.
  `test_export_reproducible` gains the case: two imports of the giant with scrambled ids, written at one path,
  give the same bytes after the tool, its `.md5` record names the new checksum, a second pass writes nothing.
- **Two exports, two checkouts**: the same `PCK_SHA256` (below, "Measurements").

### 7. The memory growth under a starved browser (LD-1)

`tools/web/memory_probe.mjs` (a measuring tool, not a check): the Web build played as `creator_check.mjs`
plays it, the browser starved by the DevTools CPU throttle (`--throttle=N`) or by pinning every process of it
to one CPU beside busy loops (`--pin-cpu=K --busy=M`, as the M10 gate reproduced the growth); once a second the
renderer's and the GPU process's resident memory, the JS heap, the WebAssembly memory and the page's own
counts (animation frames asked for and run, the audio worklet's messages, WebGL draws and upload bytes), and at
chosen sizes a memory-infra dump of the renderer by allocator. The sampler reads `/proc` on its own timer and a
watchdog stops the run at `--limit-mb` whatever the page answers (a starved page left DevTools calls waiting for a
minute while it grew by a gigabyte); `--no-position-reports` leaves the engine's sample-position reporters
unconnected, `--no-audio-context` makes the page's `AudioContext` throw, `--blind-s` serves a build without the
UI probe (0.9.0-m9).

**What it found** (0.10.0-m10's code with this package, the release preset, 640x360, the whole browser pinned to
one CPU beside four busy loops from the title on; three runs on this machine):

| Run | Renderer at the start | After about 95 s | Rise | The page meanwhile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| as shipped | 485 MB | 1,795 MB (stopped at 1,800) | about 14 MB a second, steady | 38 animation frames in all, then none: the main thread answered DevTools once at 1.7 s and never again; JS heap 12 MB, WebAssembly memory 138 MB, WebGL draws and uploads, audio-worklet messages handled and chunks posted all frozen from the first seconds |
| the position reporters unconnected (`--no-position-reports`) | 488 MB | 1,793 MB (stopped at 1,800) | the same | the same |
| no `AudioContext` (`--no-audio-context`) | - | - | - | the engine never reached its title (600 s): this build does not start without its audio driver, so the suspect cannot be removed this way |

So the memory grows while **no line of the game runs**: the page's main thread - the only thread the
single-threaded Web build has, where every GDScript, the engine's frame, its WebGL calls and its audio mixing run
- does not get one animation frame in 95 s, and the WebAssembly heap (where everything the game allocates lives)
does not change size. What grows is the renderer process's own memory outside the JS and WebAssembly heaps,
off the main thread, at a steady 14 MB a second. That is not the game's, and nothing in the project can bound
it; the engine's sample-position reporters (one message per voice per render quantum) are ruled out. The
remaining suspect is the browser's side of the audio render thread (the engine's `AudioWorkletNode` and its
messages to a main thread that never drains them, "SyncReader::Read timed out" in Chromium's log at the M10
gate) or the compositor; telling them apart needs the browser's own allocator dump, which a starved renderer
did not deliver (the dumps timed out). The M10 gate already showed 0.9.0-m9 doing the same pinned to one CPU
(16 MB a second); it was not run again here. **Decision**: recorded as the browser's and the engine's
behaviour under a starved CPU, not fixed; the checks' memory guard (`tools/web/memory_guard.mjs`, 3 GB) stays.
On an ordinary busy machine (loads 3-12) the checks' pages peak at 1.2 GB (`creator_check.mjs` below), within
plan §2.12's 1.4 GB.

## For the W29 merge with P48

- **The budget rule's text.** In `tools/build_web.sh` the milestone chain reads, in this order:
  `if [ "$MILESTONE" -le 6 ]; then BUDGET_MB=7.5` / `elif ... -le 7 ... BUDGET_MB=21` / `elif ... -le 9 ...
  BUDGET_MB=23` / `elif [ "$MILESTONE" -ge 13 ]; then BUDGET_MB=44` / `elif [ "$MILESTONE" -eq 12 ]; then
  BUDGET_MB=32` / `else BUDGET_MB=26`. So the **last** numeric `BUDGET_MB=` in the file is still the current
  milestone's 26 (the line `ARG_BUDGET_MB="$BUDGET_MB"` further down has no number), and
  `build_giant.web_budget_mib()` and `test_giant_tools.py`'s `BudgetTest` keep reading 26 unchanged. The package
  that sets the version to `-m12` (then `-m13`) moves that milestone's branch to the end;
  `test_predeclared_m11` checks the last default against `config/version`. If P48's reader is changed instead,
  `tools/build_web.sh --print-caps --version=<v>` prints `CAPS budget_mb=.. giant_share_mb=.. people_share_mb=..`
  for any version without reading a pack.
- **The shadow proxy.** `RigPaint.use_shadow_proxy()` finds a `MeshInstance3D` named `ShadowProxy` under the
  model and does nothing without one (this branch). With P48's model, `GiantModel.apply_defaults()` has already
  set the proxy to shadows only (on layer 3) and the other meshes to cast none, the free cape on; the rig's pass
  then sets the same values (it leaves the proxy's layer alone and the cape's shadow as the model set it), and its
  `child_entered_tree` hook turns off the shadow of a part loaded later - which agrees with P48's index, whose only
  `free` mesh is `Garment_cape`. After the merge run `tests/slow/test_giant_shadow_proxy.gd`: it then uses the
  model's own proxy, asserts the rig found it at `_ready` and that it is one surface, and saves its frames under
  `user://m11_frames/P49/`. Re-measure `medium` (the commands in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`) with the real proxy.
- **`docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md`**: both packages append a section at the end; keep both.
- **The imported scenes.** `tools/normalize_imports.gd` rewrites the node ids of `giant.glb`'s imported scene
  (and of any imported glTF scene P48 adds) in `.godot/imported/` only; it never writes `assets/giant/**`.
