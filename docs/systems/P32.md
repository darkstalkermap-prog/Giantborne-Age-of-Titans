## What was built

### Schemas and GameData (anchors P32)

- `action_schema.gd`: `attacker_height_override_m` (positive), refused with the default size policy
  (`attacker_growth`) — the override never scales damage (decision 21); grab mode `rampart`.
- `prop_schema.gd`: the `engine` block (action, crew radius, minimum crew, reload, range band, turn rate,
  morale radius, grab stage, muzzle, throw action), and its semantic rules.
- `enemy_schema.gd`: `tactics.wheel_min_m` ≤ `wheel_max_m`; a record of role `crew` has outmatched
  behaviour `crew`.
- `encounter_schema.gd` (its own anchor P32): the `engines` list (`engine` world id, `crew` spawn ids).
- `weapon_schema.gd`: the `fists` visual shape; the perk kind `grab_reach` with `grab_reach_add`.
- `game_data.gd` (anchor P32) calls `SiegeRules.data_errors` (`scripts/world/siege_rules.gd`, pure): an
  engine's shot is a projectile enemy attack and its throw flies the engine (visual `engine`); every crew

### The size override (`scripts/combat/hit_factory.gd`, anchor P32)

`HitFactory.build` sets the hit's `attacker_height_m` to the action's `attacker_height_override_m`. The
poise ratio, the knockback ratio and the stagger length follow it; damage keeps the action's own size
policy (`none` for every override). A bolt reaches a stage-4 giant (10.97 m) at a 0.73 poise ratio —
102 poise against its 100 threshold — where an archer's arrow (1.8 m) sits at the 0.25 floor; the charge
reaches a stage-4 giant's legs with about 22 poise.

### Siege engines (`scripts/world/siege_engine.gd`)

`SiegeEngine extends BreakableProp` (group `siege_engines`) with a `CombatController` and an
`AttackRunner` of its own, registered with `Sim` as an actor.

- **Crewed**: while at least `min_crew` of its crew stand within `crew_radius_m`, alive and neither
  fleeing, routed nor held. Its reload runs only while crewed; the crew haul meanwhile (one
  `crew_reload` every 2 s). `Events.siege_engine_crewed` announces each change.
- **Shooting**: crewed, loaded, free and facing its target (the giant) within 5°, the giant in its range
  band and in sight, it takes one of the giant's ranged tokens and a launch slot — the pools archers and
  mages use — and holds the launch slot through its whole windup, so launches at one target stay at
  least 1.0 s apart with every ranged attacker's. Ready engines take turns, oldest first. The ballista
  tracks its target until the aim lock, then keeps the point; the catapult commits at once. A new engine
  waits half a reload before its first shot.
- **Breaking**: the prop's debris, XP and saved damage; then the siege morale event — every soldier of
  its side within 30 m loses 20 morale, debounced 2 s per unit (`EnemyBrain`) — and its crew runs.
- **Seized** (stage 6): `grab_refusal(profile)` is its rule — `engine_stage` below `grab_stage` (or
  without `siege_disruption`), `engine_crewed` while crewed and not staggered; held, it leaves the
  simulation and its crew runs; let go, it is set down upright and staggered 0.6 s; thrown, it rides the
  throw's projectile and smashes where it lands (one hit by its thrower, so its debris, XP and morale
  event follow).
- A `faction` export makes a placed engine follow its faction's standing (neutral engines never shoot).

### Crews (`enemy_actor.gd`, `enemy_brain.gd`, `encounter_director.gd`, anchors P32)

The director hands each engine of an encounter's `engines` list its crew when they spawn
(`SiegeEngine.add_crew` → `EnemyActor.p32_assign_engine`). A crew member holds its place beside the
engine (state HOLD, running when more than 4 m off), faces the giant, asks for no token and no attack of
its own and hauls when its engine asks. Without an engine — broken, seized, gone from the tree, or none
within 3 s of its first decision — it runs and routs. The siege morale event is debounced per unit.

### Cavalry (`scripts/actors/cavalry_enemy.gd`, `scripts/ai/cavalry_brain.gd`)

- **The cycle**: the rider rides to a start point 24 m from the giant; inside the start window (from the
  charge's 8 m to the shorter of 38 m and the run less 12 m, measured from the giant's surface) it stops,
  turns and, seen and ready, takes one of the giant's tokens and charges. After the charge it gives the
  token back and **wheels**: on along the charge line to a point 30–50 m beyond the giant, which
  `wheel_resolver` makes reachable on its nav layer within NavQuery's per-tick search budget (the flee
  and firing searches' `_search_affords`), for at most 6 s; then, after 0.8 s, it approaches again from
  there. Caught close it thrusts its lance. A start point it cannot reach turns its approach round the
  giant (−60°, +60°, −120°, +120° …) until a charge starts.
- **The charge**: the base's scripted `motion`; while the run moves the horse it leaves the actor layer
  and drops it from its mask, so it rides through the giant's legs and its own ranks without shoving
  anyone, and walls still stop it. Its hit is the horse's capsule swept tick to tick, once per charge,
  each hit's knockback coming from beside the line.
- `QuadrupedRig` body (horse, rider and lance merged), hoof footfalls, the "lunging" pose at the gallop.

### Siege disruption, Rampart Grip and the fists (`scripts/actors/player/player_grab.gd`, W19)

- An engine is a grab candidate; its rule replaces the broken-poise rule; Crushing Grip refuses it
  (`too_heavy_to_crush`); every refusal goes through the controller (nothing spent, the reason's
  sentence on the HUD). Held with both hands at most 2 s; light or heavy hurls it with its own throw
  action, the engine riding the projectile (`ProjectileSystem.p32_carry_engine`).
- **Rampart Grip** (mode `rampart`): seizes a small or medium actor or an engine; a small body is crushed
  and let go as Crushing Grip does; a medium body or an engine is hurled at once (the throw forced at the
  grab's first active tick, no further cost).
- **The fists' finisher**: a light whose action is a grab is requested with the grab's eligibility as
  its precheck (`try_finisher`), so a third light with nothing to seize costs nothing.
- **Grab reach** grows with the weapon's `grab_reach` perk; the statuses' and passives'
  `grab_reach_add` (P33's Iron Grip) arrive through the profile's `grab_reach_m`, which P33's
  PlayerProgression raises (wave 19 merge: PlayerGrab no longer adds them a second time).

## Measurements

### Engines and riders under every stage 4–6 moveset (tests/slow/test_siege_battery.gd)

Every strike of the reinforced club, sword and hammer, the axe, the great pine and the fists (their
lights and heavy; the fists' grab is no strike) at stages 4, 5 and 6, swung straight ahead at a
ballista, a catapult and a cavalry rider from contact + 0.3 m to 0.9 × the stage's melee reach: **378
swings, none missed**. (The titanforged forms swing their base's moveset.)

### The battery and the cavalry pair

On the siege test level a stage-6 giant 95 m from the battery draws the four engines' first shots
within 13.2 s of their crews standing by them (a new engine waits half a reload; two ranged attackers
draw at once; launches at least a second apart); the cavalry pair charges a stage-5 giant twice each in
15.5 s, every charge striking once, never shoving it.

### Per-tick cost

Measured headless in an integration world: a stage-1 giant alone 0.38 ms a tick; eight idle engines add
0.11 ms; eight crewed engines firing at the giant with their crews' AI on add about 0.8 ms (about 0.1 ms
per crewed engine).

## Decisions and deviations

- **The bolt's poise is 140, not §5.11's 60.** At 60 the 8 m override gives a stage-4 giant 43.7 poise
  against its threshold of 100, and the plan's test "a bolt staggers a stage-4 giant" cannot hold; 140 ×
  0.73 = 102 does. The catapult's stone keeps 70 (its splash and the landing circle make it the
  area threat, not the staggering one).
- **An engine's throw is its own action** (`ability.throw_engine`, projectile visual `engine`), not
  `ability.throw_held` with the engine in a `thrown_body`: its numbers are §3.16's (P40 × 1.6, a 5 m
  splash) rather than a body's, and the engine rides the projectile (`p32_carry_engine`) to smash where
  it lands instead of being set down. `SiegeRules` checks that every engine's `throw_action` flies the
  engine.
- **Two ability records beyond the owned list**: `data/abilities/throw_engine.json` (above) and
  `data/abilities/cavalry_lance.json` (§5.11's lance, 40, 0.7 / 0.15 / 0.6): the plan gives their numbers,
  not their files.
- **`scripts/world/siege_rules.gd`** is a file beyond the owned list (as P30's `FormationRules`): the
  GameData post-pass runs at start-up and must not load the actors.
- **Hook lines outside the anchors** (one call each into the P32 sections, as P22, P24b and P30 did):
  `projectile_system.gd` (`_move_visual`, `_make_visual`), `telegraph_visuals.gd` (`_ready`, `_process`,
  `_ends`, `_draw_record`), `enemy_brain.gd` (`decide`, `on_morale_event`), `encounter_director.gd`
  (`_ready`).
- **The engines run their own controller and runner**, so `attack_runner.gd`'s P32 anchor stays empty:
  the launch goes through the engine's `launch_resolver`.
- **The encounter schema got its own anchor** (the plan's hotspot table names none for it) for the
  `engines` list.
- **An engine turns NEUTRAL as it breaks**, before `Events.entity_died`: otherwise every soldier near it
  counted its death as an ally's (−10) besides the siege event (−20).
- **Ready engines take turns** (oldest waiting first) for the launch slot; without it the engine with
  the lower id won every slot and its neighbour never fired.
- **A crew member's haul is an attack action** (`crew_reload`, no hit), every 2 s while its engine
  reloads: the router voices an attack call for each haul.
- **The cavalry record has no awe**: once its voice is the horse's (the mix entry the cue tests ask for),
  an awe needs `sfx.voice.horse.awe`, which P28's bank does not have; riders are drilled, and the plan's
  cavalry row names no awe.
- **Audio data in P28's files** (additive): the mix's voice, step and material entries the cue router's
  tests require for every enemy and prop record (P28 expected none to be needed), and one alias cue.
- **Thunderhead staggers riders** (`data/abilities/thunderhead.json`, P31's): `cavalry` joins its
  `on_hit_stagger.archetypes`, as P31's notes asked of P32; crews are soldiers already.
- **The siege test level's navmeshes use a 0.25 m cell height**: at 0.5 m the large one lay 1 m over the
  floor and a rider's agent never reached its first waypoint (the level has no `nav_query` settings, so
  `NavQuery.level_waypoint` does not level waypoints there).
- **The default tier's siege telegraph tests moved to the slow battery** (the ballista's aim line and the
  catapult's landing circle), with the reach measurement, to keep the default tier within its budget.

## Known gaps (for later packages)

- **The cue router mutes the off-camera threat sting of engines and charges against big giants**:
  `CueRouter` weighs an attacker by its own height (a ballista 3.2 m, a catapult 4.0 m, a rider 2.6 m),
  not by the action's override (8 m, 4 m), so the poise-floor rule drops the sting of a ballista's windup
  at stages 5–6, a catapult's at stage 6 and a charge's from stage 4. The windup cue and the telegraphs
  still play. The router is P28's (no owner in W19–W21): it should read `attacker_height_override_m`.
  Recorded in `docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md` ("From P32").
- **Engines shoot only the giant**: the siege of Skarn (P36) bombards longhouses; `SiegeEngine._target`
  is the seam for a prop target (P30's target provider lives in `EnemyActor`).
- **The toll fort's stage-5 tower ballista** is P36's placement. Placements name engines as P28's router
  expects: `Events.siege_engine_fired` carries the engine's world id, and the router plays the catapult's
  fire cue when that id contains `catapult` (the ballista's otherwise), so a catapult's world id must say
  so (the test level's do).
- Formations do not crew engines; crews are individuals (the battery's shield wall is a separate
  formation).

## M8 gate

- **F12: a siege engine's shot strikes the prop it bombards.** P36's siege lines aim at NEUTRAL props
  (Skarn's longhouses), but a shot is its engine's (team ENEMY) and an enemy may strike only the player's
  side (`CombatEntity.can_damage`), so every stone and bolt passed through its target. `ProjectileSystem`
  now asks the shooter's actor for `p36_shot_target()` when a shot is spawned (`SiegeEngine`: the prop it
  bombards, alive and neutral; nothing for a shot at the giant) and lifts that one prop to the player's
  team for the shot's own sweep and splash only, then drops it back (P30's provider target does the same
  for a melee swing): other props, civilians and the engine's own side are never struck. The shot's prop
  is forgotten when the shot ends. Tests: `tests/integration/test_siege_prop_shots.gd` (default, about
  0.1 s: a catapult bombarding a fence strikes it, and a second fence and a villager in the stone's splash
  take no hit) and the gate's `test_stonehold_walkthrough` (P36.md, "M8 gate").
- **F40 (lane m8b): an encounter's engines stand down with it.** P29's stand-down (`stand_down_when`)
  changed only the encounter's members; its engines took their team from their faction alone, and a
  stood-down crew still crews (`SiegeEngine.serves`). With Aldmere at war, Fort Aldmere's wall ballistae
  kept their crews and shot the giant who carried Brannock's banner within 150 m of the gate, or held the
  herald's accepted parley (on the real world six 103-damage bolts in the banner's 25 s, two more in the
  parley's next 25 s). Now `EncounterDirector` (its P29 section) stands each engine of the encounter's
  `engines` list down with its members: NEUTRAL while the stand-down holds - an engine at peace never
  shoots (`can_shoot`), and a shot already loosed harms no one (`CombatEntity.can_damage`) - with its
  ranged token given back; when the stand-down ends it takes its faction's standing again
  (`_apply_standing`, the engine's own rule). Applied with the members at every update, as a crew member
  spawns (before the crew takes its engine), and on the tick after any faction change (the engine's own
  faction handler would otherwise re-arm it until the next update). `p29_engine_stood_down(world_id)`
  reports it. SiegeEngine itself is unchanged. Test: the gate's `tests/gate/test_fort_gate_world.gd`
  (Aldmere hostile, the banner and then the accepted parley, the wall crews in the world: no hit from the
  fort; the ballistae Aldmere's again once the token is gone): 4 failures on the old code (both ballistae
  ENEMY while the garrison stood down; the banner's bolts, the parley's), 0 after.
- **F34 (lane m8q): the Grow summary says what siege disruption unlocks.** The shrine's Grow tab, growing
  to stage 6, listed `siege_disruption` as "Wreck siege engines", but blows break an engine at any stage
  (the Aldmere walkthrough breaks all seven at stage 4); what stage 6 adds is seizing a staggered or
  uncrewed engine and hurling it (`PlayerGrab`, the engine's `grab_stage` 6). `PlayerGrowth.PERMISSION_TEXT`
  now reads "Seize a staggered or uncrewed siege engine and hurl it". Test: `test_m8_gate_quests`
  (`test_the_stage_6_grow_summary_says_what_siege_disruption_unlocks`; it failed on the old text).
- **F27 (lane m8p): an engine is one draw call.** The builder merged each engine's Intact and Broken into
  one mesh per material: a ballista or a catapult drew four calls standing and two or three broken, and
  the war camp's six engines in view were 24 of its 46 draw calls at `low` (plan §5.2: at most 40 static
  draw calls a view). `tools/builders/siege.gd` (`_merged`) now merges each group's parts into one
  vertex-coloured mesh "Merged" with the rig material (`SceneKit.merge_vertex_colored`; the parts' colours
  become vertex colours): one call a state. The engine's nodes, collision, hurtbox, grab anchors and
  scripts are unchanged; SiegeEngine finds its meshes generically (`p36_set_draw_range`). The war camp
  46 -> 26, Ballista Ridge 45 -> 30, the Moot battery 38 -> 27 (P36.md, "M8 gate"). Rebuilt: the siege
  builder (`siege_ballista.tscn`, `siege_catapult.tscn`; the crew, the cavalry and the test level came out
