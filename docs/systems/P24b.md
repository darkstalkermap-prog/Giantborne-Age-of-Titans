Wave 15, lane A, beside P26 (growth to stages 4-6). Branch `pkg/P24b`. Plan: `docs/plans/M6_M9_PLAN.md`
§3.7, with §2.2 (the nav layers and the titan gate bits), §2.8 (the streaming and navigation runtime,

## What was built

- **NavBake** (`tools/world/nav_bake.gd`, tools only): one lean navmesh per 256 m chunk and nav layer
  (human 1: radius 0.75 m, height 2, climb 0.25, cells 0.25 / 0.25; large 2: 1.5 / 6 / 0.5, cells
  0.5 / 0.5; titan 16: 4.0 / 19 / 2.0, cells 1.0 / 0.5; 40° slopes), baked from the chunk's
  terrain (the builder's HeightField) with the brief's fixes: the bake AABB grown by the border
  (radius + 2 cells, whole cells) with the terrain source reaching 8 m beyond the chunk, so the mesh
  ends exactly on the chunk edge; the voxel grid's y origin floored to a world-wide multiple of the
  cell height; the obstacles of the chunk and of its eight neighbours (solids, and the props
  sub-scenes' static collision shapes) in every bake. Lean settings: edge error 2 cells, detail
  distance 24 cells, detail error 4 cell heights. Water deeper than 1 m (`MAX_WADE_M`, found on a 2 m
  grid) is cut out of the human and large meshes (a projected obstruction over the deep cells); the
  titan mesh keeps the bed. Titan gate regions: one titan bake, split by polygon - every polygon
  that touches gated ground (the builder's `gate_at(x, z)`: 0 or a gate bit 32 / 64 / 128 for stages
  4 / 5 / 6, sampled at its centre, its corners and every 2 m along its edges; the highest bit wins)
  goes to that bit's navmesh, so a gate region shares its border vertices with the titan region
  exactly. Each mesh is moved down by its layer's `lower_m` (Recast leaves a lean mesh two cell
  heights over flat ground) to sit at the Valley's heights over flat ground (human 0.35, large 0.15,
  titan 0). The rim is left out (`walkable`). `save_chunk` writes `<chunk>_<layer>.res`.
- **The test world's navigation** (`tools/builders/test_world.gd`, rebuilt byte for byte): a gated
  ford - a channel of deep water across the x = 256 seam (c2_0 / c3_0, x 150-360 at z = -380: a 4 m
  bed, banks over 24 m, water at 10 m) whose bed is a gate-4 region of the titan layer (within 18 m
  of its axis): a stage-3 titan mask goes round its ends, a stage-4 mask wades straight across,
  humans and large units keep out of it as out of the pond; the town's two fortified gates carry one
  closed NavigationLink3D per layer (NavLinkHuman / NavLinkLarge / NavLinkTitan through the gate, at
  each layer's height), opened by BreakableProp when the gate breaks; 50 navmeshes in
  `assets/test_world/nav/` named in each ChunkData's `nav`. Three test encounters
  (`data/test/encounter_test_world_*.json`): a wolf pack across the x = -256 seam on the world walk's
  first loop (its runt `max_stage` 2), a soldier band across the x = 0 seam towards the town (its
  sergeant `min_stage` 3), and a pond watch of three soldiers on the pond's east bank (c0_2). The
  level record gains `nav_query` and `player_nav_fallback` 16.
- **NavMaps** (`scripts/world/nav_maps.gd`): the maps of a streamed level - one NavigationServer3D map
  per layer of the world block's `nav_layers` (edge connections off; its cell size and height its
  navmeshes'), the gate bits' regions on the titan map; regions are server RIDs added per chunk
  (`add_region`), links put on their layer's map (`assign_link`), both freed when the chunk unloads
  (`remove_chunk`). Readiness: a chunk is ready once every map it changed has an iteration id past
  the one it had at the chunk's last change. `deterministic` (headless runs, tests): async
  iterations off on every map and every streamed region, so a region added in one frame is in its
  map at the next physics step - the chunk is ready one tick after its last region add, every run.
- **NavQuery** (`scripts/world/nav_query.gd`, static): the one way to ask for a path. The map:
  `level.nav_map(lowest bit of the mask the level has)`. The parameters: `query_path` with a
  NavigationPathQueryParameters3D answered in the same call - unlimited polygons with a distance cap
  by purpose (`chase` 120 m, `far` 300 m) where the level record has a `nav_query` block; without one
  (the Valley, test levels) `map_get_path`'s own (4,096 polygons, no cap), so the Valley's paths do
  not change. The goal is snapped by the query itself (its end polygon is the mask's polygon nearest
  the goal); `closest_point` (flee and safe points, the bots' snapping) answers on the layer's map, keeping to the
  mask (a gate region whose bit the mask lacks is skipped). The budget: `queries_per_tick` per level
  and Sim tick; `path` and `closest_point` always answer and count, callers that can wait
  (an agent's re-path, the director) ask `budget_left()` and `spend()`. `level_waypoint(agent,
  origin)`: the agent's waypoint check made horizontal (Decisions 4).
- **LevelRoot / WorldRoot**: `nav_map(layer := 0)` (the Valley: the default map for every layer;
  WorldRoot: its streamer's map per layer, a gate bit the titan map), and at LevelRoot's anchor
  `nav_layers()` (the layers of the level's Navigation regions; WorldRoot: the world block's),
  `nav_layer_for(layers)` (the lowest of a growth profile's layers the level has, else the record's
  `player_nav_fallback`: a stage-3 giant's layer 4 paths on the titan layer 16 in the world) and
  `nav_query_settings()` (the record's `nav_query`, `{}` without one).
- **ChunkStreamer** (P24a's file, W15): owns the level's NavMaps. Once a loading chunk's own steps are
  done its links join their layer's maps and each navmesh of its ChunkData takes two steps in two
  frames - `nav_load` (deterministic: a synchronous load; else `nav_request`, a threaded request,
  then `nav_load` taking it) and `nav_add` (its region) - each alone in its frame
  (`NAV_HEAVY_STEPS`), except in the loading screen's ring slices; only then is the chunk loaded.
  `is_ready(id)` also waits for `NavMaps.chunk_ready` (so does `ring_ready`, and with it WorldRoot's
  `is_ready()` that main.gd's loading screen waits for). An unload removes the chunk's regions with
  its props (stage 0); an abandoned load frees what it added; the maps go with the streamer.
  `stats()` gains `nav`.
- **EnemyActor** (anchor P24b, and hook lines in `_ready`, `_steer`, `_wants_repath`,
  `_note_repath`): every navigation question through NavQuery on the level of the tree - flight
  points (snapped with `closest_point`, accepted when a path reaches them) and reachability. In
  a level with `nav_query` settings: the agent paths on its layer's own map with unlimited polygons
  and the chase cap; tier-0 re-paths are throttled (at least `repath_min_ticks` 6 apart and only when
  the goal moved `repath_min_move_m` 2 m) and wait for the tick's budget (each one spent); a path
  failure still asks at once (within the budget); the chase goal is snapped to the layer: when the
  agent's path ends more than 2 m from the goal (a giant in deep water) the actor stops at its end
  (the bank) and its progress is measured to it, so standing on the bank is not a path failure; the
  waypoint check is horizontal. Without settings nothing changes.
- **Civilian**: safe points snapped with `NavQuery.closest_point` and accepted by a `NavQuery.path`;
  in a streamed world its agent paths on its layer's map with the far parameters, horizontal
  waypoints. `nav_region()` is gone (`nav_level()`).
- **The debug bots** (`valley_bot.gd`, `bot_diagnosis.gd`, `valley_walk.gd`): every `map_get_path` /
  `region_get_closest_point` call site on NavQuery (`BotDiagnosis.reaches(level, a, b, layers,
  tolerance)`), with the Valley's parameters.
- **EncounterDirector** (anchor P24b, hook lines in `_update`, `_activate`, `_deactivate`,
  `_spawn_waiting`, `_can_activate`, `_spawn`, `_check_cleared`): on a level with a streamer, an
  encounter's chunk is the chunk of its centre (`encounter_chunk`) and it updates only while that
  chunk is loaded and ready (a forced activation waits); when a chunk unloads (`chunk_unloading`,
  before any of its nodes go) every living member standing in it is despawned - not killed: it comes
  back at home, at full health, on the next activation - and every active encounter centred in it
  deactivates; a member spawns only on ground that is loaded and ready (else it waits). Spawn entries'
  `min_stage` / `max_stage` sit members out of an activation by the giant's stage (they neither spawn
  nor keep the encounter from being cleared). `nav_budget_left()` / `spend_nav_queries()` for the
  director's own queries (P30, P37).
- **Schemas and GameData**: the level's `nav_query` (`max_polygons`, `chase_distance_m`,
  `far_distance_m` - a chase cap above the far cap is an error - `repath_min_ticks`,
  `repath_min_move_m`, `queries_per_tick`); the encounter's `arena` and a spawn's `min_stage` /
  `max_stage` (0-10, min above max is an error); GameData's leash rule: every encounter of a streamed
  level has a `leash_radius_m` unless it is an arena boss's (`arena: true`).

## Decisions and deviations

1. **A gate region takes every titan polygon that touches gated ground**, not the polygons whose
   centre lies on it: Recast's long thin polygons along a tile border reach from a bank into the
   ford, and one left in the ungated region let a stage-3 mask cross (found by the slow ford test;
   `split_gates` now samples each polygon's centre, corners and edges every 2 m).
2. **`NavQuery.path` does not call `closest_point` first**: the query snaps the goal itself (its end
   polygon is the mask's polygon nearest the goal), and a closest-point scan of the map costs about
   1 ms on sixteen human chunks - the plan's "goals snapped on the layer map" holds without paying the
   scan twice. `closest_point` serves the callers that need the point (flee and safe points, the bots).
3. **The chase goal's snap is the agent's path end**: when the end of the agent's (chase-capped)
   path lies more than `REACH_SLACK_M` 2 m from its goal, the actor steers to the end and stops there,
   and its progress is measured to it - no second query.
4. **Horizontal waypoints in a streamed world** (`NavQuery.level_waypoint`, not in the plan): a
   NavigationAgent3D advances past a waypoint when its parent stands within `path_desired_distance`
   (0.6 m) of it in 3D, and a lean navmesh lies up to 1.3 m off rolling terrain (brief §5.1). Two
   soldiers of the band stood under a waypoint 0.75 m over their feet, never reaching it, and failed
   their paths (the slow director test). Each movement tick the agent's `path_height_offset` is set to
   its current waypoint's height over the actor's feet. Enemies and civilians in a world with
   `nav_query` settings only; the Valley keeps offset 0.
5. **`LevelRoot.nav_map(layer := 0)` replaces `nav_map()` in place** (GDScript has no overloads; the
   default argument keeps every caller); the other three functions sit at the anchor.
6. **Asynchronous iterations stay on for interactive desktop play** (the engine's project setting):
   decision 13 turns them off "when deterministic", and readiness by iteration id holds either way.
   With them on, the gate test fails: two walks part by two ticks (tried on the branch).
7. **An encounter updates only while its centre chunk is loaded and ready** (§2.8 says the director
   indexes encounters by chunk): an encounter whose chunk is still streaming in neither activates nor
   leashes nor deactivates, and `force_activate` waits for it.
8. **The leash rule lives in GameData's post-pass** (anchor P24b): every encounter of a level with a
   `world` block needs `leash_radius_m` unless `arena: true`.
9. **The gate test compares the AUTOPILOT_RESULT lines without the probe's block and the wall-time
   fields** (every key ending in `_ms` or holding `_ms_`: the streaming work, the frame times) - those
   cannot be byte-identical - **and adds an AI trace**: the world walk's result carries no AI state,
   and `world_walk.gd` and `autopilot.gd` are not P24b's files, so the walk runs on
   `tests/support/test_world_traced.tscn` (the test world with a `WorldAiTrace` node printing a
   "P24B_AI" line every half second), and all 107 lines must be identical too.
10. **The unreachable chase spawns its 20 soldiers directly** (no director, so the pond watch does not
    add three) round the pond and keeps them aware with a shout every half second, as an alert group
    would; the giant wades back and forth over 20 m of 5 m deep water, so the goals move and the
    throttle and the budget work; at one tick all twenty are asked to re-path at once (the budget
    serves them 8, 8 and 4).
11. **The band's path failures are read when each soldier reaches the giant**: without avoidance
    (deferred, plan §0.4) a soldier blocked behind another at the giant's side reports a failure, which
    is crowding, not navigation.
12. **The test world's wolf pack, band and pond watch** are this package's test encounters (owned
    `data/test/encounter_test_world_*.json`); they meet the world walk's route, so P24a's slow world
    suites now run with AI members chasing (their times below).
13. **EnemyBrain needed no change**: the layer maps, the throttle, the budget, the chase goal and the
    flee snapping all sit in EnemyActor's anchor (the brain asks for destinations; the actor paths).
14. **main.gd needed no change**: its world loading screen waits for `WorldRoot.is_ready()`, which now
    includes navigation (every ring chunk's maps synced), and `hold_until_navigation_ready` finds no
    region nodes in a streamed world (its regions are server RIDs) and returns at once; the traced
    world walks start with 0 warnings.

## Known gaps

- **An alert group keeps itself aware while one member is** (P10a's sharing, not this package's): when
  the giant leaves an encounter whose chunk stays loaded (a teleport two chunks away in a test), each
  member whose memory runs out hears an aware one again, so the encounter never deactivates; an
  unloading chunk still ends it at once. The stage test makes the pack lose the giant first.
- Real browsers and the reference PC are **NOT RUN**: the web numbers below are Chromium with
  software WebGL on the shared container (indicative only).
- The navmeshes are the test world's: P25 bakes the real world with NavBake and re-measures.
- **The query budget bounds the count, not the time of a tick**: eight human-layer queries in one
  tick cost about 6-12 ms in the container (above), so the worst ticks of a crowded fight can pass
  8 ms while the p95 holds; the web is assumed 1.3-2.2 times slower. P30 (formations) and P39 (the
  world performance capture) should watch the worst ticks and lower `queries_per_tick` if they suffer.
- Agent avoidance stays deferred (§0.4): members crowding at a target report path failures.
- **The `Web (test)` export's pck is over the M6 budget**: 7.56 MB as shipped against 7.5 MB, with the
  test world's 0.47 MB of navmeshes (the release's is 6.54 MB, which the budget is for: test content
  stays out of it; the M7 budget is 21 MB). `tools/build_web.sh --preset="Web (test)"` warns and
  builds; the stream check is unaffected.

## M7 gate (wave 18): searches within the budget

The per-tick path-query budget (`nav_query.queries_per_tick` 8) held for re-paths, the director and
the formations, but not for the actors' searches: a firing-point search (P09's ring of 9 candidates,
P22's front arc of up to 15) and a flee search (P04's 13 directions) tried every candidate at once,
each a closest point and up to two paths. On the world a Raiders' Hollow archer or mage spent 32-34
queries in one tick (17-27 ms) every 1-3 s while the giant stood in Brannock 200 m away: no candidate
around a target beyond the 120 m chase cap can be reached, every one failed, the search fell back to an
unreachable point and ran again at the next decision. Now, in a level with `nav_query` settings
(`EnemyActor`'s P24b section, `EnemyBrain`'s):

- a search tries a candidate only while `NavQuery.budget_left()` has 3 queries and the actor's own
  share of the tick (`SEARCH_QUERIES_PER_TICK`, one candidate) allows it; otherwise it stops there,
  sets `brain.nav_search_pending` and resumes at that candidate at the next decision (within
  `SEARCH_RESUME_TICKS`); a pending flee or rout search is not taken for "cornered";
- `target_out_of_reach()` skips the firing search without a query when the target lies beyond the chase
  cap from the unit (plus its band) or beyond its band outside the unit's encounter leash;
- a firing search that found nothing holds (no unreachable fallback) and waits `FIRING_REPICK_S` before
  the next one;
- a resolver that chains searches resumes the stage that stopped and does not search the stages before
  it again meanwhile (`search_resuming()`): a ranged unit's resolver tries P22's front arc, then P09's
  ring, and a routed formation member's tries P30's rout point, then P04's flight (`_p30_rout_failed`).
  Searching the first stage again at every decision spent the tick's share and overwrote where the
  second stopped, so the second never ran: an archer whose front arc found nothing searched it again
  for good and held where it stood (found in the gate's own review of this fix);
- `search_queries_peak` records the most an actor's searches spent in one tick.

Levels without settings (the Valley, test levels) search as before. Measured (the reviewer's world walk
`--walk-stage=4` past the Hollow to Brannock, headless): `cpu_tick_ms` max 36.1 -> 10.4-14.0 ms (two
runs), p99 7.4 -> 5.6-5.7 ms (11.2 and 5.2 ms before the chain fix above); no actor spent more than 3
queries in a tick nor any tick more than 8. Slow tests in
`tests/slow/test_m7_gate_world.gd`: the Hollow's archers and mage kept aware of a giant 170-220 m away
for 20 s (at most 2 queries a tick, the actors' phase at most 4 ms; before: up to 51 queries a tick and
37 ms), a firing search with nothing to find waiting while the budget is spent, then taking one
candidate a tick until it ends, the same through an archer's own resolver (the front arc's candidates,
then the ring's, then the end: 16 calls; before the chain fix still searching after 60), and a routed
member's flight resuming after its rout point failed (the failure marked by the test: a real navmesh
always gives the rout point a partial path's end).

## M7 polish

**Civilians' safe points within the budget** (the M7 gate's follow-up in `docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md`).
The gate budgeted the enemies' searches, but `Civilian._resolve_safe_point` still tried every safe
marker at once - a closest point and a far path each - and its agent's path to the chosen marker was
never counted: with Brannock's eight villagers deciding in the same tick as the town turned hostile,
one tick ran 16 queries NavQuery counted and 8 agent paths it did not. Now, in a level with
`nav_query` settings (`scripts/actors/civilian.gd`):

- the search tries a marker only while `NavQuery.budget_left()` has `SEARCH_CANDIDATE_QUERIES` (2)
  and the civilian's own share of the tick (`SEARCH_QUERIES_PER_TICK`, one marker) allows; otherwise
  it stops at that marker, `CivilianBrain.nav_search_pending` says so, and the actor decides again the
  next tick, where the search resumes (within `SEARCH_RESUME_TICKS`) - the same choice, so
  `brain.picks` still counts choices, and a waiting search is not "nowhere reachable" (no `REPICK_S`
  wait);
- the agent's path to its marker waits for the tick's budget and is spent (`NavQuery.spend`), as an
  enemy's re-path is; the civilian stands while it waits;
- `search_queries_peak` records the most one civilian's searches spent in a tick.

Levels without settings (the Valley, test levels) search as before. Measured on the real world
(`tests/slow/test_m7_polish_world.gd`, all eight deciding in one tick): at most 8 queries a tick
(the agent paths counted), every villager in shelter by 16.7 s as before; `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`,
"The M7 polish (lane B)".

**Edge errors on the human map** (the M7 gate review: c4_1, c4_2, c5_2 and c6_6, four "Navigation
region synchronization had 4 edge error(s)" warnings each on every sync, so long world walks printed
them over and over). A map joins polygons by keying every edge on its two vertices floored to the map's
merge-rasterizer cell, the cell size and height x `merge_rasterizer_cell_scale`; more than two edges on
one key is an edge error and none of them connects, so human paths there could fail or detour. Godot
4.6.3's `NavMap3D` starts that cell at the unscaled defaults (0.25, 0.25, 0.25) and applies its 0.1
scale only when the cell size or height *changes* (and a set to the scale it already holds is skipped):
the large (0.5 / 0.5) and titan (1.0 / 0.5) maps keyed their edges in 0.05 m and 0.1 / 0.05 m cells, but
the human map, whose navmeshes use exactly the defaults, kept 0.25 m cells. The detail triangulation
leaves pairs of vertices 5-7 cm apart with sliver triangles between them (one pair in each of those
four meshes): one 0.25 m cell, four edges on one key, four errors. Now:

- `NavMaps.setup` sets every layer map's merge scale explicitly (`MERGE_RASTERIZER_SCALE` 0.1, through
  half of it first so the engine applies it: `NavMaps.set_merge_scale`), so every layer keys its edges
  at its cell x 0.1 - the human map at 0.025 m. Measured on the world's 206 navmeshes (every chunk's
  regions of each layer on one map): 16 edge errors -> 0, 8 more edges merged, and the same 455 human
  seam connections between regions (large 355 and titan 572 unchanged; the test world's 70 / 66 / 179
  unchanged too). No navmesh was rebuilt.
- `NavBake` welds every bake in the same cells (`weld_for_map`: vertices sharing a key become one and
  its collapsed slivers go - a weld inside one key changes no edge key, so seams still meet) and checks
  it (`edge_conflicts`: a key left with more than two edges is a build error naming the chunk's origin
  and the place; `merge_cell` and `point_key` key as the server does, in single precision). No bake of
  either world has such a pair at the fine cells: rebuilding c4_1, c4_2, c5_2, c6_6 and their
  neighbours wrote the same bytes.
- Tests: `tests/integration/test_m7_polish_ai.gd` (a NavMaps human map merges a 5 cm sliver without an
  edge error; it had two at 0.25 m) and `tests/slow/test_m7_polish_world.gd` (every chunk's navmeshes of
  every layer of the real and the test world, loaded through NavMaps, merge with no edge error; on a
  failure it names the chunks that fail alone). Both fail without the explicit scale (the slow one:
  "16 edge errors ...; alone: c4_1 (4), c4_2 (4), c5_2 (4), c6_6 (4)").
