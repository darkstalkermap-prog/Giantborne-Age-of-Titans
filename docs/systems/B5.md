# B5 — The milestone B gate's fix-up: the duels' leak, the Rootwarden in his grove, the Valley after the aim fix

B5 answers what the B4 gate left open (`docs/systems/B4.md`: G11, G10 and its "not run"), in that order, on `pkg/b5`
from the dev head (`7e84380`, `0.15.0-m15`; the version stays). The decisions are B5-1..B5-6 (`docs/DECISIONS.md`
§2.7) and §3.10; the numbers are `docs/BALANCE_LOG.md`, "B5"; what is left is in `docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md`, "From the
milestone B gate's fix-up (B5)". Evidence outside the repository (the hand-back names the folder): the harness's dumps
and diffs, the grove duels' traces and the Valley batch's results.

## 1. G11: why a seeded duel depended on what ran before it

**The harness** (`tools/duel_leak/`, decision B5-1): `test_duel_leak.gd`, a suite run on its own
(`run_tests.tscn -- --suite=res://tools/duel_leak/test_duel_leak.gd`, the runner's new `--suite=`), fights one duel
(Stoneback's in his quarry, or the Rootwarden's in his real grove) alone or after another fight in the same process
(`--leak-pre=king`: the Ogre King's stage-2 duel to its end) and writes the duel's state every tick: every script
variable of the giant, the titan and the duel bot and of what they hold three levels down (the brain and its timers,
the controllers, the pools, the plates, the camera rig, BotCombat), their places, velocities and RNG states, and Sim's
globals (the tick's phase, the crit rng and every named stream, the tokens, the launch slots, the attack instance
counter), the aim deferral's held swings, NavQuery's count and the projectiles' id. `duel_leak_diff.py` takes Sim's
tick offset out of both dumps and prints the first tick and every field at which they part.

**What it found.** Stoneback's stage-3 duel, seed 1, alone and after the King's duel (`--leak-legacy`: the suites' start
before B5) parts **at the duel's first tick**, in 67-78 fields: the titan stands at z -20.47 moving at 7.4 m/s in the
first run and at -22.0 still in the second, his brain in another state (3 against 2) with another perception, last
sighting and state clock, the camera rig elsewhere. Nothing global differed but the counters (attack instances,
projectile ids) and the aim deferral's stale swings. The cause is the duels' own start: each waited for "a whole
second of Sim's clock" (`Sim.tick % 60 == 59`) *after* its level was loaded - and the titan's AI runs from the load.
How many ticks that wait took (0-59) depended on Sim's tick at the load, that is on how long every earlier suite had
run, and in those ticks Stoneback (awake in his quarry) already saw the giant at its spawn and walked at it. The first
duel of a process differed from the second (A2's note) for the same reason. The grove's duel had no wait at all and
never reseeded Sim: its tick phase (the actors perceive and decide on ticks of their own phase of Sim's clock,
`EnemyActor._phase_offset`) and the crit rng were whatever the earlier suites left (alone and after the King: phase 39
against 26, the warden's facing at the first tick 105.0 against 103.7 degrees).

**The fix** (decision B5-1): `TestWait.duel_start` - every titan duel starts on a whole second with Sim reseeded,
*before* its level is made - in all six titan suites and the harness. With it the two runs above agree tick for tick
over the whole duel (Stoneback: 5,544 ticks, both dead at 92.4 s - A5's number; the grove: the first 4,603 ticks, all
the dumps held). A2's warm-up duel is gone (`test_stoneback_seeded`). And one game-side leak the harness showed, not
the cause but of the same kind: the aim deferral (`HitFactory._aimed_swing`) cleared every held swing once it held 64,
at a moment the earlier fights' stale swings decided; it now drops only swings older than 10 s
(`DEFERRED_KEEP_TICKS`), so only a fight's own ticks decide. Nothing else of an earlier fight reaches a later one: the
game's crits keep drawing from Sim's one stream (the session's randomness, as any input timing is), and a player's
fight after another fight is the same fight given the same inputs at the same tick phase.

**A second, finer leak: the physics space.** With the start fixed, the Rootwarden's grove duel at stage 6 still won at
322.1 s run alone and at 291.1 s run after the stage-5 grove duel in the same process. The harness (sampled every 30
ticks over both whole duels, `--leak-pre=grove`) found no difference in any field until the duel's tick 5,910, where the
giant's velocity, facing and the camera's yaw parted in the seventh digit (7.223046 against 7.223033 m/s) as it slid
along a trunk: nothing the fight held had leaked - the bodies of the earlier fight, freed from the one shared physics
space, had left its broadphase in another order, so the contacts of a slide were met in another order and came out a
rounding apart, which a fight of minutes grows into another fight. **The fix**: `TestWait.duel_host` - every titan duel
in its own physics space (a SubViewport with its own World3D, nothing drawn into it, given the 120 m floor every
suite's world starts with, as a process's first duel had it). With it the two stage-6 duels agree at every sample to
the same 322.1 s, every suite gives the same numbers alone and in the titans' tier, and the numbers are those of a
process's first duel (the Ogre King's, Stoneback's and the grove's did not move; the Rootwarden's arena duels after
the first did, BALANCE_LOG "B5"). In the game this is not a fairness matter: a rounding in the seventh digit is far
below a player's own inputs.

**The new test** (`tests/titans/test_stoneback_seeded.gd`): Stoneback's seeded duel, then the Ogre King's fight to its
end, then the same duel again - tick for tick the same (every hit, his plates, his sequence, both places); and the
deferral's pruning.

## 2. G10: the Rootwarden in his real grove

### Why the grove was harder

B4 measured stage 5 dying 3 of 3 in the grove. Part of that was G11: with the duel's start fixed (and nothing else
changed), the grove's stage-5 duels gave a win, a death and a near-win (him at 5 % at the 600 s limit) - the B4 numbers
were each one draw of a start the earlier suites set. The rest was found by watching the duels: an instrumented duel
(a scratch suite: every blow taken with what the bot was doing in the 2.5 s before it - evading, how fast, against a
wall and against what) traced every 0.1 s, in the grove and on the arena floor, then the walls the giant met named by
their colliders.

| What | Found | Player or bot? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The dead trees** (six, 1.3-1.9 m thick, 12-26 m from his spot) | Solid: the giant's evasions ran into them (a fifth to three quarters of its wall contacts in a duel), and a pole between the giant and him stops its blows (the hit's line of sight). On the test arena they are scenery without collision. | Both: a 15 m giant snagged on a rotten pole in the middle of his reach is the level's fault |
| **The ring's south trunks** | His spot is 14 m south of the hollow's centre: four trunks stood 34-43 m from him, their bark 30-39 m off - within a step of his root-lash's 25 m reach plus a stage-5 giant's own size. Out of the lash's reach on that side meant against a trunk (trunk 11 alone: 226 of 531 wall samples in one duel). | Both: there was no room to stand out of his reach there |
| **The eruption's first burst** | Its impact radius (5 m) reached past the telegraphed line (6.5 m wide): a giant that had stepped off the line drawn on the ground was struck. The same on the arena. | The fight's fault |

### What changed (decisions B5-2..B5-4)

- **The grove** (`tools/data/world/greywood.json`): the dead trees have no solids (rotten poles a giant shoulders
  through, as on the arena); the four ring trunks south of his spot stand 46-48 m from him. Chunks `c0_3`-`c1_5` and
  their nav layers, the skyline's landmarks and the map rebuilt with the world builder (`--chunks=c0_4`); a second
  build changes no byte; the seam check passes.
- **The eruption** (`data/abilities/rootwarden_erupt.json`): its first burst is as wide as its telegraph (3.25 m
  either side). Stoneback's smash had the same fault (its root ball's 4.5 m impact on a 5.5 m-wide line): 2.75 m.
  `test_stoneback_seeded.test_every_titan_strike_stays_inside_its_telegraph`: no strike of the three titans reaches
  past what its telegraph shows (a charge's line, his run, aside).
- **The duel bot** (`scripts/debug/titan_duel_bot.gd`): `see_obstacles` - an evasion goes the open way (off a line,
  the open side; out of a ring or a circle, the nearest open turn of the way out; a sphere of its size cast at its shins
  and its middle), on in the Rootwarden's duels only: the King's fight was measured against the old evasions (with it
  on his keg broke in 1 duel of 7 seeds, not seed 2's at 119.0 s) and Stoneback's keep the evasions A3's baiting into
  stone was tuned with.
  `steady_pick` - the lock-switch's pick kept until it breaks, grows back or goes out of reach, as a player's is
  (decision A5-1) - and `_shin_pick`: a broken knot is not picked again (its work is done; back to his shins, the
  topple). These two for every titan (the King's duels are unchanged by them; of Stoneback's only stage 3 seed 1, dead
  at 60.0 s instead of 92.4 s, and the Quarry set's seed 3 moved).

### Then the numbers (decision B5-5)

With the grove made fair and the bot fixed, stage 5 in the grove won 1 of 3 and the arena's stage-4 duels came close to
winning (seed 2 alive at the limit with him at 7 %): the fixed bot fights better everywhere. His health went
12,500 -> **11,500** (stage 5 wins sooner: the arena's wins were 7.7-9.3 minutes against an aim of about 8) and his
phase-1 root-lash 58 -> **66** (the phase a stage-4 giant fights in: it cannot reach his knot, while stages 5 and 6 break
it in the first minute). The tables are `docs/BALANCE_LOG.md` "B5".

**Stoneback** moved too, by G11 not G10: from the fixed start his stage-5 duels at seeds 1-3 all died (him at 56, 3 and
13 %) - A4's "at least one win of three" had held on one draw of the old start. The smash's narrower root ball alone
did not bring a win; his health 16,000 -> **13,000** did: stage 5 wins two of three (three of five over seeds 1-5) in
2:08-2:44, stages 3 and 4 never win, stage 6 wins all three - in 2:12-2:28, under A4's "about three to five minutes"
(FOLLOWUPS).

| --- | --- | --- |

The grove and the arena now agree: stage 4 deadly (never a win; on the arena three deaths, in the grove two and a
stand-off: rooted in his first phase he struck that giant three times in ten minutes), stage 5 a hard fight won two of three in 6.7-7.9 minutes, stage
6 fair (three of three in 4.3-5.9 minutes). The giant's wall contacts in the grove fell from 8-10 % of the fight to
3-5 %. The step-by-step table (the start fixed, then the grove, then the bot, then the numbers) is BALANCE_LOG "B5".

### The arena's stage-6 loss of a seed stage 5 won (B3's oddity)

B3's stage-6 seed 1 died at 252.3 s with him at 45 % having broken only his knot (the eruption 948 of its health) while
stage 5 won that seed. Watched: its eruption hits came as it stepped off the line backwards into a brazier (two of four
while against one: 43-54 % of the 2.5 s before them; a stage-6 giant's 3.7 m body meets the braziers 14-16 m from him
where a stage 5's 2.9 m slips past), and its lock, re-picked every tick, kept going back to his broken knot - at a
stage-6 giant's chest height, a broken plate the lock prefers - so it never broke a shin and never brought him down
(zero downs). Not the lock's rule in the game (a player's pick is kept, and the knot is a fair weak point) but the
bot's play: with the open-way evasions the duel wins (396.5 s before the tuning, 325.2 s after), and the bot no longer
picks a broken knot again (`_shin_pick`: its work done, back to his shins), so stage 6 topples him as stage 5 does
(12-14 downs a duel; with the pick kept but the knot still allowed, none: it kept the knot).

## 3. The Ogre King after the aim fix: the Valley's bot batch

P17b's 15 runs (the four outcomes x novice seeds 1-3, two assisted, one expert) on B5's final Valley code against B2's
before and after (BALANCE_LOG "B5"): every run done, **no novice death at the King (0 of 14)**, his damage a boss
attempt 0.91 of the giant's health at the median (B2: 0.85; the range 0.60-1.09 against 0.73-1.06), the boss segment
52.9 s (52.4), the assisted 0.23-0.24 (0.21-0.29), the expert untouched in 80.5 s (75.3), deaths elsewhere the same two
runs. The band holds: nothing was changed for it. (The aim fix's direction: a novice locked on his bell now strikes the
bell, which keeps a share of each blow, until it breaks.)
