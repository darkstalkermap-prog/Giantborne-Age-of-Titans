# C4 — Settlement development: Last Hearth's three tiers, and the older settlements' second

Milestone C's last content package, on `pkg/c4` from C3b's merge (`089eb03`). The version stays **0.16.0-m16** and the
save stays **schema 3**. The owner's bar - "a reason to be a Giant", "realistic and not clunky", "look natural" - is read
here as: a settlement grows only because of work no man could do, the giant does that work with his body (not in a
menu), and each tier is a visible, physical change in the world made of what the place has. Decisions C4-1..C4-12 are
in `docs/DECISIONS.md` §2.7; what is left is FOLLOWUPS "From C4".

## 1. The design in plain words

A settlement is a record (`data/settlements/settlement_*.json`, schema `settlement`) of **tiers**. Tier 0 is the place as
the giant finds it. Each tier above it is earned by one or two **projects**, and a project by its **needs**:

- **haul** - loads the giant carries in both arms from where they lie to the project's site: a great timber from the
  Windfall (the old kings' frost-killed pines), a king-stone from the toppled pier of the Kings' Causeway, a quarry
  block, a heartwood trunk, an ashlar block;
- **give** - materials from his pack: hides, iron, titan iron, a titan's own parts;
- **deed** - something done in the world, any quest condition: the rockfall heaved off the Hearth path, the trolls
  driven off the crags, the Rimward's raid beaten back, the dire pack on the pilgrims' track killed.

When every need is met the giant **raises** the work himself at its site (E: "Raise the roof-tree", "Set the
gate-stones", "Lift the beacon's crown", "Bed the Colossus Anvil"): dust, a camera shake, the raising's sound, and the
work stands at once. The tier's **life** - its folk, fires, smoke, banners, the garrison on the walls - comes once he has
gone beyond the settlement (`settle_m`) or on a later load: A3-4's "the settlement changes behind the giant's back",
generalised. The older settlements' titan aftermaths are now their first tier, raised by their own folk the same way
(an `auto` project).

The panel is the settlement's **tally-board** (at Last Hearth beside Maera by the fire) and the Character menu's new
**Settlements** tab: the tier track, the projects being earned, what each still needs (where a load lies, a Give button
for what the pack holds), how it is raised and what its tier will give; the trades; every tier's words and unlocks. The
map draws a roof over a developed settlement's mark and a pip per tier; the HUD says "Last Hearth: Roof the hall - ready
to raise", "Last Hearth: The Roofed Hall (tier 1)" and, when its life has come, the tier's line.

Six king-stones and three great timbers in all: a stage-6 giant carries at 0.7 of his pace, about 35-45 s each way.

**What it looks like.** Built at a man's scale (so a 19-29 m giant over it reads as one), of the place's own stuff,
weathered by the frost material (snow on what faces up, lichen, grime): the hall is a dry-stone longhall laid in rough
courses, at tier 0 tumbled at one end with its old roof-timbers fallen in and a hide awning over the other; roofed, it has
crucks of whole timbers, the ridge-tree the giant carried standing proud of both gables, a turf roof over a thatch skirt,
rafters' ends, a sooty smoke-hole, gable boards, and the fire's glow in its door (a lit flame and an orange light inside).
The ring is a curtain of dry-stone on a rubble bank with a ragged turf top, bowed out round the waystone and the lookout,
its gate a trilithon of the three king-stones the giant carried with timber leaves swung open, firing steps of rubble and
turf behind it. The beacon is a tower of four king-stone drums with a ladder of lashed poles, a whole pine for its mast and
an iron basket with its fire (lit with tier 3's life, seen from the Breach). Huts of dry-stone with conical turf roofs
replace two of the tents when tier 1's folk arrive; the stall, banners, a woodstack. C1's canvas ridge tents are hide
tents on poles now (their roofs drew half-culled under the frost material: flat pale sheets). Every load set down shows at
its site (a king-stone drum, a great timber) until the work is raised.

**The raid** (`event.hearth_raid`, from tier 1): three shieldmen, two pikemen and two archers come over the upland from
the east hunting the Hearth's folk, and a rimkin warleader holds the giant; the folk run for the hall; three dead lose it.
The tier matters: from tier 2 the ring's four archers loose beside him (`SettlementGarrison`: a post per firing step, each
tied to its archer of `encounter.hoarfell_hearth_garrison` - no archer, no arrows), from tier 3 six and the beacon's great
bow. The garrison's hits go through Sim's resolution as the garrison's (no XP or loot for it). Defended: 200 XP, and the
beacon's project's deed. Fell: the dead are mourned, and newcomers take their places once the giant is away (the record's
`mends`).

## 4. How it is built

- **Rules** (`scripts/progression/settlement_rules.gd`, pure): records, tiers, a project's status (each need's have / want
  / met), wants_load / deliver, can_give / give, can_raise / raise (the tier on its last project), the flags a tier holds
  and the flags a state owes the world, the tier the flags say, trades, `written_flags`, the cross-record checks.
- **The live side** (`scripts/world/settlement_work.gd`): the session's state (`GameSession.expansion.settlements`), the
  world's flags, the inventory, `Events.settlement_*`, a checkpoint after each change; deliver, give, raise (refused in a
  fight: `Armour.change_blocked`), trade, rest, `mend_flags`, `debug_set_tier`.
- **SettlementWatch** (`scripts/world/settlement_watch.gd`, a runtime node of both campaign levels): mends the flags and
  settles every built tier as a level loads; brings a tier's life when the giant is away; raises auto projects; mends a
  raid's dead; announces a project ready.
- **The giant's side** (`scripts/actors/player/player_haul.gd`, the `haul` component): take_up, deliver_to, put_back; the
  speed multiplier, the refused edges, the two-handed hold pose (the grab's), the load's mesh (`scenes/world/haul_loads.tscn`,
  built by `tools/builders/forge.gd`, scaled by his height).
- **The world's nodes**: `HaulSource`, `SettlementSite`, `SettlementBoard` (its look `scenes/world/settlement_board.tscn`),
  `SettlementRest`, `SettlementGarrison`; the world builder's kinds `haul_source`, `settlement_site`, `settlement_board`,
  `settlement_rest`, `garrison`, `settlement_fire` (emissive flames, a doorway's glow, an optional flickering light that
  fades with distance; FLAG_WRAP) and `settlement` (styles hall, hut, ring, pile, windfall, beacon, stall, banners, stack,
  frame, tent and `group`: several in one mesh). `condition_forge` is FLAG_WRAP now (a forge not raised yet is hidden, its
  prompt and mark with it: `PlayerInteract` and the place marker skip what is hidden) and takes a `look_scale`.
- **Flagged meshes** are saved as compressed resources beside the structures meshes
  (`assets/world/structures/flags/<id>.res`), not inside the props scene's text.
- **The forge's colossus tier** (`Forge.TIERS`, `DataValidator.FORGE_TIERS`, `Progression.FORGE_TIER_RANK`): five
  rimforged weapons (`weapon.*_rimforged`, fit stage 8), the titanforged weapons' upgrade.
- **UI**: `ui/screens/settlement_screen.gd` (ScreenStack `settlement`), the Character menu's Settlements tab, the map's tier
  marks (`MapScreen.settlement_tier_of`), the discovery toast's settlement lines.
- **Sound**: `settle_load` (two), `raise_work`, `tier_horn` in `gen_world_sfx.py`; `sfx.settlement.set_down`, `.raise`,
  `.tier` (bank_world_objects.json); the router's handlers (the garrison: the arrow's launch, the great bow's bolt).
- **The tour**: `tools/world_tour.gd` places `c4_hearth_tiers`, `c4_hearth_work`, `c4_older_settlements`, `c4_film`
  (free cameras, a capture-only dusk, the HUD hidden for the stills, settlement steps).

## What is weak (judged from the stills and the film, bluntly)

- **It is still a low-poly world's settlement.** From a giant's eye and from far off Last Hearth now reads as a place that
  grew - a roofed longhall, a ring with a gate, huts, tents, a beacon, smoke - but up close every piece is a primitive: the
  dry-stone courses are seeded lumps in rows, the turf roof and its thatch skirt are two flat slabs a side, the huts are
  cones on stone rings that read as tents, the gate-stones are smooth pale drums, banners are flat boards. No snow banked
  against walls, no tracks, no interior to the hall. It meets "natural materials, not white boxes" better than C1b's
  boxes, not "realistic".
- **Nothing is built in front of you.** A load set down appears beside the site; a work raised appears at once under dust,
  a shake and its sound. The giant's carry is the grab's two-handed hold (the load across his arms at the belly), with no
  lift, set-down or levering motion, and the folk stand where their encounters put them - nobody lays turf or stacks
  stone. The tier's life "arriving behind his back" is honest to A3-4 but means the change is a pop when he returns.
- **The raid is thin.** Seven men and a rimkin against a stage-6 to 8 giant; the garrison's arrows (that cannot miss)
  kill most of the men before they reach the folk in the film's run. The tier matters - the archers are there or not - but
  the event is not yet a fight a colossus feels; its balance was not measured by a bot or a person.
- **Firelight by day is barely there**, and the night is the capture's dusk (the game has no night): the hall's door glows
  and lights the ground before it, the pits and the beacon burn; nothing else is lit.
- **The older settlements' changes are small and plain**: Millford's masons' hall reads as an A-frame hut on a ring of
  stones beside its stacks; Hollowmere's moot-house is a timber frame under thatch; the toll-keep's mended breach is two
  stacks of ashlar and a frame; Thornwick's bell hangs in a frame of four posts over the square (the Valley's own blocky
  look round it).
- **The carried loads are modest at stage 8** (a 17 m timber at stage 7 grows with him: the size reads, the weight
  does not - no strain in the pose, no slower turn).
- **The existing smithy's smoke shows a dark disc at the chimney's top** in some views (C3's look, seen in the Hearth's
  and Millford's forges; not changed here).
- **Hauling takes time**: Last Hearth's six king-stones and three timbers are about six minutes of carrying for a stage-6
  giant; whether that is satisfying or a chore was not played by a person.
