## What was built

### Discovery (`scripts/world/discovery.gd`, `class_name Discovery`)

The runtime node `Discovery` that `LevelRoot.RUNTIME_NODES` makes in `level.valley` and `level.world`
(`LevelRoot.discovery()` finds it, anchor P27). Every 30 ticks (a Sim `POST_RESOLVE` hook, order 160:
after Quests, before the waystones and GameSession; `tick % 30 == 0`, so the timeline is the same
however the level was entered) it calls `scan(position, stage, tick)` for the living giant, in POI id
order; `scan()` is the whole check, so tests and tools call it for any point. Everything it learns is
a WorldState flag (saved with every checkpoint, rolled back with a reload):

| Flag | Set when |
| --- | --- |
| `discovered.<poi short>` = `true` | the giant stands within the POI's `radius_m` at its `min_stage` or above; a sealed place (`kind` `secret`) only once its seal is open (the seal's world delta destroyed, opened or taken). Its `xp` once (`Events.xp_award_requested(xp, "discovery:<poi id>")`), then `Events.poi_discovered`. A vista then reveals the places around it. |
| `discovered.<poi short>` = `"revealed"` | a found vista's `reveals_m` (default 400 m) covers a non-secret POI: on the map (dim) and the compass, no XP and no event until the visit, which overwrites it with `true` and pays (decision 33). |
| `sealed.<poi short>.seen`, `sealed.seen_count` | a sealed place comes within 60 m (the count is the sealed-place tutorial's trigger). |
| `map.seen.<chunk>` | the giant enters a chunk of a chunked level (the level record's `world` block: `chunk_size_m`, `grid`, `origin_m`); a found vista also clears every chunk within its `reveals_m`. The Valley has no chunks, so no fog. |
| `region.<short>.entered` | `Events.region_changed` names the region (WorldRoot's 15 m hysteresis); the region's `discover_xp` once (`"discovery:<region id>"`). |

`visited.<checkpoint>` (fast travel's) stays GameSession's (P20, on `checkpoint_reached`). Static,
pure helpers: `poi_table(level)` (the level's `poi` records merged over its index's POIs), `chunk_grid`,
`chunk_of`, `chunk_rect`, `chunks_within`, `is_found` (`== true`), `is_known` (truthy), `seal_open`,
`short_id`, the flag names, `pin_of(flags)` and `pin_on(flags, level)` (the pin `[x, z, level]`, only
on its own level), `of_tree()`. `found_log` keeps what the node found (`visit`, `reveal` with the
vista as `by`, `sealed`, `region`).

### The HUD and its draw calls (`ui/hud/hud_kit.gd`, `ui/hud.gd`, the widgets)

Godot 4.6's Compatibility canvas renderer batches runs of the same kind of command with the same
texture, in draw order. Measured under Xvfb (llvmpipe): a `StyleBoxFlat` is a triangle array and
never batches (one draw call per panel, and the run after it starts over), as are `draw_circle` and
coloured polygons; plain rectangles (`draw_rect`, `canvas_item_add_rect`, `ColorRect`) batch, rotated
ones too; a Label's outline glyphs live in another texture than its fill glyphs (two calls per
outlined label), while a drop shadow without an outline uses the fill texture; each font size has its
own glyph texture; a clip rectangle starts a new batch. So `HudKit` builds the HUD from parts that
batch:

- `HudKit.Box`: a StyleBox drawn as rectangles (the fill and a 1 px border as four thin rectangles),
  used by `HudKit.panel()`; `HudKit.bar()` (a ProgressBar with Box styles), `HudKit.rect()`;
- `HudKit.label()`: text with a drop shadow and no outline, in one of four sizes (11, 14, 18, 26);
- `draw_diamond`, `draw_diamond_outline`, `draw_pointer`, `draw_dot`: shapes made of rotated or
  stacked rectangles instead of polygons and circles; `draw_text` for shadowed drawn text;
- absolute z layers below 0 (`z_as_relative` off): every rectangle on `Z_BACK` (-60), each text size
  on its own layer (-59..-56), the edge overlays (threat arrows, the objective marker: -50/-49), the
  death overlay (-40/-39) and the help overlay (-30/-29). A view's HUD is one batch of rectangles plus
  one per text size on screen; everything stays under the modal screens and the debug panel (z 0 on
  the same canvas layer).
- `HudKit.batch_breakers(root)` lists what would break this (a StyleBoxFlat, an outline, a shadow
  outline, clipping, a panel or text left on the default layer); `test_hud_layout` checks the live HUD
  with every part built.

`ui/hud.gd` and every widget were moved onto these parts (the resources panel, the hotbar and its
slots, the target frame, the messages, the death and help overlays, the crosshair and the lock marker
as rectangles; `interact_prompt`, `status_row`, `checkpoint_toast`, `xp_bar`, `tutorial_prompt`,
`ability_notice`, `boss_bar` with rectangle notches and pips, `quest_tracker`, `objective_marker` and
`threat_arrows` with rectangle shapes). The layout of §2.14: the target frame and the boss bar 30 px
lower (`TARGET_FRAME_TOP` 44, under the boss bar 94; the bar's top 44), the ability notice at
162..200. The lock line shows `PlayerTargeting.lock_label()`; a formation's banner mark (⚑) becomes a
drawn flag (`Hud.lock_line()`, `lock_line_now()`). `T` / D-pad Left (`track_next`) tracks the next
quest and says "Tracking: <quest>" (`track_next_now()`). The help overlay lists the map, the pin, the
journal and the tracked quest.

### The compass (`ui/hud/compass.gd`, `HudCompass`)

Top centre, (-270..270, 4..28), turned with the camera: ticks every 15 degrees, N/E/S/W, and on it
the tracked objective's gold diamonds (one per marker of a multi-marker stage, held at the strip's
ends when behind), up to three leads (hollow diamonds), the six nearest known POIs (found or revealed,
`compass: true`) within 600 m whatever the giant's size, "?" for undiscovered POIs within 250 m (a
`skyline` POI within 1,000 m), and the pin (on its level, at any distance). Hidden where the level has
no world index (the sandbox, the title) and with the HUD. The geometry is pure and tested:
`markers_for`, `heading_of`, `bearing_of`, `offset_deg`, `objective_positions`, `cardinal`.

### The map (`ui/screens/map_screen.gd`, `MapScreen`)

`ScreenStack.SCREENS["map"]`, opened by M / D-pad Down (the stack's `HOTKEYS`), pausing the game; the
first opening sets `map.opened` (the map tutorial's dismissal). Args: `{"level": id}` (default: the
level being played).

- **What it shows** (`entries_for(level, index, flags)`, pure): the index's picture over its
  `map_bounds` (the world's 1,024 px map) or, for a level without one, a vector map of the districts
  and roads (the Valley); fog over every unseen chunk; the regions' names with "Recommended stage
  N-M" (warning colour below); waystones and shrines that are visited or stand in a seen chunk (bright
  once visited); exits in seen chunks; POIs once known (found bright, revealed dim); sealed places once
  seen, with a lock and "Stage N" until the seal opens; a shrine's POI is shown by its shrine alone;
  the pin; the player and its facing; the tracked objective (every marker of a multi-marker stage).
- **The panel** (`detail_lines()`, pure): the place's kind, its region with the recommended stages
  ("(you are stage 3)" below them), and visited / not visited, "Too narrow above stage N", found / seen
  from afar / "Sealed. Grow to stage N to open it."; the result line shows a refusal. The controls of
  the device in use are listed above the buttons (the stack's footer owns the bottom line).
- **Travel** on a visited waystone or shrine: `Events.travel_requested(level, id, "fast_travel")`;
  GameSession's refusal shows on the panel (`travel_refusal_for`: an aware enemy within 40 m, dead,
  grabbed, holding, not visited, `requires_flags`, a `max_stage` below the stage; anything else "You
  can only travel to a waystone or a shrine."). The open map is not a blocker (P20's
  `travel_blockers("fast_travel")` ignores `screen`). Level tabs show the other campaign levels
  visited (`progress.levels_visited` with an index), so travel works between levels.
- **Pin here** (P / Y; a right click pins the clicked point): the place in focus, else the map's
  centre (a cross marks it); **Clear pin**. The flag `map.pin` = `[x, z, level]`.
- **Input**: mouse (click a place, drag to pan, the wheel zooms, right click pins), keyboard (arrows
  between places, W A S D pans, - / = zoom, Enter chooses, P pins, Esc or M closes), pad (D-pad and
  left stick between places through Godot's focus over the icons, the right stick pans, LB / RB zoom,
  A chooses - on a destination it moves the focus to Travel, elsewhere the panel says why not - Y
  pins, B closes). Three zoom levels (1x, 2x, 4x); the map follows the focus.

### The journal (`ui/screens/journal_screen.gd`, `JournalScreen`)

`ScreenStack.SCREENS["journal"]`, J / D-pad Right, pausing. One scrolling page from `sections()`: the
active quests (title, region or Valley district, "Step 2 of 5: <objective>", "Track" / "Tracked"),
the waiting ones with their `waiting_text`, the completed ones, the oaths settled
(`oath.<march|wood|crown>.outcome`), the sealed places seen and still shut with the stage they need
(green once the giant is big enough), and each region's M7 note. The Track buttons then Close form
the pad's focus rows (wrapping); the focus stays on a button after Track rebuilds the page; J, Esc or
B closes it.

## Decisions and deviations

- **The compass spans ±270 px**, not ±300: at 1280 px the plan's strip met the quest tracker
  (`test_hud_layout` keeps every region apart at five resolutions).
- **The region banner sits at 206..252**, not 150..196: the plan's place met the ability notice at
  162..200 (the plan's own "132+30").
- **`discovered.<poi>` = `"revealed"`** for a POI a vista showed: the map and the compass know it, but
  its XP and `poi_discovered` wait for the visit (decision 33), and the `discovered` condition needs
  `true`. A vista also clears the fog of the chunks within its `reveals_m`.
- **A POI is found at its `min_stage` or above; a sealed place only once its seal is open** (seen
  from 60 m whatever the stage).
- **A region's `discover_xp`** is paid by Discovery on the first entry.
- **The pin is `[x, z, level]`**: it shows on its own level's map and compass only (`pin_on`).
- **Two flags beyond §2.3**: `sealed.seen_count` (the sealed-place tutorial's trigger) and
  `map.opened` (the map tutorial's dismissal).
- **The world's six tutorials wait for `progress.world_entered`**, so the Valley prologue shows none.
- **The quest tracker leads with the tracked quest**, then the main one (`lines_for(..., tracked_id)`).
- **"Track" / "Tracked" and "Tracking: <quest>"** (the plan's "Track" from the journal; the binding's
  label "Track the next quest").
- **Files beyond the owned list**: `ui/hud/hud_kit.gd` (the batching parts), the existing widgets
  (`interact_prompt`, `status_row`, `checkpoint_toast`, `xp_bar`, `tutorial_prompt`, `ability_notice`,
  `boss_bar`, `quest_tracker`, `objective_marker`, `threat_arrows`; `hud_widgets.gd` comments only) -
  the wave's note gives P27 the whole HUD's draw calls - and `tests/support/world_ux_fixtures.gd`
  (the stub level: records and an index built in code, no level loads).
- **The map's controls hint lives in its side panel**: at the bottom it met the ScreenStack's footer.

## M7 polish (after the M7 gate)

- **An objective on another level leads to the door there** (the M7 gate review). `Quests.objective_position`
  looks only at the loaded level and its index, so a marker another level holds had no position
  (`Vector3.INF`): `quest.sealed_citadel` tracked in the world (its markers are the Valley's
  `valley.marker.citadel_gate` and `citadel_hall`), or `quest.world_main`'s beacon and oaths while the
  giant revisits the Valley, showed text alone in the tracker and nothing on the compass, the map or
  the HUD marker. `objective_for()` and `leads()` now use `Quests.objective_route(marker, targets)`:
  this level's position when it knows the marker or a target, else the exit of this level to the
  level whose index knows it (`exit_toward`): among the open exits, the shortest straight-line way -
  the giant to the door, plus the stone the door arrives at to the target. A door is open
  (`exit_open`) when its LevelExit node, while loaded, is open, the stone it arrives at has its
  `requires_flags`, and so does the stone beside it on this side (the checkpoint the other level's
  exits bring a giant back to, within 40 m): the Valley's Citadel stair, behind the sealed gate with
  no flag of its own, opens with the breach as its stone does. So the Sealed Citadel leads through the
  Kings' Door, then (breached) through the Broken Stair; the world's main thread in the Valley through
  the Kings' Door. Markers behind one door are one diamond and one lead. The objective carries
  `via_exit`, `via_name` and `via_text` ("by the Kings' Door"); the tracker's title line adds it
  (`QuestTracker.title_of`: "The Sealed Citadel  -  by the Kings' Door") and the journal names the door
  instead of the region around it. `objective_position` itself stays the level's own: the `near`
  condition reads it and must never hold beside a door.
- **A won oath's lead leaves the compass and the map** (the M7 gate review; `docs/systems/P29.md`,
  "M7 polish"): `objective_for()` lists the stage's open markers (`Quests.open_markers`: a marker
  whose `markers_done_when` condition holds is dropped), so the diamonds, the marker the giant is led
  to and the compass's leads follow the oaths still open.
- Tests: `test_tracked_quest` (`test_a_done_lead_leaves_the_objective_the_compass_and_the_leads`;
  `test_a_marker_on_another_level_leads_to_the_door_there` on the stub level's door;
  `test_the_sealed_citadel_leads_out_of_the_world_and_the_oaths_out_of_the_valley` on the shipped
  indexes, the journal's line included). Default tier: about 30 ms more.
