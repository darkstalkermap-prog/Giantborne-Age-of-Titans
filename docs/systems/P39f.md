### The HUD hides behind every open screen

FOLLOWUPS: "the HUD stays drawn round an open menu", "a tutorial tip stays behind the shrine window", "the last
message shows through the end screen".

- **Cause.** Nothing hid the HUD while a screen was open. The screens' panels are see-through and none covers
  the whole view, so the stack's footer hint ("Arrows / Tab: move  Enter: select  Esc: back") printed over the
  hotbar's cost labels and red notes, the quest tracker stood beside the shrine window and the journal, the TIP
  box showed behind the shrine window (its lines cut by the panel's edge: they were whole, half hidden), and the
  last message's tail stuck out under the end screen's buttons. The tutorial tip and the message are HUD
  widgets, so the three rows had one cause.
- **Fix.** `Hud.MenuWatch`, a child that runs while the game is paused, hides the HUD while
  `ScreenStack.any_open_in(tree)` (any stack has a screen open: pause, shrine, dialogue, yield prompt, map,
  journal, inventory, attributes, forge, ancestry, end) and shows it when the last one closes. The HUD itself is
  pausable, so nothing on it changes meanwhile and it comes back as it was. The whole HUD hides, not parts of
  it: every screen covers the middle of the view, nothing on the HUD can be used under one, and all but the
  title (which has no HUD) pause the game.
- **Test.** `test_hud_layout.test_the_hud_hides_behind_an_open_screen`: a busy HUD (boss bar, lock, tracker,
  TIP, messages) hides under the pause menu, the journal and a screen that does not pause
  (`tests/support/test_overlay_screen.gd`), and shows again when they close.
- **Evidence.** `pairs/02_hud_round_menus__*` (shrine tabs, journal, Corvane's yield, both end screens),
  `pairs/06_tip_behind_shrine__*`, `pairs/08_end_screen_message__*`.

- **Evidence.** `pairs/09_yield_prompt__*`.

### The loot panel and the tracker show their lines whole

FOLLOWUPS: "long HUD lines are cut with '...'".

- **Cause.** The loot line was one unbacked 244 px label of every item carried, trophies and keys included,
  trimmed with an ellipsis ("Loot: Iron Ingot x1, Ogre King's Tu..."). The tracker gave the tracked step two
  lines and the second quest's step one, each trimmed, in a fixed 118 px box.

  the giant carries (stackable items: iron, titan iron, wood scrap, the forge's stock), packed whole onto lines

  quest items are listed by the inventory screen, and every pickup is still announced by its message. The
  tracker wraps each step onto up to `TEXT_LINES` (3) lines and its panel is as tall as what it shows; its
  layout region is its tallest (`max_height()`).
- **Test.** `test_hud_layout.test_the_loot_panel_lists_the_materials_whole` (every stackable item in the data at
  once fits `LOOT_MAX_LINES` lines with every entry whole; the live panel lists the materials and no trophy, on a

- **Evidence.** `pairs/03_loot_and_tracker__*` (the before frames show "Hollowm..." and "Tu..."; after, the

### Ability names fit their hotbar slots; the red notes clear the edge

FOLLOWUPS: "ability names too long for their hotbar slot are cut with no '...'", "the red notes under the

- **Cause.** A 58 px slot less its box's margins left a 42 px title, too narrow for RAMPART (52 px), FOOTFALL

  `REASON_GAP` (6 px) under the slot, ending at least `REASON_EDGE_PX` (3 px) above the edge. Stonewall Stance's
  `hotbar_label` is now STANCE (`data/abilities/stonewall_stance.json`): at 66 px it fits no slot of a nine-slot
  row that leaves the bars and the loot panel their room at 1280 px. The cost labels fit too ("100 FUR", which
  read "100 FU").
- **Test.** `test_hud_layout.test_hotbar_names_and_notes_fit`: a slot's title spans 56 px and every ability's

- **Evidence.** `pairs/04_hotbar_names_notes__*` (RAMPA, FOOTF and the cut "Too big" before).

### The scenery fade's test runs the shader's stages

- **Cause.** `test_scenery_fade` checked the shader's fade rule against `SceneryFade`, but not the lines of
  `vertex()` and `fragment()` that feed it: the world position, the eye, the vertex colours' albedo.
- **Fix (tests only).** `ShaderRule.run_body(stage, env)` runs a stage's own statements with the built-ins the
  test hands it (swizzles and swizzle assignment, `discard`, the conditional operator, statements over several
  lines, the vector constructors, `pow`, `mix`, `lessThan`). `test_scenery_fade.test_the_shader_stages_feed_the_rule`
  runs 200 random model matrices through `vertex()` (the world position), 64 vertex colours (sRGB to linear as
  `Color.srgb_to_linear`, only with `vertex_color_albedo` and without `OUTPUT_IS_SRGB`) and 200 fragments through
  `fragment()` (a discard exactly where `SceneryFade.fade_at` with `INV_VIEW_MATRIX`'s eye and the 4x4 Bayer
  threshold says so, none in the shadow pass, the albedo, roughness and metallic). Mutating any one of four lines
  of the shader fails it (1, 118, 40 and 64 failures).
- **Evidence.** The test's own line: 117 fragments discarded, 83 kept, 40 shadow-pass; worst colour error
  2.4e-7, worst position error 0.

### Left, and why

- **Far people and giants as plain posts** (the distance LOD's one-box proxy, P22). Each actor scene's
  `LodProxy` box is built by its builder (soldier, wolf, npcs, big, formations, heavy, ranged, siege) and copied
  by the rig as its far proxy. A figure-shaped proxy means rebuilding every actor scene, then measuring the far
  LOD's draw calls in the slow and gate tiers again and looking at every family at 45 to 90 m. Not cheap, and
  the owner finds the graphics fine.
- **Faint seams in the distant haze** where the loaded hills meet the skyline backdrop. A fix is a matter of
  the skyline's and the fog's tuning at the streaming edge (`scripts/world/skyline.gd`, the render presets),
  to be judged by eye at many viewpoints and every quality preset, the web's included. Not cheap either, for a
  seam the pass calls faint.

## Part "world": world, AI and content fixes

The world, AI and content rows of `docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md` that P39 owns ("From P37", "From P36", "From the M8 gate

### Reeve Tamsin speaks of the raid on Millford

- **Cause.** `event.millford_raid` sets `world.millford.burned` (fell) or `world.millford.defended`, but
  `dialogue.reeve_tamsin` had no node that read them (plan §5.10: after `fell` "the reeve's dialogue changes").
- **Fix.** Two greetings in `data/dialogue/dialogue_reeve_tamsin.json`: `burned` (the houses lost, two families in the
  mill, "we'll build again") and `defended` (her thanks, every door open), entered after `hostile` and before the quests'
  `thanks`; a burned Millford comes first (the raid can run twice, and its houses stay burned). Both lead on to her
  troubles. The raid's description no longer says the words wait for the dialogue's owner.
- **Test.** `test_crownmarch_content.test_the_reeve_speaks_of_the_raid` (the raid's outcomes write the flags she reads;
  greet / burned / defended / burned over defended / the raid over the mill's thanks / hostile still first /
  reconciled). On `wave-22`: 9 failures.

### Raiders keep no target rather than a giant they do not see

- **Cause.** P30's target provider (`EnemyActor._p30_target`) fell back to the giant whenever none of its NPCs or props
  was in sight. A raider still aware after its house burned then perceived a stage-4 giant 100 m off - out of sight,
  but "existing", alive and far taller - and the outmatched rule (decision 26: join a formation or flee and rout at stage
  4) routed it. A raid at stages 4-5 thinned itself out and could end `defended` with the giant standing by.
- **Fix.** With nothing of its targets in sight and the giant not engaged (within `aggro_player_m` and seen or aware of
  it) the provider keeps no target: the raider patrols the raid's round of the houses or idles until it sees a villager or
  a house, and the giant comes first again once he is within `aggro_player_m` and in sight.
- **Test.** `test_target_provider.test_with_nothing_in_sight_a_raider_keeps_no_target_and_does_not_rout_from_a_far_giant`:
  the raider takes the house; the house gone, a stage-4 giant 60 m off is no target and in 2 s the raider neither flees
  nor routs; the giant 14 m in front of it is the target again. On `wave-22`: the target is the giant and the raider is
  ROUTED (3 failures).

### Raiders never fix on a target they cannot strike

- **Cause.** The provider had no give-up rule and took every living NPC of its factions in sight: a villager in the mill's
  shelter (inside: hidden and out of the world's physics, but alive) kept a raider at the mill for the rest of a raid, and
  Reeve Tamsin (`protected`: every blow refused) was as good a target as anyone.
- **Fix.** `EnemyActor._p30_out_of_reach`: a civilian in shelter and a protected NPC are never targets. `_p30_futile`: an
  NPC or prop the provider has been fixed on for `P30_GIVE_UP_S` (12 s) without a blow of this unit damaging it
  (`_p30_on_hit_landed` restarts the count) is given up for `P30_GIVEN_UP_S` (60 s) - a door behind a wall, a villager on a
  ledge.
- **Test.** `test_target_provider.test_a_raider_skips_sheltered_and_protected_people_and_gives_up_one_it_cannot_damage`
  (the sheltered villager and a protected one are no targets; a damaging blow restarts the count; 12 s without damage
  gives the target up; after 60 s it may be taken again).

### Templates are not counted where their records draw them up

- **Cause.** `FactionState.armed_members_near` (a hostile faction's respawn guard) and the formation clusters of
  `FormationRules.cross_record_errors` read every encounter record, so a template's spawns counted at its nominal centre:
  P37 kept the Crownmarch's templates away from checkpoints and clusters by hand, and a region package adding templates had
  to do the same.
- **Fix.** Both skip `template: true`. A template never stands where its record draws it up (its copies stand where the
  WanderDirector places them, never by a checkpoint, and are admitted only while the live caps hold all of them): it is no
  member for the respawn guard, joins no placed encounter's cluster, and is a cluster of its own, so a template must still
  fit the caps by itself. No shipped record changed its verdict (every shipped cluster still fits; Brannock's guard still
  counts its six).
- **Tests.** `test_factions_world.test_a_template_drawn_up_by_a_checkpoint_is_no_guard` and
  `test_formation.test_cross_record_errors_check_banner_roles_and_the_caps_of_every_cluster` (a template 100 m away joins no
  cluster; a template of 45 alone is still over the desktop's 40). On `wave-22`: 2 and 2 failures.

### Godot's exit-time leak report: process-lifetime state after a whole campaign; recorded, not fixed

- **Reproduced** on this branch: a whole campaign from a new game (`--mode=world --new-game --scenario=world
  --bot-preset=peaceful --bot-skill=novice --bot-seed=2 --bot-assist`, run with `--verbose`; completed in 1,489 s
  simulated, no death, no error, no invariant failure) printed P38's counts exactly: 523 resources still in use, 96 Jolt
  shapes, 163 meshes, 46 materials and 4 shaders, and 659 leaked instances.
- **What is held.** Every resource still in use is a GDScript (191 of the 249 scripts under `scripts/` and `ui/`) or what
  the process-lifetime caches hold: the world's warm preload list (`WorldRoot._preloaded`: all 44 of its entries, scripts
  and shared prop scenes), the actor scenes of `EncounterDirector._scene_cache` (17 enemy and 2 NPC scenes) with their
  sub-resources (161 array meshes; 57 box, 19 sphere, 19 capsule and 1 cylinder shapes - the 96 Jolt shapes; 47
  materials), and the world's two indexes (`index_world.res`, `index_valley.res`). The leaked instances are those, the
  scripts' native-class wrappers, the contents of script statics (the formations' registry's WeakRefs, the autopilot's
  random number generator) and five RefCounted script instances. No node, no chunk, navmesh or other streamed resource,
  no autoload script and none of the HUD's: nothing that play makes and frees.
- **When.** Only after a campaign completed in the process: this run, P38's six, and the stability lane's completed
  runs and loop runs (a loop's second campaign kept the same 523 after 1,254 s more of play). Not after the same
  completion from a save (`--continue` before the champion: 116 s; before the throne: 45 s; both completed, both
  clean), nor after long runs that ended otherwise (the stability lane's breaches after 808-1,778 s, its 330 s Valley
  loop).
- **Verdict.** The same fixed set in every run - the size of those caches - so nothing grows with play: not a leak
  during play, and the memory goes back when the process ends. What keeps those scripts and their statics from being
  released at shutdown after a completed new-game campaign, and not after a completion from a save, was not pinned
  down in this part (the leaked instances carry no script paths). Not fixed: clearing the caches before the quit would
  only hide the report. It follows the run's result and counts no error (exit 0). Evidence: `world/leak/` (the three
  verbose logs and the full run's exit report).

### `tools/web/world_check.mjs`: a Continue, a fast travel and a walk across borders

`stream_check.mjs --world` walks the world from a New Game's start. Nothing measured the world as a returning player
meets it: a world save Continued from the title, a level reloaded by fast travel, and the music across region borders
while the ground streams in. The new check does that on the release build (which takes its arguments) and reports what
`stream_check.mjs` reports for its walk (its header has the details):

- **A world save in the browser.** Before Play the check writes the fixture into the page's IndexedDB where Godot's
  web file system keeps `user://` (Emscripten's IDBFS: database `/userfs`, store `FILE_DATA`, the slot
  `/userfs/godot/app_userdata/Giantborn- Age of Titans/saves/valley_1.json` with its folders, and the modes and dates
  the engine writes - as a probe of a release build's own database after a save showed:
  `probes/idb_after_world_start.json`), then reads it back.
- **Continue.** Play, the title's music, five seconds, then Enter (the title's first focus is Continue when a save
  exists), again after 45 s if the title has not left (at most three presses; the ignored ones are counted). The game
  runs with `--save-dir=user://saves` beside `--no-user-settings`, because `--no-user-settings` alone turns saving
  off, and the title's Continue with it. The level, the respawn and the stage the walk reports are checked against the
  fixture's.
- **Fast travel.** The world walk's new `--walk-travel=<checkpoint>` (`scripts/debug/world_walk.gd`) waits 30 ticks on
  the level, asks `GameSession.travel_refusal` whether the travel may go and asks for it the map's way
  (`Events.travel_requested`, "fast_travel"). A travel held back by a blocker (an aware enemy close by) is asked again
  each tick for at most 60 s, its refusals counted; a refusal no wait can end (a place not visited, a run outside the
  campaign) ends the walk at once with its reason. GameSession reloads the level at the waystone, and the walk made
  there sets off where the travel put the giant (the arrival is the route's first point; the travel's record - from,
  to, refusals, ticks - lives in a static across the reload), failing when he arrived more than 30 m from the place.
- **The walk.** At least 60 s of game time along `tools/web/world_check.json`'s route from the King's Road waystone,
  where three regions meet, south into the Drowned Steps, east into the Aldmere March and back west: 787.5 m, three
  region changes and five chunk seams on the real world (`tests/slow/test_world_check_route.gd`), the AI on.
- **The report** adds to `stream_check.mjs`'s: the Continue's and the travel's load times (from the game's new
  `WORLD_WALK_UNLOAD` line - the autopilot printing the scene its walk leaves - to the walk's level line on the level
  loaded), the streaming hitches (the frames whose streaming work passed the streamer's 4 ms budget:
  `ChunkStreamer.stats()`'s new `work_ms_over_budget`) with the heaviest steps by kind, and the engine's warnings listed apart
  from its errors. It ends `world_check: OK`, `OK (the plan's audio criterion NOT MET: ...)` or `FAILED - ...`.

The fixture, `tests/fixtures/saves/v2_world_check_aldmere_shrine.json`, is a save the world bot wrote: the mixed preset,
novice, assisted, seed 1, on this branch's code (`world_check.json`'s note has the command), the checkpoint it took when
the giant grew to stage 5 at the Aldmere shrine 296.7 s into its play - level 10, five stones reached, the King's Road

### The heaviest streaming step (the optional item): the text scene's parse, not the instancing

P38 measured the worst streaming work frame at 14.5-17.6 ms headless (the target 12 ms), its heaviest step `props_load`
at 11.8-16.7 ms. `props_load` is one `ResourceLoader.load` of a chunk's props part, a text `.tscn` (each part's
dependencies already load one a step, `props_dep`); `props_add` (instantiate and `add_child`) is a step of its own. A
probe timed each of the world's 42 parts three times, its dependencies loaded first and the part's cache ignored
(`probes/props_parts_probe.log`; the probe script `probes/props_parts_probe.gd`):

| | Largest part (`c1_2_0`, 77,693 bytes) | Every part (median of three) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The text scene's parse (`props_load`) | 12.70 ms | the 3 largest 11.8-12.7 ms; 2 over 12 ms; mean 3.2 ms |
| Instancing (`props_add`) | 1.09 ms | at most 2.0 ms; mean 0.8 ms |
| The same part saved binary (what an export ships: `.tscn.remap` to a `.scn`) | 0.88 ms | at most 1.2 ms; mean 0.4 ms |

Spreading a part's instancing over more streamer steps cannot bring the worst frame under 12 ms: the instancing is at
most 2 ms, and the heavy step is the parse of a large text scene - the base64 arrays of its merged meshes, its collision
shapes and its node lines - which one `ResourceLoader.load` call does whole. The parse is a headless and editor cost:
exports convert the scenes to binary (`c1_2_0.tscn.remap` in the pck), whose parse takes a tenth of the time or less.
The ways under 12 ms headless are the world builder's (parts capped in bytes, which changes the merged meshes and the
draw calls, or binary parts in the repository) or threaded loading (which deterministic streaming excludes); none is a
contained change for this part, so nothing changed (FOLLOWUPS, "From W23 (lane P39f, the web part)").
