# P21 — Sound effects

Wave 13, lane A (beside P22). Branch `pkg/P21`. Plan: `docs/plans/M6_M9_PLAN.md` §3.3, with §4.1
(every cue), §4.2 (synthesis by family), §4.4 (classes, priorities, caps), §4.5 (formats and
budget) and decisions 4, 7 and 8. The goal: original, generated sound effects for every combat,
ability, enemy, world-object, interface and progression event that exists today, with directional
threat cues, inside the budget; P19's engine plays them and only data and generated files were
added, plus four routing rules.

## What was built

- **A synthesis library** on top of P19's `tools/audio/synth/core.py` (numpy only, float64,
  single-threaded, every random number from the cue's own seeded generator):
  - `synth/fx.py` — vectorised filters (zero-phase and minimum-phase FFT shaping, low/high/band
    passes, resonances), a time-varying filter (`stft_shape`, `swept_band`, `moving_lowpass`),
    envelopes (`env_ad`, `env_swell`, `env_points`), noise colours and slow wander, modal synthesis
    (`modal_ir`, `excite`, `partials`), grains (`scatter`, `poisson_times`), `ring_mod`,
    `tremolo`, `soft_clip`, a one-pole DC blocker done as an exact convolution, early
    reflections and the Valley's slope echo, `distant()` (the far versions:
    low-passed, softened transient, longer tail), `match_rms` (balancing a layer against another)
    and `finish()` (DC out, the silent tail trimmed at -50 dB, at most 1.98 s, a fade so it starts
    and ends at zero; a ring still above -40 dB where it is cut is tapered instead of chopped).
  - `synth/instruments.py` — modal impacts by material (wood, stone, iron, flesh, armour), bursts,
    whooshes, granular crunch and debris, crackle, creaks (stick-slip pulses through a swept
    resonance), bells (additive inharmonic partials), FM tones, plucked strings, blips and buzzes,
    the footfall layers by size class and surface (`STEP_SIZES`), and `variant_cues()` (a cue's
    1-4 seeded variants as build entries); iron renders at `METAL_RATE` 32 kHz.
  - `synth/voices.py` — a Rosenberg glottal pulse train with jitter, shimmer and aspiration through
    moving cascade formants (`glottal`, `articulate`, `call`, `breath`), growls, snarls, jaw snaps,
    barks and yelps (`snarl`, `snap`, `bark`, `yelp`) and crowds of six or seven mixed voices
    (`crowd`); `voice_params()` holds each voice's pitch and tract length.
- **Seven generators**, `tools/audio/gen_{combat,abilities,steps,voices,threat,world,ui}.py`
  (seeds 210001-216099, one block of 100-200 per family), building **209 WAV masters, 98.8 s**,
  every one mono 16-bit at 22.05 kHz (32 kHz for the 9 metal masters), normalised to -6 dBFS peak,
  0.04-1.5 s long, 1-3 variants a cue. Imported as QOA they come to **979,066 bytes: 0.934 MB** in
  the pipeline's MB of 1,048,576 bytes (cap 0.95 MB; the plan estimated 188 files, 94.7 s,
  0.93 MB). Their `.wav.import` files, manifest entries (`assets/audio/manifest.json`, 218 files
  with P19's placeholders) and provenance rows (`assets/audio/PROVENANCE.md`) are committed;
  `build_audio.py --verify` regenerates all of them bit-exact.
- **Seven effect banks**, `data/audio/bank_{combat,abilities,steps,voices,threat,world,ui}.json`:
  215 cues (33, 54, 26, 46, 7, 26, 23) over the 209 masters, every family default an alias of a
  real sound; P19's placeholder bank keeps only `mus.stinger.default` (P23's).
- **The mix**: the Valley's surfaces name `"water": "wet"` (the router's wet-ground rule below).
- **Four routing rules** in `scripts/audio/cue_router.gd` (the single W13 owner of the file):
  hurt calls for every voiced body, voiceless bodies sounding by material, roar actions voicing a
  roar, and wet ground by the level's water (see *Decisions*).
- **Tests**: `tests/unit/test_sfx_banks.gd`, `tests/integration/test_sfx_coverage.gd`,
  `tests/integration/test_threat_cues.gd`, `tests/gate/test_sfx_valley_pass.gd` (see *Runtime*).

## The families, for the owner's listening pass

Every master is in `assets/audio/sfx/<family>/<name>_<variant>.wav` and plays in any audio player;
in the game, start the sandbox or the Valley without `--no-audio`. Levels below are measured on the
masters (peak -6.0 dBFS for all): *loud* is the A-weighted RMS of the loudest 300 ms in dBFS (what
the banks balance on), *centroid* the spectral centroid, *onset* the time of the loudest 5 ms.
None clips (the build fails on clipping, DC, silence and anything over 2.0 s).

- **Combat** (`gen_combat.py`). Hits: a blow on flesh is a thump, a muffled body, a thwack and a
  slap, heavy ones with a crunch (centroid 99-163 Hz); armour adds a short plate ring (32 kHz,
  partials 1.5-6 kHz); wood a knock; stone grit, stone modes, a thud and crumbs (about 1 kHz).
  Heavy variants are about 1.4 x longer and 0.8 x lower. The critical accent is a bright crack, a
  biting upward chirp and a sub drop laid over the hit; the immune blow a dead, damped "tunk".
  Blocks: the giant's (a heavy wooden thud on flesh and a scrape) and an enemy guard's (a shield
  thud and a short clank); the parry a clean bright ring with an upward glide (2.2-2.5 kHz); the
  guard break a metal crash, a wooden crack and a low boom. Bodies fall small, large and huge (two
  thuds, the gear's rattle, dirt; big ones add debris and rumble, the King an echo off the
  slopes). Swings are whooshes swept to their apex (onset 145-359 ms): blunt, blade (ringing
  faintly, 1.8-2.2 kHz) and the uprooted tree (dragging its leaves); the giant's roll, sidestep,
  guard and jump are body thuds, cloth, leather and kicked dirt. *Listen for*: hits that read as a
  giant's blows (weight in 80-250 Hz, no click on top), the parry standing out, swings never
  masking hits.
- **Abilities** (`gen_abilities.py`). The stomp (Heavy Footfall, the ogres' smashes, the King's
  slam) is a sub drop, a crack, the earth's granular rumble, debris raining back and a slap off the
  Valley's slopes, with a far version (its boom and rumble only, 48 Hz centroid) beyond half its
  range; Boulder Hurl tears a boulder from the ground (rock cracking free, grinding, earth falling
  off it) and flies with a whoosh; Runic Bolt and the mage's casts are arcane FM with a ring
  modulator and shimmer, the mage's barrier a glassy FM swell; Ancestral Mend a soft rising breath
  and chime, then a warm inharmonic bell chord; Stone Skin grinding stone building up, the shell
  crackling into place and crumbling away; Thunder Roar a deep breath while the air rumbles, then
  the giant's formant roar over thunder; Titan Surge a short roar, a great drum hit and a rising
  swell; the archer's bow a plucked string with its slap on the bracer. Projectiles launch and land
  by visual (arrow, bolt, boulder, a thrown body); statuses turn on and off; grabs seize, crush,
  throw and drop. *Listen for*: the stomp's weight on small speakers (a 150 Hz punch and a
  180-900 Hz body sit above its 64 Hz boom), the casts being recognisably different from one
  another, the roar not clipping in a fight.
- **Steps** (`gen_steps.py`). A footfall is a sub thump (40-90 Hz with a fast pitch drop; lower
  and longer with every stage), an upper-bass punch that carries the weight on small speakers, a
  body of earth and the surface: soft (a granular crunch of soil and grass), hard (clicks and grit)
  and wet (a squelch with bubbles; centroid 156-188 Hz, against 50-91 Hz for the dry steps of
  stages 1 and 2). Stage 3 adds a tail of
  pebbles and earth that its `.cruise` variants leave out; people, ogres (heavy, lowered further by
  size pitch, with a far version) and wolves' paws are short and light. *Listen for*: a steady walk
  never tiring (three soft variants at stages 1 and 2, two and two cruise ones at stage 3, the
  cruise rule's -5 dB after 10 s), the wet steps reading as the river bank, stage 3 sounding bigger
  than stage 1.
- **Voices** (`gen_voices.py`): calls, never words, from a glottal source through moving formants.
  Humans (f0 120-260 Hz): alert, attack, hurt, stagger, death, awe, flee, greet; the elder greets
  with her own older voice and borrows the human calls a little higher. Wolves: snarls, barks,
  yelps, a whine, jaws snapping (centroid 1.2-2.2 kHz). Ogres (f0 60-90 Hz, a 0.72 tract) and the
  Ogre King (f0 45-60 Hz, a 0.6 tract, a subharmonic and a growl): bellows, effort grunts, pain,
  long deaths, the King's roar with a far version. The giant (f0 55-70 Hz): pain, heavy pain, a
  dazed groan, its death and the growling intimidation. Crowds: a gasp, a murmur, a cheer.
  *Listen for*: nothing that sounds like speech or a recognisable word; the King clearly bigger
  than an ogre; human deaths not comic.
- **Threat** (`gen_threat.py`): built for localisation and kept apart from everything else:
  broadband, a short tick at the start, a band-pass sweep that rises 2 -> 8 kHz so the cue swells
  towards its end (impacts peak at once and decay), 0.26-0.45 s. Windups by telegraph shape: line
  (a rushing sweep with a rising glide), circle (the sweep over a low swell: a slam around the
  attacker, the one threat cue with low end), cone (a swish across the arc). The sting: a
  broadband hit, a fast rising sweep and a bright glissando (6.1 kHz centroid), played at the
  attacker when it is off-camera or far. The aim lock: a two-tone mechanical tick, the second
  ringing a little (the commit of a ranged attack). *Listen for*: each of them being audible over a
  fight and placeable left/right/behind on headphones; the sting not becoming tiresome.
- **World** (`gen_world.py`): wood splinters (clouds of small modal grains) and timber crashes,
  stone cracks and collapses with tumbling rubble (far version beyond half the range), gates
  opening (stick-slip creaks through a swept resonance; stone grinding), shrine chimes (additive
  inharmonic bells over a low hum) for waking and resting, the waystone, forge strikes and a
  quench (the upgrade), the iron gate of the Ogre King's arena, Thornwick's alarm bell, pickups,
  racks, talking and the oak uprooted, and the giant's growth (creaking like old timber under a
  rising rumble, 1.5 s). *Listen for*: breaks matching what broke, the shrine chimes calm.
- **UI** (`gen_ui.py`): short sine and triangle blips with fast envelopes and two- or three-note
  intervals from the score's key (D dorian: fifths and fourths, no thirds, so they sit under any
  music), a low square buzz for a refusal, a paper rustle for the map, a few tactile ones (a clink
  for metal, a wooden thunk, a blade's slide, leaves) for items and weapons. Quiet and short on
  purpose. *Listen for*: focus moves (40 ms) never irritating when held.

How the cues are balanced: each cue's `volume_db` was set once from its masters' measured
A-weighted loudness: target loudness for its role - measured loudness - 8 dB, clamped to -30..+3 dB
and rounded to 0.5 dB. Targets by role: threat -15 to -13, voices -19 to -13, abilities -20 to -13,
hits -20 to -15, swings -18 to -16, breaks -18 to -14, world -22 to -14, statuses -22 to -19,
interface -25 to -18, footfalls -26 to -20 (people quietest). The banks are plain data: change a
`volume_db` by ear and nothing else needs regenerating.

## Public APIs

### CueRouter (`scripts/audio/cue_router.gd`, P21's additions)

```
const HURT_GAP_S := 0.6                       # a body's hurt call at most this often
const WET_MARGIN_M := 6.5                     # footfalls this close to the level's water are wet
func footfall_surface(pos: Vector3) -> String # Audio.surface_at, or the mix's "water" surface near water
func water_rects() -> Array                   # Rect2 (x, z) of the level's "Water_*" planes, read once a level
static func plane_rects(mmi: MultiMeshInstance3D) -> Array   # the rectangles a plane MultiMesh covers
```

Both footfall handlers (the giant's and every other body's) take their surface from
`footfall_surface()`; `wade` (water above the foot, `water_at`) still wins for the giant and heavy
bodies. `reset()` forgets the water.

### Generators and the library (Python)

Every `gen_<family>.py` exposes `FAMILY` and `CUES` (P19's build contract); a cue's variants come
from `instruments.variant_cues(family, name, func, seed, count, params, rate=...)`, which gives
variant *i* (0-based, also passed to the render function as `variant`) the seed `seed + i` and the
path `res://assets/audio/sfx/<family>/<name>_<i+1>.wav`.
Regenerate one family with `python3 tools/audio/build_audio.py --only=<family>` (the manifest keeps
the others).

## Decisions as built

1. **The giant's own body is non-positional** (`spatial: false`): its casts, buffs, grabs, block,
   dodges, jump, voice, hurt, death, growth and the forge's upgrade. They start at its chest or
   hands, a small fraction of a class's unit size from the listener (which sits at the chest),
   where Godot's level, `-20 ln(d / unit) + volume_db`, is far above `max_db` (3 dB) and is clamped
   to it: as 3D voices they would all play at +3 dB whatever their `volume_db`, so the banks could
   not balance them. Its footfalls stay positional (class `player`, scaled by its own height) so
   they move with the feet, and its swings share the enemies' swing cues (class `human`) at the
   weapon's pivot.
2. **Masters are normalised to -6 dBFS peak and balanced in the banks** by measured A-weighted
   loudness (above), not by ear; the owner's listening pass adjusts `volume_db` only.
3. **The threat family stands apart by construction** (rising 2-8 kHz sweeps, swelling to a late
   peak) and by test: `test_sfx_banks` reads every threat and hit master and requires each threat
   cue's RMS frequency above 1.2 x the brightest hit's, its peak at least 60 ms in (hits within
   40 ms) and the family's mean RMS frequency at least 3 x the hits'. (RMS frequency - from the
   ratio of the derivative's energy to the signal's - stands in for the plan's spectral centroid:
   the test reads WAVs in GDScript without an FFT; the measured centroids above agree.)
4. **Routing rules** (the router is P21's hotspot; recorded here as the plan requires):
   - *Hurt calls for every voiced body*: a hit that does health damage and neither kills nor
     staggers its target calls the target's `hurt` (at most once per body per 0.6 s); P19 voiced
     only the giant's hurt. §4.1's voices have a hurt call each.
   - *Voiceless bodies sound by material*: a hit on a body whose voice is `none` (the sparring
     post) plays `sfx.hit.<material>` like a prop instead of a flesh hit.
   - *Roar actions voice a roar*: an enemy action whose id ends in `roar` (the Ogre King's phase
     roar) requests the voice's `roar` call (then its `attack`) at priority 75, not an attack grunt.
   - *Wet ground by the level's water*: a footfall within 6.5 m of the level's water takes the
     surface the mix names for `"water"` in that level. The Valley has no water area record and no
     `water_at` (that is P24a's world); its river exists only as the builder's `Water_Plane`
     MultiMesh instances (four rectangles, x 100-112). The router reads them from the MultiMesh's
     buffer, once a level, so the river banks and the King's Bridge (between two water rectangles
     6.2 m off its centre line) sound wet.

## Deviations

- **The P19 tests that encoded the placeholder bank follow it** (deliberate behaviour change):
  `tests/unit/test_cue_router.gd`'s fallback test now expects the P21 cues (a specific cue resolves
  to itself, an unknown material to `sfx.hit.default`, a missing cruise variant to its base step,
  `mus.stinger.growth` to the placeholder stinger, an unknown ability's roar to
  `sfx.ability.default`) and its variants test uses `sfx.hit.flesh.light` (three variants);
  `tests/integration/test_audio_director.gd` frees the voices before waiting for a footfall after a
  jump or a stomp (the giant's steps are capped at two voices, and the Dummy driver keeps the
  earlier steps playing in real time while the run is faster) and lets the loading gate register
  about a fifth of the level's files a frame (it registered two a frame: 209 files would have taken
  longer than its 60-frame limit).
- **Cue counts**: 215 cues over 209 files against the plan's 188 files; the extra ones are
  variants of the most repeated cues (three soft steps a stage, three light flesh hits, three human
  hurts and attacks) and far versions. 1-3 variants a cue (the plan allows 1-4).
- **Cruise variants exist only for stage 3**: stages 1 and 2 have no debris tail to leave out, so
  their `.cruise` request falls back to the base step (still 5 dB down by the router's rule).
- **Caps and guards per cue, not per family**: §4.4's "a voice 3 per archetype and 6 in all", "UI
  4" and "retrigger guard 40-60 ms" are applied cue by cue (the engine has no per-archetype or
  per-family cap): a call is capped at 3 (1-2 for the long ones), an interface cue at 1-2 (they are
  40-600 ms and repeat), and long one-shots get guards of 80 ms to 1.5 s so an event repeated in
  the same instant does not stack the same second-long sound.
- **The `player` class carries only the giant's footfalls**: §4.4 also lists its swings and its
  voice there; its voice is non-positional (decision 1) and its swings are the shared
  `sfx.swing.<family>.<weight>` cues of class `human` (one set of swings for every body).
- **The threat family's circle windup has low end** (a swell under its 2-6.5 kHz sweep: a slam
  around the attacker), so its centroid is 1.3-1.9 kHz; it still passes the separation test.
- **An arrow's launch is a quiet whoosh** and falls under the human class's audible level beyond
  about 19 m (the plan's class: unit 4 m, max 30 m); the bow's twang (`sfx.ability.archer_shot.cast`,
  louder) is what carries, and the aim lock before it is a threat cue on the `threat` class.
- **Size**: 2,838 lines of Python (library 1,321, generators 1,517), 3,746 of bank JSON (one
  object per cue with every field written out) and 1,106 of tests, plus 91 in the router, over the
  plan's estimate of about 2,700 in all.
- **The gate test reuses P15's walkthrough** (`tests/slow/test_valley_walkthrough.gd`'s helpers on
  an instance of that suite, its checks folded into the gate suite's) rather than copying its
  route; a change of those helpers' names breaks it.

## Known gaps and notes for the next packages

- **No listening pass yet**: every sound was checked objectively (levels, peaks, spectra, onsets,
  durations, clipping, DC, seams) and on spectrograms, not by ear on real speakers. The balance in
  the banks is computed; expect `volume_db` adjustments of a few dB.
- **M7-M9 cues are P28's**: `sfx.threat.windup.wall`, `sfx.threat.incoming`,
  `sfx.world.splash.<small|large>`, the giant's steps for stages 4-6, `wade` and hooves,
  `sfx.gate.<water|stone|timber>`, `sfx.formation.*`, `sfx.siege.*`, `sfx.trample`,
  `sfx.ui.travel`, `sfx.voice.crowd.<abandon|flee>`, `sfx.world.horn_alarm` and the troll, dire wolf,
  matriarch, horse and rival giant voices. Their router rows are connected only when their
  signals exist.
- **Wolves' footfalls** wait for P22's `QuadrupedRig.footfall` (the router connects it when the
  signal exists; `sfx.step.paw.*` is ready). Until then wolves run silently.
- **Cosmetic props** (fences of the dressing, barrels that only wobble) send no event and stay
  silent; the interaction cues sound only through `Events.interacted` (the walkthrough's direct
  `interact()` calls send none).
- **The web path** (samples registered on the web, 16 voices, 8 starts a frame) is P23's browser
  check; the 98.8 s the Valley registers is within the 110 s cap (about 35 MB of browser memory).
- **Default-suite time**: P19's `test_audio_assets` and `test_audio_director` grew with the library
  (to about 0.5 and 0.35 s: the asset test's peak scan reads all 4.35 M frames in GDScript).
- **A load-dependent gate flake** (not a P21 change of behaviour, recorded for the merge): in the
  first of two full checks P19's determinism gate failed because its audio soak counted one
  warning, "Jolt Physics job system exceeded the maximum number of jobs ... Waiting for jobs to
  become available", so the two AUTOPILOT_RESULT lines differed in `"warnings"` only (every other
  field matched). The 4-core machine was running three of P22's Valley bot processes at the time
  (load average 6-8). Reproduced in 1 of 9 audio soaks started from a parent Godot process and in
  none of 6 silent ones or 5 standalone audio soaks; the second full check passed. Jolt's job pool
  runs dry when its worker threads are not scheduled; the audio run has one more busy thread (the
  mixer) and, with P21's longer sounds, more voices to mix than with the placeholder tones.

## Running
