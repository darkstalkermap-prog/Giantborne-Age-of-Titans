# P59 — The people pipeline: the human generator, roles, atlases, LODs, crowd poses and clips

Plan: `docs/plans/M11_PLAN.md` §3.12 (and §1.1, §1.5-§1.7, §2.8, §2.11, §2.12, §6.3, the probes' §7 entry 10, the
owner's answer to question 6). Wave W34, lane B (the wave's asset writer), beside P58 (the 0.12.0-m12 release). Built
on 21 November 2026 on `pkg/P59` from `a271b75` (main after 0.12.0-m12). Every number was taken on the shared
container (4 CPUs, no GPU: llvmpipe natively, SwiftShader in Chromium).

The goal: §2.8 for humans - every role's file generated, verified and measured, with nothing in the game using them
yet (P60 is the first). **The split point was taken: the `notable` and `rider` roles move to P61** (the brief's own
split; see "What was cut or moved"). Six roles and seventeen variants are built; the people's Web share was measured
at **6.11 MiB of the 12.0 MiB share** in a Web export of the would-be 0.13 build, so no variant had to be cut.

## What was built

### 1. The 23-bone skeleton and its weights (`kit/people_roles.py`)

`BONES`: the game_engine rig's 53 bones less the 30 finger bones (`Root`, `pelvis`, `spine_01`-`03`, `neck_01`,
`head`, and per side `clavicle`, `upperarm`, `lowerarm`, `hand`, `thigh`, `calf`, `foot`, `ball`) - exactly the
giant's skeleton's names, closed under parents. `fold_bone` maps every game_engine bone to a kept one (a finger's to
its hand), `fold_weights` sums the folded weights (each vertex's total kept), `top4` keeps each vertex's four
largest, normalized. The hands are posed half closed first (`clips.hand_spec` at curl 0.5, the thumb 0.4), and that
pose is the people's rest: the fingers fold into the hands as they are posed.

### 2. A person (`kit/people_body.py`)

`build_person(macro, modifiers, name, ref)` makes a human from the giant's own CC0 data and code
(`giant_body.BodyData`, `build_giant`): the hm08 mesh morphed by MakeHuman's macro targets (gender, age, muscle,
weight; MakeHuman's ideal-proportions target at 0.8; a mix of the three race targets) and a face's few detail targets
(`people_roles.FACES`: broad, narrow, round, lean, soft, aged), **with MakeHuman's natural proportions** - the giant's
reshape (`PROPORTIONS`) is his own, made to undo the strongman's build, and is not applied. Everything is built at the
giant's stage-1 height (3.6576 m) so that his garment shells and his clip generator apply in the units they were
written in, and exported at 1.8 m (`EXPORT_SCALE`).

So that one set of clips poses every variant: each variant's pelvis stands exactly where the reference man's does
(the variant is scaled by its pelvis's height, not its crown's: a person's height varies by a few centimetres with the
build and sex, recorded per variant as `height_m`, with a suggested `natural_height_m` for P60's height scale: 1.0
for men, 0.93 for women), its 23 bones take the reference man's rest orientations at their own joints, and its `Root`
is the reference's. A clip's absolute rotations and the pelvis's track then place every variant's joints as the
reference's would on its own skeleton (the crowd-pose test reads it back).

**Women** (question 6, answered yes): the female macro targets of the same CC0 release, vendored and audited apart
from the giant's (item 8): two of the five villagers are women.

### 3. The outfit, the culling, the merge, the decimation and the atlas (`kit/people_outfit.py`)

Per role (`people_roles.ROLES`): the role's outfit, giant garment ids built slot by slot by the giant's own
`giant_garment.Wardrobe` on the first variant's body (each slot over the envelope of the ones under it); then:

- **cull**: every skin triangle (and every inner garment's) whose three corners and centre all lie under another
  piece - a ray along the triangle's normal meets it within 12 cm at 1.8 m (2 % further at the cull than in the test,
  for the float32 export) - is removed: about 8,400-11,200 body triangles and 570-5,000 inner cloth triangles a role;
- **merge** body and outfit into one mesh of about 27,500-28,400 triangles and **decimate** it (Blender's collapse,
  symmetric, the face and the hands weighted to keep more: `DETAIL_WEIGHT`) to the role's shared budget -
  `TRIS_MAX` 5,000 less the largest of its variants' own pieces and a margin: 3,600 (villager) to 4,317 (crew);
- **one UV atlas a role**: the shared mesh is unwrapped (smart projection, islands packed with rotation) into the
  512 px albedo's 384 px square (`MAIN_UV`); the variants' own pieces map into 128 px tiles beside it (`TILES`: hair,
  metal, faction cloth, cloth, leather, the eyes, fur), mirrored about the head or body axis so no seam shows;
- **the variants** share the shared mesh: every outfit vertex rides on the skin vertex it was built over, and each
  low vertex sits at its barycentric place on the high mesh, moved with the variant's own body - so the role's one
  atlas serves every variant. Each variant then adds its own pieces, built on its own body and its own wardrobe (the
  outfit rebuilt there for the envelope): hair (a crop shell over the scalp, or long hair down the back over the
  clothes), a beard (short or full shells over the beard mask), a helm, cap or hood (the giant's `g_iron_helm`,
  `g_leather_cap`, `g_hood`), the eyes, and the iron guard's tabard and pauldrons and the women's long skirt - and
  loses every skin triangle they or the outfit cover (the same rule);
- **one mesh, one surface** of at most 5,000 triangles on its 23-bone skeleton with four weights, the tangents
  exported (the normal map's frame), in `<role>.glb` with the role's **five crowd-pose meshes** (`hold`, `brace`,
  `advance`, `flee`, `corpse`: the first variant skinned in its clip's key, then decimated to at most 600 triangles,
  the atlas's UVs kept) and its **far mesh** (the hold pose at at most 150).

### 4. The atlas and the palette mask (`kit/people_bake.py`)

Per role, from the first variant: albedo 512 px, normal 256 px and one 256 px map with **R the ambient occlusion,
G the roughness and B the palette band** (`people_roles.BANDS`: none, skin, hair, cloth, faction; the byte
`(index + 0.5) / 5 x 255`, decoded `int(B * 5.0)`; "none" keeps the albedo's own colour - leather, metal, wood, the
eyes - and the others are multiplied by the band's colour in P60's shader). Each texel is found on its shared-mesh
triangle (a rasterizer here; a texel within 0.85 px of a triangle that no centre test took is that triangle's, so a
sliver owns its texels) and read from the high mesh (the nearest vertices of the triangle's material facing the
texel's way, inverse-distance weighted): the material gives the albedo pattern and the roughness, MPFB2's masks the
lips, brows and nails, the high mesh's normal in Blender's MikkTSpace tangent frame (the exported tangents) the normal
map. Tiles are painted by the project's own patterns. **The ORM map is imported lossless** (`compress/mode=0`): in a
VRAM block two materials' bands moved by a level; read it with a nearest sampler.

**Not Cycles** (the plan's §2.8 says Cycles): the bake is numpy plus the ambient occlusion's rays against Blender's
BVH (24 cosine rays a high-mesh vertex within 22 cm), deterministic, so a regeneration is bit-exact - no tolerance
is needed anywhere. `--skip-bake` reads the occlusion back from the committed maps' R channel after checking that the
expensive pass's inputs still hash to the manifest's.

### 5. The crowd poses, the corpse, the far meshes and the gear

The crowd poses and the far mesh are in item 3; the `corpse` is the death clip's last key (the body lies on its back,
the lowest joint 17 cm over the ground). `gear.glb` (`people_outfit.GEAR`, the project's own shapes, 1.8 m scale):
sword, spear, shield, banner, bow, quiver, staff, hammer - each its grip at the origin and its length along +Y,
`COLOR_0` RGB its colours and A 1 where the faction's colour tints it (the shield's face, the banner's cloth).

### 6. The people's clips (`people_clips.glb`; `kit/clips.py`'s proportions parameter)

`clips.configure(step_m)` (the only change to `clips.py`; the giant's pipeline never calls it and his clips are
bit-identical) sets the step the gaits are fitted to: the soldier's 0.9 m at 1.8 m. The clips are computed by
`clips.py`'s generator on the reference man's 53-bone rig and keyed on the 23 bones: **idle** (60 frames), **walk**,
**run**, **flee** (the sprint's gait with flailing arms) (32 each), **brace** (1), **cower** (30, a crouch with the
arms over the head), **seated** (1, the riders'), **held** (30, limp), **tumble** (24), **death** (30: the knees go,
then the body falls back and lies), **grip_r** and **grip_l** (one frame, the hand only). The walk's stride measures
1.800 m a cycle, no slide; the pelvis drops 0.136 m at its lowest in the walk (the 0.9 m step is long for a walk at
1.8 m; P60's look at it decides whether its stride should be shorter).

### 7. The manifest, `--verify`, `--skip-bake`, `--budget`, the check options (`tools/giant/build_people.py`)

`build_people.py` (steps `--humans --clips --gear --all`; `--horse --brutes --wolves` say they are P61's and P63's)
runs each role in its own interpreter, three at a time (`--jobs`), and writes `assets/people/manifest.json`: every
file with its step, input hash, sha256 and size, the toolchain, the expensive key, the roles' facts and the index
P60's `PersonModel` reads (per role its scene, maps, variants, poses, far mesh, gear and users; the bones, the bands
and their decoding, the budgets). `--verify` regenerates into a temporary folder and compares bit for bit;
`--budget` estimates the shipped bytes from P59's measured rates and fails over 12.0 MiB. `tools/check.sh --people`
runs the tests, `--verify --skip-bake` and `--budget`; `--people-full` the full `--verify`.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| A full regeneration (`--all`) | 26-29 s (the roles 8-13 s each, three at once) |
| `--verify --skip-bake` / `--verify` | 26 s / 26 s (the occlusion's rays cost about a second a role) |
| `check.sh --people` (tests, verify, budget) | about 30 s of its 6 minutes; `--people-full` the same of its 45 |
| Repository growth | the generated files 9.53 MB (glTFs 8.00 MB, maps 1.53 MB), the added CC0 targets 3.83 MB (cap 4 MB) |

### 8. Provenance and the audit (`tools/giant/fetch_mpfb2.py`, `mpfb2/PEOPLE.sha256`)

77 more targets of the same CC0 release (3,828,578 bytes: the women's macro targets, more age, muscle and weight
combinations, the faces' detail targets' other directions), copied byte for byte from the audited wheel already in
the cache (no network was needed). **They are listed in `mpfb2/PEOPLE.sha256`, not `FILES.sha256`**: the giant's
inputs hash (`build_giant.py`'s `mpfb2`) is the hash of `FILES.sha256`, and his manifest and bake keys would have
changed with it. `fetch_mpfb2.py` audits both lists against the wheel (`AUDIT OK: 214 vendored files (the giant's
137, the people's 77 more)`), `--extract` writes both. `assets/people/PROVENANCE.md` states what derives from the CC0
data and what is wholly the project's code; `CREDITS.md` and `mpfb2/SOURCE.md` name the additions.
