# P48 — The body pipeline II

Wave 29, lane A (the wave's asset writer), beside P49. Branch `pkg/P48`, from the tag `m11-plan`. Plan:
`docs/plans/M11_PLAN.md` §3.1 (design items 1-10), with §2.1-§2.3 (folders, the body II, the clips II),
§2.12 (budgets), §1.1 (definition of done), §1.2 (probes 1 and 2), §1.5-§1.6 (shared files) and §1.7 (test
caps: default 0.3 s, slow 5 s, gate 0). The goal: regenerate the giant once, so that everything after it
builds on the final skeleton and vertex data, without changing the body or M10's clips; make styles and
garments loadable one at a time; give the motion package every clip and landmark it needs.

## What was built (plan §3.1's design list)

### 1. The freeze test first (`tools/giant/freeze.py`, `fixtures/m10_freeze.json`, `test_giant_tools.py`)

`freeze.py` reduces a glTF binary to hashes that do not depend on what else the file holds: per body mesh
the sorted rows of positions, positions with normals, with UVs, with the region id (`floor(COLOR.r x 16)`),
with the four weights by bone name, all of them together, and the triangles over the de-duplicated rows; per
bone its parent and a hash of its node's translation, rotation, scale and inverse bind matrix; per clip and
bone a hash of its tracks (path, interpolation, key times, values). `fixtures/m10_freeze.json` (29 KB) was
written once, from `giant.glb` and `giant_clips.glb` as committed at `5ff50f0`, before any other change
(`freeze.py --write`; nothing runs it again). `FreezeTest` compares every regeneration with it: the three
body meshes in all nine hashes, the 53 bones, the manifest's `body_hash` (still `9cd81c08...`: the hash now
skips the leaf bones), the five full-body M10 clips track for track and the four hand clips' finger tracks.
A positive control shows that a vertex moved by a millionth changes the hash and a reordering does not.
Everything below was built with it green.

### 2. Leaf bones and the cards' weights (`kit/giant_parts.py`, `kit/export_game.py`)

`giant_parts.add_leaf_bones` adds 18 bones to the game_engine rig after `giant_body` built it (so
`giant_body.py` and `split.py`, the bake's inputs, are untouched): under `head` the chains `hair`
(`hair_01`-`03`, from the back of the skull at the ear lobes' height, 0.15 m a bone), `hair_l` and `hair_r`
(`hair_l_01`-`02`, `hair_r_01`-`02`, from the sides of the head, 0.19 m a bone, leaning forward) and `beard`
(`beard_01`-`02`, from the chin, 0.13 m a bone); under `spine_03` the chains `cape_c`, `cape_l` and `cape_r`
(three bones each from under the shoulders to the hips, 0.28 m a bone). The skeleton has 71 bones. No body,
eye or proxy vertex is weighted to one.

Hair and beard cards (grown by `giant_hair.py`, unchanged) are re-weighted in the export
(`giant_parts.card_weights`): along a chain a point's weight passes from the bone above to the bone below
joint by joint (`hat_weights`: a point at a bone's head is wholly the bone before it, so nothing tears at a
joint; above the chain's root it is the head's); the chain is chosen by where the vertex is round the head
(the back chain behind, the side chains beside and in front, blended over 95-135 degrees of azimuth); and
the whole is scaled by how far the vertex is from its card's root along the card (nothing for the first
3 cm at human scale, everything from 14 cm). So every card's root is wholly on `head`, a short card never
leaves it, and a long lock hangs on the chain beside it. A braid's lobes (short cards of their own along the
braid) skip the last factor: a braid hangs whole from where it leaves the head. A chain that no vertex of a
mesh hangs on by at least half is folded back into `head` (`fold_weak_chains`): the crop and the short beard
are wholly on the head. At most four influences a vertex, renormalized.

The index gains `chains`: per chain its `parent`, `bones`, `kind` ("hair", "beard" or "cape"), rest `root`
(game frame), `length_m` and the secondary motion's starting values (`stiffness`, `drag`, `gravity`,
`max_deg`: guesses for P50 to tune); and `meshes[id].chains`, the chains a mesh is weighted to:

| Mesh | Chains |
| --- | --- |
| `Hair_long_back`, `Hair_warrior_tail`, `Hair_wild_mane` | `hair` |
| `Hair_long_braided` | `hair`, `hair_l`, `hair_r` |
| `Beard_full`, `Beard_forked`, `Beard_braided` | `beard` |
| `Hair_crop`, `Beard_short`, every garment but the cape | none |
| `Garment_cape` | `cape_c`, `cape_l`, `cape_r` |

### 3. Build weights (`kit/mh_data.py`, `kit/giant_parts.py`, `tools/giant/mpfb2/`)

`COLOR.g` holds the brawn weight and `COLOR.b` the girth weight as `0.5 + 0.5 w`, w in -1..1. Each is a
MakeHuman target's displacement projected on the vertex normal: the giant's own universal muscle-and-weight
target (both at their most) less the one with the muscle back at its average (brawn), or with the weight back
at its average (girth), mixed by age as the phenotype is (`mh_data.build_displacements`), scaled to stage 1,
smoothed twice over the mesh (creases would speckle), made the same left and right by averaging mirror
twins, and divided by the largest of the two (`build_max_m` = 0.099254 m in the index; brawn's largest is
0.0993 m, girth's 0.0963 m). A slider at +1 pushes as far again as the giant's own build stands over the
average; at -1 it takes that build away. The head keeps its shape: the weights fade with the head's own skin
weight (the face has sliders of its own in the plan, and the eyes, the hair and the beard stand on it).
Garments take the weights of the body vertex under each of their vertices, a card takes those of the skin it
grew from, the shadow proxy those of the nearest skin; `COLOR.a` stays 1. `COLOR.r` is untouched (the freeze
test's region hash).

The two directions need four CC0 targets that M10 had not vendored (it held only the giant's own corner of
the muscle-and-weight family, which gives one direction): `universal-male-{young,old}-averagemuscle-maxweight`
and `-maxmuscle-averageweight`, copied by `fetch_mpfb2.py --extract` from the same audited wheel
(`AUDIT OK: 137 vendored files`, 181,311 bytes more; `FILES.sha256`, `SOURCE.md`, `CREDITS.md` and
`PROVENANCE.md` updated). That changes the bake's input keys (the vendored list's hash and `mh_data.py` are in
them), so the manifest's keys are new and the bake ran again in full; the body it reads is the same (the
freeze test), and every texture came out byte for byte M10's: albedo, ORM, masks, the designs, the atlas and
the detail maps at once, the two normal maps after a second bake. (One texel of the tangent-space normal bake,
row 878 and column 699 of the 1024 px map, flips by 1/255 from run to run: two bakes gave the other value, the
third gave M10's, and `--verify` reports the flip as `TOLERATED` when it meets it. This is M10's known jitter,
`manifest.JITTER_TEXELS`, now with an address.)

### 4. Landmarks (`kit/giant_parts.hand_landmarks`, `kit/designs.side_of`)

Added to the index's `landmarks`, at stage 1: `grip_r`, `grip_l` (the centre of the closed fist in the
model's frame: half a first phalanx off the knuckle line toward the palm and half a second phalanx back toward
the wrist; the right one at (1.142, 2.170, -0.591)), `grip_local_r`, `grip_local_l` (the same point in the
hand bone's own space, which is the same in Blender and in the game), `grip_axis_r`, `grip_axis_l` (the
handle's line through the fist, a unit vector in the hand bone's space, from the little finger's side to the
forefinger's), `knuckle_r`, `knuckle_l` (between the fore and middle knuckles) and `toe_l`, `toe_r` (the
tip of the foot on the ground). Every design gains `side`: "left", "right" or "centre", from where its ink
lies in the bake's rest positions (at least 80 % on one side of the middle). Eight designs are one-sided: the
full sleeve, the forearm slash, the cheek scar and the chest spiral on his right; the brow scar, the chest
claws, the knuckles and two marks on his left.

### 5. Parts (`kit/export_game.py`, `build_giant.py`, `scripts/presentation/giant_model.gd`)

`giant.glb` holds the skeleton, `Body_Head`, `Body_Torso`, `Body_Limbs`, `Eyes` and `ShadowProxy`
(1,213,552 bytes; M10's one file was 4,561,876). Each of the nine hair and beard meshes and the twelve
garment meshes is written to `assets/giant/parts/<mesh id>.glb` on the same armature (21 files, 4,294,472
bytes). The manifest lists 34 files (M10's 13 and the 21 parts); a part the export stops writing leaves the
folder and the manifest. `ensure_import_files` writes each part's import settings: a scene with LODs,
tangents and its named skin, no animation, no shadow mesh. The index's `meshes[id]` gains `part_path`
(`res://assets/giant/parts/<id>.glb`; absent for the meshes inside `giant.glb`).

`GiantModel` (see "Public APIs"): as soon as the scene is instanced it puts an empty `MeshInstance3D` under
the skeleton for every part, named as its mesh and carrying the model's skin, so everything that finds a
mesh by its node keeps working unchanged (`GiantLook`, the first-person layers, the tests). A part's mesh is
loaded the first time its node is shown, or by `ensure_part(id)`, and let go when the node is hidden or
`release_unused(shown)` leaves it out. Loaded parts are shared: a static table holds each loaded mesh weakly,
so two giants showing the same hair hold one `Mesh` and a part nothing shows is freed; the last parts let go
(by a hidden node or a freed model) stay in a recent list of at most 6 parts and 1 MB, so a style turned off
and on again, the next giant after a reload, and the next test's giant do not read the same files again.

### 6. `ShadowProxy`

The whole body (before it is split) copied and decimated by Blender's decimate modifier to 2,500 triangles
(1,252 vertices) with its vertex groups interpolated, moved 12 mm inside the skin along its normals so it
never shades the skin it sits in, without UVs, with the build channels of the nearest skin. In the game it is
drawn in shadow passes only (`cast_shadow = SHADOWS_ONLY`) and every other mesh of the model casts none, so a
shadow cascade draws the giant once instead of 9-12 times; a free mesh (the cape) keeps its own shadow. It is
on render layer 3 (the head's), which the first-person camera never draws, so the crosshair probe's rule
"nothing of the giant on a drawn layer covers the centre" needs no exception; shadow passes do not use a
camera's mask. The index names it under `shadow_proxy` (`node`, `tris`), not under `meshes`: it is no look's
mesh and takes no material from `GiantLook`.

### 7. The free cape (`kit/giant_garment.build_cape`)

Cut from the body's back as M10's shell was (from the neck to the hips and over the shoulders), with a band
round the base of the neck that closes it at the collar. Below the shoulders it leaves the back: every
vertex takes the rearmost place of the cloth above it in its own column (so it hangs straight down from the
shoulder blades instead of following the small of the back), the hem flares by up to 7 cm at human scale and
falls in nine shallow folds, and its lower edge is ragged as before. Above the chains' roots it keeps the
body's weights; below, it passes to `cape_l`, `cape_c` and `cape_r` by where the vertex is across the back
and joint by joint down each chain (`giant_parts.chain_weights`). 1,236 triangles (M10's shell: 1,228). The
index marks it `free`; it still `covers` the lower back (region 6), judged for a free mesh by a longer ray
(0.30 m at human scale) and a share of 0.85, so `set_outfit.json`'s "back" and the designs' rule hold as in
M10. Nothing moves the chains until P50's `SecondaryMotion`; at rest it is the hanging shape.

## Public APIs

### `GiantModel` (`scripts/presentation/giant_model.gd`), added

| Member | What it does |

## Decisions as built

- **Nothing of `giant_body.py`, `split.py`, `giant_hair.py` or the skin's code changed.** The leaf bones, the
  cards' weights, the build weights and the proxy are added in the export after those modules ran, so the
  body the bake reads is M10's by construction as well as by test.
- **One `Skin` for the whole model.** Each part's glTF carries the same 71 joints and inverse bind matrices as
  `giant.glb` (the tool test and `test_giant_parts` compare them), so `ensure_part` puts the part's mesh on a
  node that keeps the model's own skin: one set of bone matrices for all of the giant's meshes, as in M10. A
  part whose skin differed would keep its own (`same_binds`).
- **Parts load through their nodes' visibility.** `giant_look.gd` is P51's in W30 and must not change in W29,
  and it finds meshes by node name on any root. An empty node a part, loaded on show and let go on hide, gives
  "a look's parts are the loaded ones" today and leaves `ensure_part` / `release_unused` for P51 to call.
- **The recent list is strong, small and bounded twice** (6 parts, 1 MB by `part_cost`): without it every
  test's giant read five files again (5.9 ms a spawn where M10 took 0.2-0.6); with it a respawn costs 0.6-0.7 ms.
- **A chain belongs to a style only if some vertex hangs on it by half.** Otherwise a few centimetres of the
  crop's nape would have asked for three spring chains.
- **The build weights are zero on the head**, and smoothed and mirrored, at the cost of a slightly softer
  field than the raw target (the largest push is 9.9 cm at stage 1).
- **The sprint's stride is the brain's.** The clip is seeked on the brain's walk phase; "longer" is the
  flight.
- **The stomp plants where the foot stood** and brings the hips over it, so the recovery has nothing to slide
  back.
- **The proxy sits 12 mm inside the skin** and shares the head's render layer.

## Deviations

- **`part_path`, not `part`**, for a part's file in the index: M10's `part` (a garment's body part) is read
  by `test_skinned_rig.gd`, which is not this package's file (FOLLOWUPS, for P53's index v2).
- **`GiantLook.apply` does not call `ensure_part` / `release_unused`** (the plan's §2.2 sentence): the file is
  not P48's in W29. The model does both through the part nodes' visibility; the two calls are there for P51.
- **Four more CC0 targets are vendored** (item 3). The plan's text assumed the averages were on hand; they
  were not, and without them the two sliders would have been one.
- **`test_giant_hair_cards.gd` (slow, P46's) asks `ensure_part(id)` where it asked `mesh(id)`** (four
  places): it reads the cards of styles no look shows, and a hidden part's node holds no mesh now. Its
  assertions are unchanged.
- **`test_rig_merge.gd` gained one line** (it is not listed among P48's files): its giant row lists every
  `MeshInstance3D` under `Visual/Body`, and the plan's contract adds the shadow proxy there.
- **The proxy is on render layer 3, not 1**: on the limbs' layer the first-person crosshair probe counted it
  as geometry in front of the camera although it is never drawn in a colour pass.
- **The cape still covers region 6** (the plan's slot table says the cape covers the back; a free mesh could
  be argued to cover nothing): the outfits' data and `test_giant_look_real` expect it.
- **The slow suite takes about 6 s** under this machine's load against the package's 5 s cap (one rendered
  probe: 21 frames for the parts, 14 for the chains, the shadows and the memory; see "Checks").
