# P59b — The people's first look pass

Plan: `docs/plans/M11_PLAN.md` §2.8, §2.12, §3.12 (P59), §3.15's list of named characters, §3.18 (P65, which still
takes the owner's notes). Added to the plan after P60 drew P59's people in play: in proportion and variety they were
right, but close up they were clunky. P59b fixes the known defects of the assets before more scenes are built on them.
Built on 21 November 2026 on `pkg/P59b` from `d4f62b6`, on the shared container (4 CPUs, no GPU: llvmpipe), beside P63.

Every judgement below was made from renders with the game's person shader and `PersonModel` / `PersonRig` in the
formation test level's light (a scratch harness, not committed; the people sheet is P64's). The frames are in the
package's report: heads (front, three-quarter, side) of eleven P59 variants before and after and of the ten new
ones; every role front, side and back before and after; the boots, the hands, a soldier's sword and a pikeman's pike
close up; the mail; the crowd poses and far meshes; the squad and the formation of 40 from the stage-1 game camera;
the riders on the tacked horse.

## What was wrong, and what changed

### 1. Faces and heads

- **The normal map drew the facets back in** (the main cause of the faceted faces): P59 read each texel's high-mesh
  normal as the inverse-distance mean of the eight nearest high vertices, so where the low mesh cut across the high
  surface the map tilted the normals by the low mesh's own triangles. With the map off (`normal_depth` 0) the same
  faces were smooth. `people_bake.bake_role` now reads the normal (and the occlusion and the masks) at the nearest
  point on the high mesh's triangles of the texel's own material that face the texel's way (`_nearest_on`; a lip
  never reads the inside of the mouth, a garment never the skin under it). The tangent frame and the green channel
  were checked in Godot with a debug bake whose every normal points up: P59's encoding (glTF's: green along
  Blender's bitangent) is the right one. The inside of the mouth is dropped from the body piece.
- **More of the atlas for the face**: the atlas is laid out by region (`people_roles.ATLAS_REGIONS`): the head's
  skin has a quarter of the 512 px albedo (256 px; P59's whole body and outfit shared a 384 px square), the face
  islands at `FACE_ZOOM` 2.2 times the scalp's density inside it. The albedo stays 512 px (the Web share).
- **Triangles**: the decimation's face and hands weights now add a cost comparable to the collapse's own
  (`PEOPLE_DETAIL_FACTOR` 0.06; at P59's 1.0 a weighted vertex was all but locked: the face kept nearly all its
  triangles and the clothes were starved). The face and ears keep 490-1,310 triangles a variant (the head's skin
  above mid-neck: P59 kept 720-1,580, but drawn with the broken map); the variants use 3,660-4,490 of the 5,000.
- **Eyes**: a polar map on the eyeball (`eye_uv`, `people_bake` "eye" tile): a pupil, an iris with a darker limbal
  ring and flecks, the white shaded to the corners. P59's eye was rough 0.2 and band "none", which the person shader
  reads as metal: dark specks. The eye is now rough 0.4.
- **Hair and beards**: shells of the scalp and beard masks whose thickness rises smoothly from 1.5 mm at the edge,
  the open edge smoothed along itself (the body's quads' steps straightened), then Taubin-smoothed (`smoothed`): the
  hairline and the beard's edge are smooth lines, the full beard deepens toward the chin and falls below it.
- **Helms, caps, hoods** (`kit/people_parts.py`, new): the iron helm is a cap laid over the skull by rays from the
  head's centre (a brow band, a rolled rim, a soft peak, a nasal down the bridge); the iron guard's is deeper with
  cheek plates (`iron_helm_guard`: the same variant, a heavier helm as plan §2.8 asks); the leather cap is lower with
  a rolled band; the hood is rows of rays round the head whose face opening is a smooth oval, loose over the crown,
  drawn back to a soft fold and falling to a short cape over the shoulders. P59 used the giant's shells, whose
  decimated rims left flaps at the ears and crumpled hoods.

### 2. Boots and shoes

`people_parts.boots`: each boot is the union, remeshed into one smooth surface (Blender's voxel remesh), of the foot's
convex hull pushed out by the leather (a rounded toe box: the toes are never drawn), a sole slab wider than the foot
(the welt), a heel block, and a tapered shaft over the trousers to half the calf with a turned-down cuff. The mage's
and the elder's wrapped sandals (their bare toes and spiky straps) are now shoes, the same build cut at the ankle
(`people_roles.FOOTWEAR`). The boots are over the trousers.

### 3. Mail, helms, hems, specks

- The mail's islands lie in the atlas's side rect at a fixed density (`MAIL_UV_PER_M` 0.15 UV a metre), upright and
  unturned, cut in quarters so they fit; `person.gdshader` draws the rings there from the mail tile
  (`MAIL_TILE_RINGS` x `MAIL_TILE_RINGS` riveted rings in the albedo and normal maps) at `MAIL_RINGS_PER_UV` (1.25 cm
  rings), over the macro normal, as metal (metallic 0.85, the ring tops smoother), fading out where a ring is under
  about three pixels; the crowd shader draws the mail as dull metal. The mail and helm albedos are a lighter steel.
- The plate metal (helms, buckles) was rough 0.38, over the shader's metal threshold (0.35): it drew as grey
  plastic. It is 0.30 now.
- The notches: the mail dipped into the tunic's neck slit under it, and two garments of one slot were built side by
  side (the crew's vest inside the shirt, the mage's kilt inside the trousers), so decimated they showed through each
  other in patches. Now one slot's second garment is built over its first (`build_outfit` closes the slot after each),
  the belt goes over the mail (a belted hauberk), the skirted garments' hips are drawn in to the body where the
  A-pose elbows had bulged them (`tame_bulge`), the mail and skirts are smoothed with their hems straightened, and
  after the decimation the layers are kept apart (`layer_push`: an outer piece's vertex under an inner piece moves
  out over it, an inner piece's vertex through an outer one is drawn in).

### 4. Hands and the grip landmark

The rest hands are relaxed (`people_roles.HAND_CURLS`: the fingers curled a little more from the index to the
little finger, the thumb along the index's side) - P59's half-closed hands with the thumb on the index's tip read as a
pinch. Each variant's manifest entry carries `grip_local_l/r` and `grip_axis_l/r` (`people_body.grip_landmark`: the
mean centre of the four fingers' curls and the line from the little finger's to the index's, in the hand bone's
frame at 1.8 m). `PersonRig` reads them (`grips_of`) in place of its `GRIP_AXIS`: the weapon's socket, the arms' fist
and the landmarks' palm are the grip; the clips turn the hanging forearms a little and bend the wrists so a carried
sword points forward and down from the fist with its handle through the curled fingers.

### 5. Far meshes and crowd poses

The far mesh is collapsed from the hold pose welded into one closed skin (no UV seams for the collapse to tear: the
spikes at the feet), each triangle coloured from the pose's triangle nearest its centre. The crowd poses are
regenerated from the new bodies (the boots, the mail, the hoods read at distance); 598-600 triangles, the far meshes
149-150.

### 6. The walk

The walk takes a 0.7 m step (P59's 0.9 m, the soldier rig's stride, dropped the pelvis 13.6 cm a step): the pelvis
drops 7.4 cm, the stride is 1.4 m a cycle without slide. The run takes 1.1 m steps (planted longer, lifted less:
`people_body.GAIT_CHANGES`), the flee 1.15 m; each is slide-free at its own stride. `PersonRig` plays the walk at the
brain's walk phase times `2 stride_length_m / walk_cycle_m` (`walk_ratio`), and the run and flee on their own phase
at the manifest's `run_cycle_m` (2.2 m); the index carries both.

### 7. The notable and rider roles

- `notable` (eight variants, plan §3.15): `edda`, `reeve_tamsin`, `sedge`, `ferrywoman`, `herald` (five women, as
  their records call them), `toll_captain`, `deserter`, `corvane`; the tunic in the faction's colour, each its own
  hair, headgear, beard and extras (the marchwarden's and the herald's tabards, the reeve's and Sedge's hoods and
  skirts, the toll captain's cap and pauldrons, Corvane's guard helm, tabard and pauldrons; he stands at his
  record's 2.0 m by the height scale).
- `rider` (`rider_a`, `rider_b`): the soldier's mail outfit. The `seated` clip is now a riding seat read from the tacked
  horse's manifest: the seat between the sitting bones is the model's origin (put it on `saddle_seat`), the ankles over
  the stirrup irons' treads (0.255 m out, 0.693 m under the seat), the knees out round the barrel, the hands low on
  the reins. The rider's crowd poses are all seated but the corpse (`people_roles.ROLE_POSE_CLIP`); the index's
  `seat` records the convention for P62.

The variant lists of P59's six roles are unchanged and in their order (the game hashes entity ids into them).

## The atlas and the vertices

The seams are the high mesh's own (MakeHuman's UV layout of the body and the garments' shells, carried through the
decimation; the boots and the belt get seams of their own), plus the regions' borders; Blender's angle-based unwrap
lays the islands out and each region is packed into its rect (two pinned corner triangles hold the rect as the
packer's box). A variant has 2,700-3,400 glTF vertices for 3,700-4,500 triangles (P59: 4,000-4,800 for 3,500-4,400),
which is what pays for the ten new variants in the Web share.

## What is left (for P65 and P62)

- The faces are one atlas a role: the normal map is baked from the role's first variant, so a variant whose face
  differs most (a woman's on a man's role atlas) shows soft smudges on a cheek under a low sun.
- Hoods show a few pale specks along the face opening at the tile's edge; the helm's highlight is large (the shader
  holds metal at roughness 0.46).
- The crowd poses are still collapsed with their UVs: close up a few slivers show at the boots' seams (at crowd
  distance they are sub-pixel).
- The women of the notable role are built on the same lean and narrow face targets as the men's and read a little
  severe.
- P62 places the rider at the horse's `saddle_seat` and drives the seated clip; the lance is P62's.
