## 1. The creatures' cost (the Web's pack of wolves over the 2.5 ms)

P64 measured the formation test level's forty soldiers and a pack of five wolves and a dire wolf at 2.69 ms of the
people's CPU on the Web (the people 1.64, the creatures 1.05) against plan §2.12's 2.5 ms. Measured natively with a
scratch bench (the rigs' `_process` timed over 200 frames, headless with `--skin`), a standing wolf cost 0.145 ms, of
which about 0.05 the brain (the box rig's pivots it still posed) and 0.09 the skinned frame (the gait's bookkeeping,
the leg solve, the neck, the jaw and the tail), and the slots' allocation 0.10 ms a frame for eight creatures.

- **The hidden box rig is not posed** (`ActorRig._box_posed()`, `QuadrupedRig._box_posed()`; `BruteRig`,
  `SkinnedWolfRig` and `SkinnedQuadrupedRig` answer false while their skinned body shows). The brain still computes
  everything the skinned body and the game read - the arm poses (`arm_pose`, `off_pose`), the torso's twist, the head
  pivot's pitch (`head_basis()`), the tail's sway (`tail_yaw()`), the footfalls - but no longer writes the hidden
  arm, leg, torso, head and tail pivots (each write moved the hidden merged meshes under them). Body is always posed
  (the skinned body hangs under it) and is set only when it moves.
- **A standing wolf keeps its legs' solve** (`SkinnedWolfRig._legs_unchanged`): when every paw is planted where it
  was and the skeleton has not moved, the legs' bones keep last frame's pose instead of being reset and solved again;
  `test_wolf_rig` checks that the kept pose is the solve's, bit for bit. The rest pose comes back from cached
  rotations (only the forelegs' upper bones are ever moved), the solve's constants are made once, the gait's
  standing frame skips the lift logic it cannot use, the rig state is read once a frame.
- **The half rate** (`CreatureSlots.half_rate`): a skinned creature wholly outside the camera's frustum, or standing
  farther than 0.75 x its record's `render_lod_m` (the wolf 30 m, the dire wolf about 58 m, the ogre 52 m) while not
  acting, poses its body every other frame with the frame's time carried over; the brain runs every frame. The Web
  check's pack stands 20 m from the camera: the half rate does not apply to it (0 half-rate frames in the check's
  rows), so its numbers below are the full rate's.
- `CreatureSlots.allocate` and the wolves' slot calls read the rig's stored state instead of the actor's
  `get_rig_state()`; the brute caches its death clip's length.
- `PeopleWebProbe` takes the median of thirty bench runs (P64: the mean of ten, which a frame the shared machine took
  from the page could move by half a millisecond) and reports the creatures' half-rate frames and camera distance.

Natively (the bench, the machine quiet): a standing wolf 0.145 -> **0.055 ms**, a trotting wolf (3 m/s) 0.174 ->
0.125 ms, an idle ogre 0.06 -> 0.04 ms, the slots' allocation 0.10 -> 0.06 ms a frame for eight creatures. The
`low` tier's slot count was not lowered: the pack fits at eight.

## 2. The brutes' sweep, two-handed (`scripts/presentation/brute_rig.gd`)

P64 solved the box rig's sector-pulse poses onto the brute's real arms: the club arm thrown straight out behind, the
off arm raised in a guard - a T-pose with one arm over the head. Now every sector pulse (the ogre's sweep, the troll's
club, the whelp's swipe, the King's two combos, the Matriarch's sweep and frenzy) swings the club with both fists on
its handle on the action's clock (`SWEEP_KEYS`, `sweep_pose`): the windup draws it back high over the right shoulder
with the torso turned away (the box rig's easing), the active phase swings it across at the chest (the arms out in
front at mid swing, the torso turning through), the recovery brings it round and down as the off hand lets go. A
second blow (an id ending `_2`: the King's backhand, the frenzy's second) is the same mirrored. The timings are the
actions' (nothing in the data changed). `test_brute_rig`: both hands on the handle (the wrists 0.8 m apart at most on
the 3.4 m ogre) and the club drawn back high at the windup's end. The readability gate: the sweeps' wind-ups change
18-23 % of their screen boxes (P64 14-22 %).

## 3. The gallop's flexion (`scripts/presentation/skinned_quadruped_rig.gd`)

At the gallop only (its weight is 0 below 5.5 m/s: the walk and the trot are unchanged):

- **The stance**: a planted leg's pastern sinks toward the level under the load (`GALLOP_SINK_DEG`: 24 degrees fore,
  16 hind, at mid stance) and the heel lifts over the toe toward the push-off (`GALLOP_PUSH_DEG`: 30 fore, 52 hind,
  from 45 % of the stance), so the knee and the hock give with the lowered fetlock and the hind leg leaves the ground
  bent instead of as a long straight strut. The hoof stays where it was planted (only the pose above it changes).
- **The swing**: the keyed fold starts sooner after the lift (`GALLOP_KEY_BLEND.x` 0.14, was 0.24) and the hind leg's
  first key is more bent (the trailing leg folds at once: a follow-through); the reaching foreleg keeps its knee a
  little bent until the landing's solve takes it.
- No bone is stretched (the rig only turns bones); `test_rider_rig` (a planted hoof never moves, every standing leg
  reaches its hoof) passes unchanged.

## 4. The rider's fall (`scripts/presentation/skinned_quadruped_rig.gd`)

The rider stays slumped with the falling horse a little longer (`RIDER_SLUMP_S` 0.5 s, was 0.42), falls clear over
`RIDER_FALL_S` 0.85 s (was 0.45) with the death clip at 0.7 of its speed (`RIDER_FALL_CLIP_RATE`), and the lance,
still in the fist, turns from the way it pointed as he left the saddle to the level instead of following the hand's
bone through the clip (it swung up and wide).

## How it looks against the bar

- **The brutes' sweep**: at the game camera it reads as a heavy two-handed club swing (drawn back, swung across,
  let round); at about 4 m the backswing is natural from the front and three-quarters, the backhand reads as a second
  blow; the off forearm can cross the face at the backswing's top and the off hand only approximately holds the
  handle. Much better than P64's T-pose; not a clip.
- **The gallop**: at the game camera as P65b (a galloping horse); at 3 m the trailing hind leg now folds as it lifts
  and the planted legs give at the fetlock, so the push-off no longer reads as straight struts; the reaching foreleg
  is still fairly straight just before it lands (as a real one is). Walk and trot unchanged.
- **The rider's fall**: eased; the lance lies down with him instead of whipping up. The horse's own roll onto its
  side (the brain's 0.55 s) is still quick.
- **The wolves**: unchanged in look; the sheet's page c3 shows them whole with and without the shell.

## What is left

- The trotting and galloping wolf still costs about 0.12 ms natively (the gait's dictionaries and the leg solve in
  GDScript); a pack running at the giant on the Web would sit near the budget again. A typed rewrite of the gait's
  per-leg state is the next saving.
- The horse's gallop at 3 m is better but not yet a filmed horse: the stance's fetlock sink and the push-off read, the
  swing is still keyed (no clip).
- The brutes' sweep is two-handed by solve, not by a clip: at 3 m the off hand's grip on the handle is approximate
  (the wrists up to about 0.7 m apart at the backswing's top on the ogre).
