## The audio-thread crash (fixed first)

**What was seen.** Two of about 45 Valley bot runs on P22's build died with SIGSEGV in the audio
mixing thread (`AudioDriverDummy`'s thread loop -> `AudioDriver::audio_server_process`), both while
the 4-core machine was oversubscribed (load 7-9), neither reproducible by its seed.

**The race (Godot 4.6.3).** Every `volume_db` set on a playing `AudioStreamPlayer`, and every mix
of a playing `AudioStreamPlayer3D`'s panning, calls `AudioServer.set_playback_bus_volumes_linear`,
which *replaces* the playback's bus details: the new details go into the playback with an atomic
store, the old ones onto `bus_details_graveyard`. `AudioServer.update()` (the end of every main-loop
iteration) moves the graveyard to `bus_details_graveyard_frame_old`, and the next `update()` deletes
it - about one frame later. The mixing thread, in `_mix_step`, loads a playback's details pointer and
then copies the details (a `StringName` per bus, `volume`) *without any lock*. If the mixing thread
is preempted between the load and the copy for longer than a frame, the main thread frees the
details under it: the copy reads freed memory. A headless `--fixed-fps` run draws thousands of
frames a second, so "a frame" is well under a millisecond, and an oversubscribed machine preempts
threads for milliseconds - the window is wide open there and nearly closed on an idle desktop.
P19's music and ambience directors wrote every stem's and bed's `volume_db` every frame, every
playing 3D voice replaces its details at every mix, and P22's quadruped footfalls added voices.

**Reproduced** in a crash lab (a tiny project, not in the repository: 48 stream players whose
volumes change every frame, or 24 positioned players moving), with the mixing thread pinned to one
core beside a SCHED_FIFO "stall injector" that freezes it for a few milliseconds at random points:
without the fix **14 of 17 runs failed** (2/3, 7/8 and 5/6 in three configurations: 13 SIGSEGVs,
7 in the mixing thread and 6 on the main thread, and one hang); two of the 3D runs crashed at the
very addresses of the two P22 crashes (`0x4808608 <- 0x3a25759 <- 0x4c9fbc5` in the stripped
4.6.3 binary). Without the stall injector the lab did not crash in 25 runs: the race needs the
preemption.

**The fix** (`scripts/audio/audio_director.gd`, `music_director.gd`, `ambience_director.gd`):

1. **A mixing barrier once a frame.** `Audio._process` begins with `mix_barrier()`:
   `AudioServer.lock(); AudioServer.unlock()`. The threaded drivers (Dummy, PulseAudio, ALSA,
   WASAPI) hold the driver lock for the whole of `audio_server_process`, so taking it waits for a
   mix step that is still running; none that loaded details before this frame's replacements can
   outlive their freeing in the next `update()`. (The single-threaded web build mixes on the main
   thread, where the race cannot happen.) The barrier costs one uncontended mutex a frame and
   waits only while a mix step runs. `Audio.mix_barriers` counts the frames that passed it.
2. **Volumes written only when they change** (`MusicDirector.set_volume`, `VOLUME_STEP_DB`
   0.01 dB): a steady stem, bed or emitter replaces nothing; a fade replaces at most once a frame.
   Most frames now replace no music or ambience details at all (only the 3D voices' panning).

With the fix the stall-injected lab ran **0 of 14** failures (8 + 6 runs of the two configurations
that failed 12 of 14 without it). `tests/integration/test_audio_thread_safety.gd` holds the
behaviour: a helper thread holds `AudioServer.lock()` for 40 ms, as a mix step does, and
`Audio._process` may not return before the helper releases it (without the barrier it returned
41 ms before the release); the barrier costs nothing when nothing mixes, there is no barrier while
audio is off, and a volume that does not change is not written.

**The proof on the game.** Valley bot runs (`tools/run_bots.sh`: New Game to the end of the
prologue, the score and the ambience playing through the Dummy driver's mixing thread, as in P22's
crashed runs) in two batches, each three runs at a time, beside three busy processes
(`python3 -c "while True: pass"`) on the 4-core machine, each from a frozen copy of its commit:

## What was built

- **A score library**, `tools/audio/synth/music.py`: modal pitch (D dorian, D aeolian, MIDI and
  note names), the zone frame (96 BPM, 4/4, twelve bars, 661,500 frames at 22.05 kHz) and the
  King's (104 BPM, 610,615 frames); instruments that render one note with its release tail - a
  plucked lute (additive strings through a wooden body), a breathy low flute (a near-sine with a
  chiff and a growing vibrato), horns and low brass (harmonics that brighten with loudness, a scoop
  into the note), a vowel choir (a wavetable under formant envelopes, detuned, with vibrato), warm
  and dark pads (detuned band-limited saws), a sub, a tremolo string ensemble, bells, and drums (a
  frame drum, taiko, toms, rim, shaker, a metal crash, timpani); **a seeded modal phrase writer**: a
  set's motif - a one-bar cell of (beat, length, scale step) - stated, sequenced, inverted,
  augmented or cut on an anchor degree bar by bar, or a bar written out, with seeded ornaments
  (grace notes and turns), velocity arcs and a little timing, and `check_phrases()` holding every
  phrase inside its four-bar segment; rendering into sample-exact loops (events mixed into a
  buffer one loop long plus their tails, the tails folded onto the head, a circular reverb) and
  one-shots.
- **An ambience library**, `tools/audio/synth/ambience.py`: looped noise shaped in the frequency
  domain over exactly one period, periodic slow curves, wind with gusts, insects (narrow-band
  chirp trains), leaves, murmur (far formant grains from P21's voices, never words), hens, drips,
  stone whistles, water with bubbling grains, fire with crackle, and the eighteen spots.
- **Loudness**: `core.k_weighting` (the ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting: the standard's 48 kHz biquads
  evaluated at each frequency, exact below 24 kHz), `core.loudness_lufs` (400 ms blocks, 75 %
  overlap, the absolute and relative gates) and `core.normalize_lufs` (to a target under a peak
  cap); `build_audio.py` takes `"norm": {"lufs": ..., "max_peak_db": ...}`; `check_signal` reports
  non-finite samples. A full-scale 997 Hz sine in one channel reads -3.01 LUFS, as the standard
  says.
- **Three generators**: `gen_music.py` (19 loops, seeds 230001-230032, 1.99 MB of Ogg),
  `gen_stingers.py` (11 one-shots, 231001-231021, 0.27 MB), `gen_ambience.py` (4 stereo beds, 2
  emitter loops and 18 spots, 232001-232118, 0.58 MB). Every file is in
  `assets/audio/manifest.json` and `PROVENANCE.md`; `build_audio.py --verify` regenerates all 263
  files bit-exact. The placeholder family (P19's test tones, bed and stinger) and
  `data/audio/bank_placeholder.json` are deleted, as the plan asks.
- **Records** (`data/audio/`): seven music sets (`music_valley_{calm,wild,ancient}`, `music_title`,
  `music_end`, `music_campaign_end`, `music_boss_ogre_king`), five ambience sets
  (`ambience_{meadow,forest,village,stone,sandbox}`), six Valley regions
  (`region_valley_{hollow,village,wood,quarry,ancient,boss}`), four context regions
  (`region_context_{title,end,campaign_end,sandbox}`), the silent list (`region_silent.json`), and
  two banks (`bank_stingers.json`: 11 cues; `bank_ambience.json`: 24 cues).
- **The silent list**: `audio_region` records gain `silent_levels` and `silent_contexts`
  (`scripts/core/schemas/audio_region_schema.gd`: a silent list names nothing else);
  `Audio.silent_levels()`, `Audio.silent_contexts()` and `Audio.is_silent_level()` (the list, or a
  level record that lives under `data/test/`: P22's test levels and P24a's test world).
- **The web**: `tools/build_web.sh` ships the packed pck in parts of at most 15 MB
  (`index.pck.gz.b64.<n>.txt`; one part keeps `index.pck.gz.b64.txt`), `--parts=N` forces N, the
  BUILD line carries `pckParts`, the build prints the audio's share of the pck, and a `.gdignore`
  keeps Godot from importing an earlier build; `web/launcher.html` downloads the parts in order
  under one meter and joins their text before unpacking, resumes a suspended AudioContext on every
  press, and says "Sound on"; `tools/web/audio_check.mjs` is the web audio check; the Web presets
  leave the build-time `assets/audio/manifest.json` out (151 KB of the pck);
  `tools/pck_files.py --audio-share`.
- **Tests**: `tests/integration/test_audio_zones.gd`, `test_music_valley.gd`,
  `test_ambience_valley.gd`, `test_audio_thread_safety.gd`, `tests/slow/test_music_loops.gd`, and
  `ScoreTests` in `tools/audio/test_synth.py` (see *Runtime*). P19's and P21's suites moved from
  the placeholder to the shipped files (see *Deviations*).
- **`docs/USABILITY_SCRIPT.md` §8**: the owner's audio sheet.

## The score, for the owner's listening pass

Every stem is in `assets/audio/music/` and plays in any audio player (the loops repeat seamlessly
in players that loop); in the game, start the title, the sandbox or the Valley without `--no-audio`.

**One frame, one harmony.** The three zone sets share `zone_96`: D dorian, 96 BPM, 4/4, twelve bars
(30.0 s), one chord progression - `Dm Dm C Dm | G G Am C | F C G Asus-A` (the statement; the dorian
lift through B natural; the journey back, whose A pulls to Dm at the loop) - so the shared danger
and combat stems fit every district and a district change always lands on a bar. Each set has a
drone and three melody variants written by the phrase writer from the set's motif, in three
four-bar segments; the melody scheduler picks a variant (or a rest) per segment.

| Set | Districts | Drone | Melody instrument | Motif |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `music.valley_calm` "The Hollow" | Giant's Hollow, Thornwick and the fields; the sandbox | a warm pad of detuned saws on the chords, a sine bass, a low-pass that opens a little in the dorian lift | plucked lute (variant C plucks its long notes in octaves) | A-D-E-F, E-D: a rising fourth and a step to the minor third |
| `music.valley_wild` "The Old Wood" | the Old Wood, the Raider Quarry | a dark pad voicing the chords low with their sevenths, a closed low-pass, a sub, a breathing wind | low breathy flute with turns | D-C-A, (B-A) G-A-F: a falling third, a turn through the dorian B |
| `music.valley_ancient` "The Broken Kings" | the Barrow, the ruins, Kingsfall | a stone choir on open fifths of the roots, a deep sub, a low bell at bars 1 and 7 | horn | D...A, G-F-E: a rising fifth and a slow fall |

Every phrase starts after its segment's downbeat and ends before its last half beat, and each

least 40 dB under its own level for 40 ms around every boundary (`test_music_loops`) and the 80 ms
variant switch never cuts a note or lets a tail in. The strong-beat notes outside the chord are
written passing and neighbour notes (over the wild set's dark pad, often the chord's seventh).

**Shared stems.** `pulse` (danger): a frame-drum heartbeat on D (DUM . . dum DUM . . dum), a ticking
shaker in sixteenths with its weight on the off-beats, a soft roll closing every fourth bar.
`combat_drums`: taiko on one and three with pickups, toms on the off-beats, rim on two and four, a
tom fill into every fourth bar, a crash at the top of the loop. `combat_brass`: a low brass
ostinato on each bar's root and fifth (DAH-da DAH DAH DAH-da DAAH), doubled softly an octave down,
a swelling triad on the last two beats of every fourth bar.

**Title and end.** `title_theme`: the game's theme on a horn choir - the tune opens on the calm
motif's rising fourth (A-D), lifts through the dorian B and ends on the C#-A that pulls back to D;
a harmony horn in thirds and sixths, a low horn on the roots. `music.title` plays it over the stone
choir; `music.end` (after the `prologue_end` stinger) over the calm set's warm pad;
`music.campaign_end` (the world's end, P36) over the stone choir with the combat brass's ostinato
12 dB under it. Each rests after 3-4 minutes left alone (45-75 s).

**The Ogre King** (`music.boss_ogre_king`, its own frame: D aeolian, 104 BPM, twelve bars, 27.69 s;
it shares no stem with the zone sets). The bars run `D D Bb A | D D G A | Bb C D A`. `boss_drums`:
double-time taiko (booms on the beat and the off-sixteenths, toms filling the gaps, rim on two and
four, crashes at bars 1 and 9, a sixteenth roll through bar 12). `boss_theme`: a chromatic
eighth-note ostinato on each bar's root (root, root, third, root, the semitone above, root, the
leading tone, root) doubled an octave up, swelling pedals under bars 1-4 and from bar 5 the King's
call on horns (D-A-D-F, E-D-C#-A, ...). `boss_frenzy` (phase 2, joins at the next bar): a string
tremolo grinding a semitone above each chord's root and a rising scream. The fight starts with the
King's one-bar intro stinger (2.43 s: taiko and crash, a low brass cluster swelling on D-Eb-A, a
sixteenth tom roll into the loop), then the set crossfades in over 0.5 s.

title's music 4.5-4.9 s, New Game to the Hollow's 8.0-9.8 s), stay 10.9 MB inside the memory cap
and log no error. The 220 buffers registered at the Valley's load hold 35,296,880 bytes (its
samples as stereo float at 44.1 kHz); the giant stands, so few copies play (6 sources, at most
0.4 MB): the cap's 12 MB of room for copies is not exercised here.

**The holes are the machine's frames.** The no-threads build mixes the streams (every stem, the
bed, the spots) in the main thread's handler of the driver worklet's `read` messages, one per 128
frames played. Software WebGL keeps the main thread in tasks of 100-600 ms for 93 % of the time
(page frames p50 150 ms), so the worklet runs dry in most frames. The check wraps the driver's own
port and logs every `read` the main thread handles: in the three runs every hole began after the
last `read` before it and ended with the first `read` after it (within -17 to +6 ms), and no `read`
was handled inside a hole. The worklet held 71-83 ms (median) when the main thread left it, not
the 185.8 ms buffer: between two long tasks the main thread refills only part of it.
**`--strict`** (any hole over 50 ms fails: the plan's criterion as written) **cannot pass on this
machine**: it needs a main thread that comes back to the mixer within what the worklet holds,
which software WebGL drawing the Valley does not allow. On a machine with a GPU the frames are
short and the audio brief measured no dropout for single stalls up to 120 ms at this latency;
that run is **NOT RUN** here (no GPU), and is the one to make on real hardware. Earlier runs of
the check gave the same picture: the first form (every hole failed it) failed all four of its
runs (the release 366 and the two-part build 618 ms longest with another package's slow suite
beside them, load 5-9; the release 273 and 337 ms with nothing beside them), and the traces of
three more runs (199, 185 and 216 holes), placed again with `--replay`, show every hole the main
thread's.

## Decisions as built

- **Loudness, not peaks**, balances the score (decision 4's "stems at -3 dBFS" is the cap): a sparse
  melody normalised to a peak would sit 6-10 dB under a dense drone. Every music loop, stinger, bed
  and emitter is normalised to a K-weighted loudness target under a -3 dBFS peak cap; the spots are
  normalised to a -3 dBFS peak and balanced by their bank volumes.
- **Melodies rendered segment by segment**: each four-bar segment has its own reverb tail and fades
  over its last half beat. A variant's tail never crosses into a segment where the scheduler may
  have chosen another variant or a rest (rendered whole, the tails were -7 to -19 dB at the
  boundaries).
- **Beds stereo, everything else mono** (22.05 kHz): the beds' width is cheap (the two channels
  share their motion and differ in their noise and in where their events sit) and the music is
  positioned nowhere.
- **The barrier rather than a voice-pool rule**: the race is in the engine and every replaced bus
  detail is exposed to it (stems, beds, emitters, the voice pool's 3D voices); only the barrier
  covers all of them.
- **District changes keep the frame's position** (a refinement of P19's director, above): P19
  started every new set at its bar 1, so for the two crossfade bars the old and new sets played
  different chords of the same progression (a G major bar against D minor, for instance).
- **The campaign's end** has its own set made from existing stems (the plan's "music.campaign_end
  from existing stems"), so P36 has nothing to add but its trigger.
- **The web check marks the Hollow by the music director's own line** (`AUDIO_MUSIC` under
  `--audio-stats`): the page cannot see the game's state otherwise, and the zone set starts the
  moment the loading screen ends (main.gd's gate waits for the samples).

## Deviations

- **The crash fix lives in `scripts/audio/audio_director.gd`** (and the volume threshold in the two
  directors): FOLLOWUPS assigned the crash to this package with that file among its suspects.
- **`region_contexts` is four records** (`region_context_{title,end,campaign_end,sandbox}.json`):
  GameData loads one record per file.
- **The silent list is an `audio_region` record** (`region_silent.json`, id `audio_region.silent`)
  with two new optional fields, `silent_levels` and `silent_contexts`, and a schema rule (a silent
  list names no music, ambience, level, context, boss or zones). Test content is silent by its
  folder too (`data/test/`), so P24a's test world needs no entry.
- **LUFS normalisation** (added to P19's `core.py` and `build_audio.py`). The plan's §4.2 says beds
  at -12 dBFS RMS; the beds are at -17 to -18 LUFS (-19.3 to -20.1 dBFS RMS). On their buses (Music
  -9 dB, Ambience -12 dB) they sit about 1.5 dB under the explore mix; at -12 dBFS RMS they would sit
  about 6 dB over it.
- **The Ogg import factor is 1.11, not 1.03** (decision 8's arithmetic in `build_audio.py` and
  P19's `test_audio_assets`): Godot 4.6.3's `.oggvorbisstr` measured x1.107 over the score's 54
  files in the release pck (x1.07 for a 100 KB bed, x1.2 for a 5 KB spot). With it the M6 audio is
  **3.93 MB** (cap 4.0; the pck holds 4,117,115 bytes of imported audio: 3,135,332 Ogg, 981,783 QOA);
  with 1.03 it read 3.72 MB.
- **More spots than the plan's 14**: 18 (the stone districts and the flush needed their own).
- **P19's and P21's tests moved off the placeholder** (deleted, as the plan asks):
  `test_audio_assets` (counts of the shipped files; the stems' frames on `drone_calm` and
  `boss_drums`; the default suite renders the *shortest* loop over two periods, the slow tier
  every loop), `test_audio_director` (its fixture sets use P23's stems and stingers; the boss intro
  is 2.43 s; the title context is overridden by id), `test_audio_policies`, `test_audio_schemas`
  (+ the silent list record), `test_cue_router` (no `mus.stinger.default` is left),
  `test_sfx_banks` (the placeholder bank is gone), `test_sfx_coverage` (a comment).
  `test_web_presets` expects the BUILD line's `pckParts` (the multi-part pck adds it) and checks
  the launcher's parts and sound.
- **Hotspot and helper edits outside the owned paths**: `tools/pck_files.py` (sizes and
  `--audio-share`), `export_presets.cfg` (the Web presets leave out `assets/audio/manifest.json`;
  P23 owns the file in W14), `tools/audio/test_synth.py` (`ScoreTests`).
- **The web check's hole criterion has two forms.** The plan's "no silent run longer than 50 ms
  in the music for 60 s" is `--strict`. By default the check passes a hole only when it shows the
  hole to be the main thread's (no driver `read` handled through it, the first one ending it) and
  fails on any other: on this machine every page frame is software WebGL's, the strict form fails
  in every run, and a check that can never pass here would say nothing about the build (see *The
  web build and the audio check*). The verdict of both forms is recorded above.
- **The default tier's time is over its 0.5 s**: the four new suites take 0.58-0.81 s, the audio
  suites together 0.20-0.67 s more than at the base (see *Runtime added to the suites*).
- **No `mus.stinger.default`**: a stinger with no cue is reported missing instead of playing the
  placeholder. The stingers of events that do not exist yet (`yield`, `rout`, `discovery`,
  `world_reveal`, `ancestry`) are the world packages'.

## Known gaps and notes for the next packages

- **P28** (the world's sets and ambience): use the zone frame (`zone_96`) and the phrase writer;
  balance by LUFS (`"norm": {"lufs": ...}`); count Ogg imports at x1.11; small files import
  relatively larger (x1.2 at 5 KB). Add regions to the world's level record, or it fails
  `test_audio_zones` (every level maps or is silent).
- **Seeking a stereo Ogg is not sample-exact in Godot 4.6.3**: a playback started at an offset in a
  20 s stereo bed did not line up with the continuous playback at that point (mono stems did, to
  the sample). Nothing seeks today (every stem and bed starts at 0); a package that starts beds at
  random offsets should not rely on sample accuracy.
- **The web check measures the output, not the music alone**: a hole is any silence over 50 ms in
  what reaches the speakers; the drone and the bed never rest, so a hole is the engine's mixer
  starving. **The plan's criterion as written ("no hole", `--strict`) fails on this machine** in
  every run, and only because of its frames: the check passes by showing that each hole is the
  main thread's (no driver `read` handled through it, the first one ending it). Players whose
  machine draws the Valley slowly will hear the same stutter: the no-threads build mixes on the main
  thread, and `output_latency.web` 200 (decision 2) covers stalls up to about 120 ms. A run with
  `--strict` on a machine with a GPU is NOT RUN; the M6 gate should make it there if it can (it
  could not: no GPU in the gate's container either - its runs had 190-191 holes, all the main
  thread's; the check now prints the criterion's verdict, a default pass reads "OK (the plan's
  criterion NOT MET ...)", a failed run keeps its trace and console tail, and the run on a GPU is
  the owner's: `docs/plans/FOLLOWUPS.md`, "From the M6 gate review"). A
  threads build (served with cross-origin isolation) would mix off the main thread; the host
  decides that, not this package.
- **The stone set's two emitters** never both reach the giant (108 m apart, reach 60 and 25 m);
  the two-emitter cap is exercised by the test only.
